## Fantasy implication

ARCH inference: the historical evidence makes protection and drive efficiency more important than a simple Super Bowl rematch narrative. Seattle's run defense supports caution about assuming Stevenson will be efficient merely because Henderson is absent. New England's 2025 passing baseline supports a credible Maye-led response if protection holds. Henry and Douglas have plausible middle-field opportunities, but replacement defenders do not guarantee targets or production. Seattle's inexperienced lead-back configuration does not erase Emanuel Wilson's NFL experience. Kupp and Arroyo deserve explicit roles in the scenario analysis without fabricated route shares.

## Evidence

### Comparable 2025 regular-season baseline

Computed from the same nflverse file and filters; [method, provenance and full denominators](../research/ne-sea/README.md). EPA measures expected points added; success is EPA above zero. Negative defensive EPA allowed is favorable. Designed runs exclude QB scrambles. These rates are not opponent-adjusted.

| Measure | NE offense | SEA defense allowed | SEA offense | NE defense allowed |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Eligible plays | 1,017 | 1,061 | 997 | 966 |
| EPA per play | +0.159 | −0.116 | +0.033 | −0.047 |
| Success | 48.5% | 39.7% | 46.3% | 44.6% |
| Early-down EPA | +0.132 | −0.065 | +0.045 | +0.009 |
| Dropback EPA | +0.306 | −0.075 | +0.121 | −0.038 |
| Sacks / dropbacks | 48/614 (7.8%) | 47/693 (6.8%) | 27/520 (5.2%) | 35/591 (5.9%) |
| Designed-run EPA | −0.064 | −0.194 | −0.063 | −0.062 |
| Designed-run yards/carry | 4.46 | 3.49 | 4.25 | 4.10 |
| 20+ yard completions / dropbacks | 11.2% | 6.3% | 11.0% | 7.1% |
| 10+ yard runs / designed runs | 9.9% | 4.9% | 11.1% | 9.1% |
| TDs / qualifying red-zone possessions | 39/61 (63.9%) | 27/54 (50.0%) | 33/59 (55.9%) | 28/44 (63.6%) |

NE used dropbacks on 58.1% of 396 neutral plays; SEA on 52.2% of 318. No-huddle rates were 1.0% and 7.2%, respectively. Our neutral filter uses Q1/Q3 and a score margin within seven. It establishes a tendency comparison, **not seconds-per-snap pace**. Custom red-zone possession counts must not be mixed with an official/vendor denominator.

Postseason EPA/play was NE offense −0.189 (251 plays), NE defense allowed −0.254 (260), SEA offense +0.108 (188), and SEA defense allowed −0.163 (184). NE's sacks rose to 21/156 dropbacks (13.5%). The playoffs involved different opponents and only four NE/three SEA games; they include the Super Bowl. They expose downside but do not replace the larger regular-season baseline.

### What February actually establishes

The official [Super Bowl LX gamebook](https://static.clubs.nfl.com/image/upload/patriots/ixew72ey6khgsxvpquw7.pdf), published February 8, records:

| Item | SEA | NE |
|---|---:|---:|
| Final points | 29 | 13 |
| Net yards | 335 | 331 |
| Sacks taken | 1 | 6 |
| Red-zone TDs / trips | 1/4 | 1/1 |
| Third downs | 4/16 | 6/15 |
| Punt net average | 47.3 | 42.5 |

Myers made five field goals. Seattle led 19–0 before New England's first touchdown, and Nwosu added a fourth-quarter interception return TD. Kupp drew 12 targets, Smith-Njigba 10; Barner caught four passes for 54 yards and a TD. Similar final yardage therefore did not mean similar competitive offensive performance. Nor did the final margin prove Seattle's offense dominated every phase. (Gamebook score, individual, team and punt tables.)

[Evan Lazar's February 10 film review](https://www.patriots.com/news/after-further-review-drake-maye-breakdown-defense-review-and-quick-hit-film-notes-from-the-loss-to-the-seahawks-in-super-bowl-lx) attributes five of Seattle's six sacks to four-man rushes. His examples show simulated pressure changing the protection count and creating an overload despite a nominally sufficient number of blockers. He also notes that late-game coverage influenced whole-game coverage frequencies. This is attributed analyst film work; ARCH did not independently chart all-22 footage.

ARCH inference: a credible rematch plan must change down-and-distance and protection answers, not just add receivers. A back assigned two rushers is not evidence of two individual blocking failures. Late production and whole-game coverage labels need score-state context.

### Special teams and field position

The official 2025 [SEA](https://www.seahawks.com/team/stats/2025/reg) and [NE](https://www.patriots.com/team/stats/2025/reg) regular-season tables show Myers 41/48 on field goals (85.4%), including 9/12 from 50+, versus Borregales 27/32 (84.4%) and 4/4 from 50+. Dickson's net punting average was 42.2; Baringer's was 40.6. Marcus Jones averaged 17.3 yards on 21 punt returns with two TDs. Those are distance/opportunity-dependent outcomes, not isolated player grades.

Baringer's historical number should not be assigned to his replacement. [Mitch Wishnowsky's official biography](https://www.patriots.com/team/players-roster/mitch-wishnowsky/) lists 38 Buffalo punts in 2025, 45.3 gross and 42.0 net, with 18 inside the 20. An experienced practice-squad elevation is not automatically a weak punter. Existing records already identified Schooler's absence and Shaheed's return role. ARCH inference: SEA's continuity matters, but NE's return threat and Wishnowsky's own baseline prevent a blanket large special-teams downgrade.
