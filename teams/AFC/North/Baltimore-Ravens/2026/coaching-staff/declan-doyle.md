# Declan Doyle — Baltimore Ravens

- Role: Offensive coordinator and expected play-caller
- Joined team: 2026 (offensive coordinator, Chicago Bears, 2025)
- Last verified: 2026-08-13

## Responsibilities

Doyle designs and is reported to call the 2026 Ravens offense, replacing Todd Monken, who left to become the Cleveland Browns' offensive coordinator. Hired on 2026-02-02, Doyle is among the youngest play-callers in the NFL and arrives after serving as offensive coordinator on Ben Johnson's first Chicago staff in 2025. His central task is to build a system around Lamar Jackson's dual-threat profile and Derrick Henry's downhill running while integrating an extended Zay Flowers.

## Scheme and tendencies

Doyle's system is not yet established in Baltimore, and his single season as a coordinator is a thin sample. The reasonable expectation is a modern, motion- and play-action-heavy offense in the lineage of the staff he came from, adapted to Jackson's mobility and Henry's early-down volume. Whether he preserves the 2024–2025 Monken structures that produced elite efficiency or reshapes concepts, protections, and personnel groupings is the single biggest offensive question of the Ravens' season and must be charted, not assumed.

## Personnel usage

The skill personnel are largely fixed: Jackson at quarterback, Henry as the lead back with Justice Hill in a change-of-pace and passing-down role, Flowers and Rashod Bateman as the top receivers, and Mark Andrews at tight end after the departures of Isaiah Likely and Charlie Kolar. The variables Doyle controls are the target distribution (Flowers's share, Bateman's role, tight-end usage without Likely), Henry's early-down workload versus Hill's passing-down snaps, and how much designed-quarterback-run and read-option volume Jackson carries.

## Pace and situational decisions

No Baltimore sample exists. Track early-down pass rate, play-action and pre-snap-motion frequency, red-zone personnel, and two-minute operation. A young first-year Ravens play-caller running an offense with a former-MVP quarterback is a high-variance profile until the tendencies are observed.

## Fantasy implications

- Lamar Jackson's fantasy floor is anchored by his rushing, but his passing ceiling and the receivers' value depend on Doyle's system and target concentration.
- Derrick Henry's touchdown-heavy value hinges on early-down volume and goal-line usage under a new play-caller; Jackson's own goal-line rushing remains a competing factor.
- Zay Flowers is the clearest target-share bet after his extension, but the ordering behind him (Bateman, tight end, backs) is unsettled until routes are charted.
- Mark Andrews is a bet on route participation and red-zone targets in a room thinned by the Likely and Kolar departures.
