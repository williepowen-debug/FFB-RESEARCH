# Finding: Baltimore returns its coverage stars but has an open safety spot and an unestablished coverage identity under Weaver

## Scope

- Unit: secondary (cornerbacks and safeties)
- Area: role competitions and coverage projection under a new coordinator
- Season: 2026
- Status: active preseason baseline
- Last verified: 2026-08-13

## Finding

Baltimore returns the high-end pieces of its secondary — safety Kyle Hamilton, cornerback Marlon Humphrey, and cornerback Nate Wiggins — but changed defensive coordinators (Anthony Weaver for Zach Orr) with new secondary coaches, lost starting safety Alohi Gilman, and added Jaylinn Hawkins in competition for the vacancy. The talent at the top is strong, but the second safety spot and, more importantly, the unit's coverage identity under Weaver are unestablished and must be charted.

## Evidence

### Facts

- Kyle Hamilton, Marlon Humphrey, and Nate Wiggins return as the core of the secondary; Chidobe Awuzie was re-signed as the No. 3 corner (one year, $5M).
- Starting safety Alohi Gilman departed for Kansas City (three years, $24M); Baltimore signed Jaylinn Hawkins (two years, $10M) to compete for the spot.
- Anthony Weaver was hired as defensive coordinator (2026-02-02) with new position coaches, including a new secondary/pass-game coordinator (Mike Mickens) and safeties coach (P.J. Volker).
- Baltimore drafted cornerback Chandler Rivers (Round 5, Duke) for slot and depth competition.

### Inferences

- The most fantasy- and matchup-relevant question is how Weaver deploys Kyle Hamilton — as a deep safety, a box defender, or a movable chess piece — which shapes both Hamilton's IDP profile and the defense's coverage structure.
- The open safety spot next to Hamilton (Hawkins versus a returning or drafted option) affects how much single-high or split-safety Baltimore can play.
- A new coordinator and new secondary staff mean the 2025 coverage tape is a weak guide; early-season coverage rates should be treated as unknown.

## Fantasy implication

- Kyle Hamilton's IDP value depends on his alignment under Weaver; a box-heavy role raises his tackle-and-splash floor.
- Opposing offenses face early-season uncertainty about Baltimore's coverage shell; monitor whether the secondary is exploitable through the seam and slot before roles settle.
- Baltimore's cornerbacks (Humphrey, Wiggins) support the team-defense outlook, but the coverage identity is the swing factor for D/ST projection.

## Assessment

- Confidence: low
- Reason: the personnel are known, but roles, alignments, and coverage rates under a new coordinator are not yet charted.
- Fact/inference boundary: the returning players, the Gilman departure, the Hawkins signing, and the coordinator change are facts; the role and coverage-identity projections are inference.
- What would invalidate this: a quickly settled safety pairing and a clear coverage identity in the preseason, or Hamilton being locked into a single defined role.
- Next review: first two preseason games with secondary snap and alignment charting.
