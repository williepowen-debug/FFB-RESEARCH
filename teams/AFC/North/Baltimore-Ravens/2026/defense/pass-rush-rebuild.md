# Finding: Baltimore rebuilt its edge rush by pivoting from a voided Maxx Crosby trade to signing Trey Hendrickson

## Scope

- Unit: edge rush and defensive front
- Area: personnel acquisition, pressure projection, and rotation under a new coordinator
- Season: 2026
- Status: active preseason baseline
- Last verified: 2026-08-13

## Finding

Baltimore reshaped its pass rush in a single dramatic sequence. The Ravens agreed to trade two first-round picks to Las Vegas for edge rusher Maxx Crosby, then voided the deal after Crosby failed his physical over knee concerns, and within roughly a day signed Trey Hendrickson (four years, $112M, $60M guaranteed) away from division-rival Cincinnati. Under new defensive coordinator Anthony Weaver, Hendrickson is the projected premier edge rusher, but the surrounding rotation, the second edge, and the interior rush are unsettled, and no 2026 pressure data exists yet.

## Evidence

### Facts

- Baltimore agreed in principle to acquire Maxx Crosby from Las Vegas for two first-round picks; the trade was rescinded due to medical concerns from Crosby's physical (he had offseason knee surgery).
- The Ravens signed Trey Hendrickson on approximately 2026-03-18 (four years, $112M, $60M guaranteed), keeping the four-time Pro Bowl edge rusher in the AFC North but moving him from Cincinnati to Baltimore.
- Anthony Weaver was hired as defensive coordinator (2026-02-02), replacing Zach Orr, with a new position-coach group.
- Veteran interior lineman Calais Campbell was re-added (one year, $5.5M) and John Jenkins re-signed; rookie edge Zion Young (Round 2, Missouri) and DT Rayshaun Benny (Round 7, Michigan) were drafted.
- Interior anchor Nnamdi Madubuike and linebacker Roquan Smith return.

### Inferences

- Adding a proven high-sack edge rusher raises Baltimore's pressure ceiling and, indirectly, its takeaway and defense/special-teams scoring potential.
- The Crosby episode means the front is built around Hendrickson rather than a two-star edge tandem; the second edge and the rotation behind him are the production swing factors.
- Weaver's scheme determines how the pressure is generated (four-man rush versus pressure packages), which affects whether the sack production is concentrated or distributed.

## Fantasy implication

- Hendrickson is the Ravens' most fantasy-relevant defensive addition for IDP and team-defense value, contingent on health and Weaver's usage.
- Baltimore's defense/special-teams outlook improves if the added edge pressure translates into takeaways and short fields, but that is a projection until charted.
- Rookie edge Zion Young is a dynasty/IDP watch for rotational snaps; treat him as developmental until preseason usage confirms a role.

## Assessment

- Confidence: low
- Reason: the acquisition is a known fact, but 2026 pressure production, rotation, and scheme fit under a new coordinator are not yet observed.
- Fact/inference boundary: the voided Crosby trade, the Hendrickson signing, and the coordinator change are facts; the pressure and takeaway projections are inference.
- What would invalidate this: preseason and early-season pressure data showing Hendrickson underperforming or the front failing to generate rush without blitzing, or a clearly productive rotation emerging.
- Next review: first two preseason games with pass-rush and rotation charting.
