# Baltimore Ravens — 2026 Coaching Regime Change Over a Continuous Skill Core

- Conference/division: AFC North
- Last verified: 2026-08-13
- Scope: changes from the 2025 team to the 2026 preseason roster and staff
- Build tier: Core

Baltimore changed its entire coaching leadership without dismantling its roster. Head coach John Harbaugh was dismissed after 18 seasons following an 8-9 finish, and Jesse Minter was hired to install an all-new, unusually young coordinator group. General manager Eric DeCosta and the offensive skill core — Lamar Jackson, Derrick Henry, Zay Flowers, Rashod Bateman, and Mark Andrews — stayed in place. The pass rush was rebuilt in one dramatic sequence, and the interior offensive line and tight-end room turned over. This is a coaching reset over a continuous core — a different shape of change from the Miami Dolphins' full regime-and-roster reset elsewhere in the conference.

## Fantasy-relevant snapshot

### Confirmed baseline

- John Harbaugh was dismissed on 2026-01-06; Jesse Minter was hired as head coach on 2026-01-22. GM Eric DeCosta remains.
- New coordinators: Declan Doyle (offense, from Chicago; reported play-caller), Anthony Weaver (defense, from Miami), and Anthony Levine Sr. (special teams). Former OC Todd Monken left to become the Cleveland Browns' head coach.
- Lamar Jackson returns at quarterback; Derrick Henry returns as the lead back; Zay Flowers was extended (four years, $140M through 2030); Rashod Bateman and Mark Andrews return.
- The pass rush was rebuilt through free agency: Baltimore agreed to trade two first-round picks for Maxx Crosby, voided the deal after his failed physical, and then signed Trey Hendrickson (four years, $112M) away from division-rival Cincinnati.
- The interior offensive line lost center Tyler Linderbaum (to Las Vegas); the tight-end room lost Isaiah Likely (Giants) and Charlie Kolar (Chargers). Baltimore added guard John Simpson and drafted first-round guard Vega Ioane.
- Baltimore made 11 draft selections, weighted toward the trenches and pass catchers.

### Working fantasy hypotheses

These are projections to test, not established 2026 outcomes:

- Lamar Jackson's rushing floor is stable, but his passing ceiling and the receivers' value depend on the system new play-caller Declan Doyle installs. The scheme, not the talent, is the variable.
- Derrick Henry remains the lead back, but his touchdown ceiling depends on early-down volume under a new play-caller and continues to compete with Jackson's goal-line rushing.
- Zay Flowers is the clearest projected WR1 after his extension; the ordering behind him (Bateman, tight end, backs) is unsettled until routes are charted.
- The defense should be more disruptive off the edge with Hendrickson, but its coverage identity under Weaver and a partly rebuilt secondary is unestablished.

## Offensive identity

### Confirmed changes

The skill personnel are largely continuous — Jackson, Henry, Flowers, Bateman, and Andrews all return — but the offensive system changes hands from Todd Monken to Declan Doyle, one of the NFL's youngest play-callers. The tight-end room lost Isaiah Likely and Charlie Kolar, removing the two-tight-end passing package and leaving Andrews as the clear TE1 with two drafted rookies (Matthew Hibner, Josh Cuevas) and blocking veteran Durham Smythe behind him. The interior line was reset by the loss of center Tyler Linderbaum, with guard John Simpson signed and rookie Vega Ioane drafted in the first round.

### Questions to resolve

- Whether Doyle preserves the efficient 2024–2025 structure or reshapes concepts, protections, and personnel.
- Zay Flowers's target share and whether the offense concentrates or distributes targets.
- Derrick Henry's early-down workload versus Justice Hill's passing-down role, and the goal-line split with Jackson.
- Mark Andrews's route participation and red-zone role in a thinned tight-end room.
- The rebuilt interior line's protection and run-blocking after Linderbaum's departure.

## Defensive matchup profile

### Confirmed changes

Baltimore installed a new defensive coordinator (Anthony Weaver for Zach Orr) with new position coaches, rebuilt the edge rush by signing Trey Hendrickson after the Maxx Crosby trade collapsed, retained interior and coverage anchors (Nnamdi Madubuike, Roquan Smith, Kyle Hamilton, Marlon Humphrey, Nate Wiggins), and reworked the safety group after Alohi Gilman's departure with Jaylinn Hawkins added in competition.

### Questions to resolve

- Weaver's coverage identity (single-high versus split-safety) and pressure rate.
- Whether Hendrickson delivers premier edge production and how the second edge and interior rotation shake out.
- The starting safety spot alongside Kyle Hamilton and how aggressively Hamilton is used in the box.
- Whether the front's added juice translates into more takeaways and short fields.

These items affect opponent passing-game and protection matchups, but no final schematic profile should be assigned before preseason and early-season evidence. The durable defensive baseline is maintained in the [defensive ecosystem module](defense/README.md).

## Coaching and scheme

Baltimore dismissed John Harbaugh on 2026-01-06 and hired Jesse Minter on 2026-01-22. The coordinator changes were:

| Unit | 2025 | 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Offense | Todd Monken | Declan Doyle |
| Defense | Zach Orr | Anthony Weaver |
| Special teams | Chris Horton | Anthony Levine Sr. |

Minter is a first-time head coach from the defensive side who delegates offensive play-calling to Doyle; the offense should be attributed to Doyle's system, not the head coach. Offensive-line coach Dwayne Ledford is a notable point of continuity. The complete role-level staff and retention flags are in [staff.csv](coaching-staff/staff.csv); authority and coach profiles are in the [coaching intelligence module](coaching-staff/README.md).

### Analytical projection

The reasonable prior is a 2026 offense built around Jackson and Henry but running through an unproven young play-caller, and a defense whose coverage identity is to-be-charted with an upgraded pass rush. Verify through preseason personnel groupings, target distribution, coverage shells, and pressure rates rather than offseason narratives.

## Special teams

Baltimore hired special-teams coordinator Anthony Levine Sr., lost punter Jordan Stout (to the Giants), and drafted punter Ryan Eckley to compete for the job. Long snapper Nick Moore was re-signed for continuity. Track the punter competition, return roles, and kicker operation through the preseason.
