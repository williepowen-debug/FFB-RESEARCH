# Finding: Zay Flowers is the anchored WR1, but the pass-catching order behind him is a new-scheme question

## Scope

- Unit: wide receivers and tight ends
- Area: alignment, route participation, and target hierarchy around Lamar Jackson
- Season: 2026
- Status: active preseason baseline
- Last verified: 2026-08-13

## Finding

Baltimore's pass-catching group returns its top pieces — Zay Flowers, Rashod Bateman, and Mark Andrews — but the offense changes play-callers from Todd Monken to Declan Doyle, and the tight-end room lost Isaiah Likely and Charlie Kolar. Flowers, freshly extended (four years, $140M through 2030), has the strongest claim on stable, high-end target volume. The ordering behind him — Bateman as the No. 2, Andrews as a thinned-room TE1, and rookies competing for snaps — depends on a system that has not yet been charted with Lamar Jackson, whose target distribution has historically been run-influenced and streaky.

## Evidence

### Facts

- Zay Flowers was extended on 2026-08-04 (four years, $140M), signaling a long-term WR1 commitment.
- Rashod Bateman and Mark Andrews both return; the projected top of the depth chart is Flowers and Bateman at receiver with Andrews at tight end.
- Isaiah Likely departed for the New York Giants (three years, $40M) and Charlie Kolar for the Los Angeles Chargers (three years, $24.3M), removing the second and blocking tight ends and the two-TE passing package.
- Baltimore drafted receivers Ja'Kobi Lane (Round 3, USC) and Elijah Sarratt (Round 4, Indiana) and tight ends Matthew Hibner (Round 4, SMU) and Josh Cuevas (Round 5, Alabama), and signed blocking tight end Durham Smythe.
- Declan Doyle, not Monken, designs and is reported to call the 2026 offense.

### Current role hypotheses

| Player | Best current hypothesis | Confidence | What would change it |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Zay Flowers | anchored WR1 and highest-volume target | medium | a run-heavy script or scheme change caps his share |
| Rashod Bateman | primary outside/complementary No. 2 | low | rookie or scheme change reshuffles the perimeter |
| Mark Andrews | clear TE1 with red-zone value in a thinned room | low | age/route-rate decline or a rookie earns passing snaps |
| Ja'Kobi Lane | rookie developmental perimeter receiver | low | injuries or camp performance open a real role |
| Durham Smythe | blocking tight end, low target volume | low | injury forces him into a passing role |

### Inferences

- Flowers's ceiling depends on Doyle's target concentration and on how run-oriented the offense stays with Henry and Jackson; a locked alpha share is not guaranteed even for an extended No. 1.
- Andrews benefits from the Likely and Kolar departures on paper, but his value rides on route participation and red-zone usage, which must be observed under a new play-caller.
- The receiver depth (Bateman plus rookies) is a committee to chart, not a settled order.

## Fantasy implication

- Flowers is the Ravens pass-catcher to prioritize on role and quarterback quality, but a run-heavy, distributed offense caps his target-share ceiling.
- Andrews is a rebound-or-decline bet tied to route rate and red-zone looks in a thinner room; monitor his snap and route share early.
- No Baltimore receiver below Flowers should get a confident season-long volume projection until first-team routes and target distribution are charted under Doyle.

## Assessment

- What would invalidate this: first-team preseason and early-season routes that establish a clearly different order, or a concentrated target share that removes the committee framing behind Flowers.
