# Finding: Chase Brown is the lead back in a pass-first offense, with game script and passing-down usage setting his ceiling

## Scope

- Unit: running backs
- Area: workload, passing-down role, and game-script dependency
- Season: 2026
- Status: active preseason baseline
- Last verified: 2026-08-13

## Finding

Chase Brown returns as Cincinnati's lead back in a retained, pass-first offense. His value rests on holding a clear early-down and passing-down role, which gives him a dual-phase floor, but the Bengals' pass-heavy identity and their reliance on the Chase–Higgins passing game cap the run game's volume, especially in negative game scripts. A rebuilt defense that produces more neutral or ahead scripts would help his rushing workload; a defense that struggles again would push Cincinnati back toward pass-heavy catch-up football.

## Evidence

### Facts

- Chase Brown returns as the projected lead back in a fully retained offensive staff.
- The Bengals' offense is pass-first and concentrates volume on the receiver duo, historically limiting running-back rushing share.
- Cincinnati's defense was rebuilt up front (Dexter Lawrence trade; Boye Mafe and Jonathan Allen signings), which could change game scripts if it improves.

### Inferences

- Brown's dual-phase usage is his floor; a defined passing-down role protects his value even in pass-heavy scripts.
- His rushing ceiling is game-script dependent: more leads mean more run volume; more deficits mean fewer carries and more empty/hurry-up looks.
- The backfield behind Brown is a lower-priority depth question until camp clarifies the rotation.

## Fantasy implication

- Brown is a startable lead back whose ceiling is tied to game script and his passing-down role rather than to pure early-down volume.
- Monitor whether the improved defense produces more positive scripts, which would lift his rushing workload.
- Handcuff and depth-back value depends on the camp rotation, which should be charted before it is projected.

## Assessment

- Confidence: medium
- Reason: Brown's lead role is established, but his workload split and game-script environment for 2026 are not yet observed.
- Fact/inference boundary: the returning lead role and the pass-first system are facts; the workload and script projections are inference.
- What would invalidate this: a committee emerging in camp, a clear change in passing-down usage, or a defense that decisively changes Cincinnati's game scripts.
- Next review: preseason usage and the first two regular-season games with snap and route charting.
