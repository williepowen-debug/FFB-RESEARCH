# Finding: Cincinnati's back end is the retained defense's biggest question after a poor 2025 and the loss of Geno Stone

## Scope

- Unit: secondary (cornerbacks and safeties)
- Area: role changes and coverage projection under a retained coordinator
- Season: 2026
- Status: active preseason baseline
- Last verified: 2026-08-13

## Finding

The secondary is the least settled part of a defense that finished near the bottom of the league in 2025 and kept its coordinator. Cincinnati lost starting safety Geno Stone to Buffalo and signed Bryan Cook (three years, $40.25M, from Kansas City) to pair with returning safety Jordan Battle, with Daxton Hill also in the mix. The cornerback picture and the coverage identity under retained coordinator Al Golden are the swing factors for whether the back end improves along with the upgraded front.

## Evidence

### Facts

- Safety Geno Stone departed for Buffalo; Cincinnati signed safety Bryan Cook (three years, $40.25M).
- Returning defensive backs include Jordan Battle and Daxton Hill.
- Cincinnati drafted cornerback Tacario Davis (Round 3, Washington) for outside/depth competition.
- Al Golden was retained as defensive coordinator after a 2025 unit that ranked near the bottom in yards and points allowed.

### Inferences

- The back end is the part of the defense most likely to remain exploitable early, given the coordinator continuity and the roster turnover.
- Bryan Cook and Jordan Battle project as the starting safety pair, but roles and coverage responsibilities are unconfirmed.
- An improved pass rush from the new front could mask coverage weaknesses, but the secondary must still hold up on obvious passing downs.

## Fantasy implication

- Opposing receivers and tight ends are worth monitoring against Cincinnati early while the back end proves whether it improved.
- Cincinnati's D/ST value hinges on the front generating pressure faster than the secondary gives up explosive plays.
- IDP value in the secondary is unsettled; snap-share winners at safety and slot are the players to track.

## Assessment

- Confidence: low
- Reason: the personnel changes are known, but roles, coverage rates, and improvement under the retained coordinator are not yet observed.
- Fact/inference boundary: the Stone departure, the Cook signing, and the coordinator retention are facts; the exploitability and role projections are inference.
- What would invalidate this: a clearly settled and improved secondary in the preseason, or continued explosive-pass problems into the season.
- Next review: first two preseason games with secondary snap and coverage charting.
