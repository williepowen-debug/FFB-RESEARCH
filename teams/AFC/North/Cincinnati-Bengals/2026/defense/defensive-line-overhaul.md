# Finding: Cincinnati replaced Hendrickson by trading a first-round pick for Dexter Lawrence and signing Boye Mafe

## Scope

- Unit: defensive line (edge and interior)
- Area: personnel turnover and pressure projection under a retained coordinator
- Season: 2026
- Status: active preseason baseline
- Last verified: 2026-08-13

## Finding

Cincinnati rebuilt its defensive front after losing franchise sack leader Trey Hendrickson to division-rival Baltimore and edge Joseph Ossai in free agency. In the most aggressive move of its offseason, Cincinnati traded its No. 10 overall pick to the New York Giants for star defensive tackle Dexter Lawrence — the first top-10 pick the franchise has ever traded — and signed edge Boye Mafe (three years, $60M) and interior lineman Jonathan Allen. The front's talent is clearly upgraded on paper, but it is a new group under the same coordinator (Al Golden) whose 2025 defense finished near the bottom of the league, and no 2026 pressure data exists yet.

## Evidence

### Facts

- Trey Hendrickson signed with Baltimore (four years, $112M) and Joseph Ossai with the New York Jets (three years, $34.5M), removing the top of Cincinnati's edge rotation.
- Cincinnati traded the No. 10 overall pick to the New York Giants for Dexter Lawrence on 2026-04-18.
- The Bengals signed edge Boye Mafe (three years, $60M, from Seattle) and interior lineman Jonathan Allen (two years, $25M, from Minnesota).
- Cincinnati drafted edge Cashius Howell (Round 2, Texas A&M) and interior lineman Landon Robinson (Round 7, Navy).
- Defensive coordinator Al Golden was retained.

### Inferences

- Lawrence gives Cincinnati a disruptive interior anchor it lacked, which can collapse pockets and help the edge rush; Mafe is the projected replacement for Hendrickson's edge snaps but is a step down in proven production.
- The unit's ceiling depends on whether Golden can convert upgraded personnel into pressure without over-blitzing, and on the health of veteran additions.
- Losing a premier edge and replacing it with a committee shifts the pressure profile from star-driven to front-wide.

## Fantasy implication

- Dexter Lawrence and Boye Mafe are the fantasy-relevant IDP and team-defense pieces; Lawrence's interior disruption is the highest-leverage addition.
- Cincinnati's defense/special-teams outlook improves only if the new front lifts a unit that struggled in 2025; treat that as a projection, not a given.
- Rookie edge Cashius Howell is a dynasty/IDP watch for rotational snaps.

## Assessment

- Confidence: low
- Reason: the acquisitions are known facts, but 2026 pressure production and scheme fit under the retained coordinator are unobserved.
- Fact/inference boundary: the Hendrickson/Ossai departures, the Lawrence trade, and the Mafe/Allen signings are facts; the pressure and improvement projections are inference.
- What would invalidate this: preseason and early-season pressure data showing the front failing to generate rush, or clearly exceeding the 2025 baseline.
- Next review: first two preseason games with pass-rush and rotation charting.
