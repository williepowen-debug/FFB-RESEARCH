# Cincinnati Bengals — 2026 Staff Continuity and a Defensive-Front Rebuild

- Conference/division: AFC North
- Last verified: 2026-08-13
- Scope: changes from the 2025 team to the 2026 preseason roster and staff
- Build tier: Core

Cincinnati made the opposite choice from most of its division: after a 6-11 season, it kept its entire coaching staff — head coach Zac Taylor, offensive coordinator Dan Pitcher, and, most notably, defensive coordinator Al Golden — and reinvested in the roster instead. The offense returns intact around Joe Burrow, Ja'Marr Chase, and Tee Higgins, while the defense was rebuilt in the trenches: Cincinnati lost franchise sack leader Trey Hendrickson to division-rival Baltimore, then traded its first-round pick for star defensive tackle Dexter Lawrence and signed edge Boye Mafe. This is continuity of leadership over an elite offense, with the season's variance concentrated on a retained defensive staff working with new personnel — and on Burrow's health.

## Fantasy-relevant snapshot

### Confirmed baseline

- After a 6-11 season, Cincinnati retained Zac Taylor, personnel lead Duke Tobin, and the entire coordinator group (Pitcher, Golden, Simmons).
- Joe Burrow, Ja'Marr Chase, Tee Higgins, and Chase Brown all return; the offense is fully continuous.
- The defense lost franchise sack leader Trey Hendrickson (to Baltimore, four years, $112M) and edge Joseph Ossai; safety Geno Stone left for Buffalo.
- Cincinnati traded its No. 10 overall pick to the New York Giants for defensive tackle Dexter Lawrence — the first top-10 pick it has ever traded — and signed edge Boye Mafe (three years, $60M) and interior lineman Jonathan Allen, plus safety Bryan Cook.
- Without a first-round pick, Cincinnati made seven selections, heavily weighted toward defense.

### Working fantasy hypotheses

These are projections to test, not established 2026 outcomes:

- The offense's identity is stable and pass-heavy; Chase and Higgins remain premium options and Burrow a top quarterback — conditional on Burrow's health, the biggest single swing factor.
- Chase Brown remains the lead back; his dual-phase usage and the game script set his ceiling.
- The tight-end role is unsettled after Noah Fant's departure.
- The defensive front should be more disruptive with Lawrence and Mafe, but the retained coordinator and an unproven back end leave the unit's real improvement uncertain.

## Offensive identity

### Confirmed changes

The offense barely changed: same head coach and play-caller (Taylor), same coordinator (Pitcher), and the same core of Burrow, Chase, Higgins, and Chase Brown. The one notable subtraction is tight end Noah Fant, leaving the pass-catching tight-end role open behind the receiver duo. The story here is continuity and health, not scheme change.

### Questions to resolve

- Burrow's health and availability across the full season.
- The target split between Chase and Higgins and whether either separates as the clear alpha.
- Chase Brown's workload and passing-down role, and the game scripts a rebuilt defense produces.
- Who fills the pass-catching tight-end role after Fant.

## Defensive matchup profile

### Confirmed changes

Cincinnati kept defensive coordinator Al Golden but overhauled the front: it lost Trey Hendrickson and Joseph Ossai, traded a first-round pick for Dexter Lawrence, and signed Boye Mafe and Jonathan Allen. The secondary lost Geno Stone and added Bryan Cook alongside returners Jordan Battle and Daxton Hill.

### Questions to resolve

- Whether Lawrence and the new edges generate pressure without heavy blitzing.
- Whether the coverage and explosive-play problems of the 2025 defense improve under the same coordinator.
- The safety pairing and slot/outside cornerback roles.
- Whether the front's upgrade translates into takeaways and better game scripts for the offense.

These items affect opponent passing-game and protection matchups, but no final schematic profile should be assigned before preseason and early-season evidence. The durable defensive baseline is maintained in the [defensive ecosystem module](defense/README.md).

Cincinnati retained its leadership across the board. The coordinator continuity was:
