# Finding: Pittsburgh reworked its secondary with veteran additions into an unestablished coverage scheme

## Scope

- Unit: secondary (cornerbacks and safeties)
- Area: role competitions and coverage projection under a new coordinator
- Season: 2026
- Status: active preseason baseline
- Last verified: 2026-08-13

## Finding

Pittsburgh reshaped its secondary this offseason, signing cornerback Jamel Dean and safeties Jaquan Brisker and Darnell Savage while re-signing cornerback Asante Samuel Jr. and drafting corner Daylen Everette and safety Robert Spears-Jennings. Combined with the new defensive coordinator (Patrick Graham), the back end's roles and coverage identity are unestablished and must be charted, which raises early-season variance against the pass.

## Evidence

### Facts

- Cornerback Jamel Dean signed (three years, $36.75M) from Tampa Bay; safety Jaquan Brisker (one year, $5.5M) from Chicago; safety Darnell Savage on a one-year deal.
- Cornerback Asante Samuel Jr. was re-signed (one year, $4M).
- Pittsburgh drafted cornerback Daylen Everette (Round 3) and safety Robert Spears-Jennings (Round 7).
- Patrick Graham, a disguise-oriented coordinator, replaced Teryl Austin.

### Inferences

- The starting cornerback and safety pairings, and the slot role, are open competitions to chart; Dean projects as an outside starter but the rest is unsettled.
- A reworked secondary plus a new coordinator raises the chance the back end is exploitable early before roles and communication settle.
- Graham's disguise tendencies could mask or expose the new personnel depending on how quickly they learn the scheme.

## Fantasy implication

- Opposing receivers and tight ends are worth monitoring against Pittsburgh early while the coverage roles settle.
- Pittsburgh's defense/special-teams value depends on how quickly the secondary coheres behind a strong pass rush.
- IDP value in this secondary is unsettled; track snap-share winners rather than name recognition.

## Assessment

- Confidence: low
- Reason: the personnel moves are known, but roles, coverage rates, and scheme fit under a new coordinator are not charted.
- Fact/inference boundary: the signings, re-signing, and draft picks are facts; the role and exploitability projections are inference.
- What would invalidate this: a quickly settled depth chart and coverage identity, or clear starters emerging in the preseason.
- Next review: first two preseason games with secondary snap and alignment charting.
