# Finding: Pittsburgh returns elite edge talent into a new defensive scheme under Patrick Graham

## Scope

- Unit: edge rush and defensive front
- Area: pressure production and scheme fit under a new coordinator
- Season: 2026
- Status: active preseason baseline
- Last verified: 2026-08-13

## Finding

Pittsburgh retained its premier edge and interior talent — T.J. Watt, Alex Highsmith, and Cameron Heyward — and added interior lineman Sebastian Joseph-Day, but installed a new defensive coordinator in Patrick Graham. The front's talent is a known quantity; the variable is how Graham's multiple, disguise-oriented scheme deploys it and whether it sustains a high pressure rate. No 2026 pressure data exists yet.

## Evidence

### Facts

- T.J. Watt, Alex Highsmith, and Cameron Heyward return as the core of the front.
- Sebastian Joseph-Day was signed (two years, $11M) for the interior rotation; rookie Gabriel Rubio (Round 6) adds depth.
- Patrick Graham was hired as defensive coordinator, replacing Teryl Austin, and brings a disguise-heavy multiple-front background.

### Inferences

- Watt remains the defense's central pressure and takeaway generator; Graham's scheme affects whether that production is scheme-aided or self-created.
- Heyward's age is a factor for the interior; Joseph-Day's snaps and the rotation behind Heyward are worth monitoring.
- A new coordinator can change alignment and blitz rates, so 2025 pressure tape is only a partial guide to 2026.

## Fantasy implication

- T.J. Watt is the premier Steelers IDP and team-defense asset; his sack and pressure floor is the safest projection on the roster, with scheme as an upside/downside modifier.
- Alex Highsmith is a secondary IDP edge to monitor for snap share and pressure under the new scheme.
- Pittsburgh's defense/special-teams value leans on the pass rush generating takeaways, which is a projection until charted with the new secondary.

## Assessment

- Confidence: low
- Reason: the personnel are known, but 2026 pressure production and scheme fit under a new coordinator are not observed.
- Fact/inference boundary: the returning players, the Joseph-Day signing, and the coordinator change are facts; the pressure and usage projections are inference.
- What would invalidate this: preseason and early-season pressure data showing a materially different rush structure, or a clear decline or spike in production.
- Next review: first two preseason games with pass-rush and rotation charting.
