# Josh McDaniels — New England Patriots

- Role: Offensive coordinator and play-caller
- Joined team: 2025 (returned to New England under Mike Vrabel)
- Last verified: 2026-09-09

## Responsibilities

McDaniels returns for a second season as offensive coordinator and play-caller, having overseen the 2025 offense that reached the Super Bowl with Drake Maye. The system carries forward with a materially upgraded personnel group: A.J. Brown and Romeo Doubs at receiver, Alijah Vera-Tucker and first-round tackle Caleb Lomu on the line, and a returning Stevenson/Henderson backfield. The official coaches roster (verified 2026-09-09) lists Thomas Brown (tight ends / passing game coordinator), Doug Marrone (offensive line), Todd Downing (receivers), Tony Dews (running backs), and Ashton Grant (quarterbacks) as his position staff, with Charles London added as offensive analyst and Jonathan Decoster as offensive-line assistant in March.

## Scheme and tendencies

McDaniels runs a veteran, multiple, personnel-driven pro system with heavy situational packaging and play-action. With a proven alpha receiver now in the building, the specific question is whether the offense concentrates volume through A.J. Brown or continues distributing across Doubs, the slot, and the backfield. His history includes both high-concentration and distributed passing offenses depending on personnel; charting is required.

## Personnel usage

Watch McDaniels's target distribution (Brown vs. a spread approach), the Stevenson/Henderson early-down and passing-down split once both are healthy, and how the interior line holds after the Garrett Bradbury trade.

Week 1 status (verified 2026-09-09): the receiver room is six deep (Brown, Doubs, DeMario Douglas, Mack Hollins, Kyle Williams, Efton Chism III) after Kayshon Boutte was traded on 2026-08-25; the team site credits Williams's preseason with making Boutte expendable. The starting five is Campbell (LT), Vera-Tucker (LG), Jared Wilson (C), Onwenu (RG), Moses (RT), set before camp per the official 2026-08-30 roster analysis; Lomu is the top backup tackle, and Greg Van Roten (signed 2026-08-18) is the primary interior backup with Ben Brown (knee) OUT. Henderson (ankle) is OUT for Week 1; Corey Kiner (acquired 2026-08-28, about five practices) is the No. 2 back and Lan Larison was elevated as the third back. Maye's mobility should continue to support movement and play-action concepts.

## Pace and situational decisions

McDaniels's red-zone and third-down menus are the areas where an alpha receiver most changes outcomes. Track whether Brown draws defined red-zone and third-down targets, which would raise the offense's scoring efficiency and Maye's touchdown projection.

## Fantasy implications

- A.J. Brown's arrival gives the offense a defined No. 1 target for the first time in this era, which supports both his own projection and Maye's ceiling.
- The backfield timeshare is the main drag on running-back projections once Henderson returns; McDaniels's usage split is the deciding variable. Week 1 shows only how he uses Stevenson and a five-practice acquisition.
- Tight end Hunter Henry's role and rookie Eli Raridon's development are secondary passing-game questions.

## Statements and reporting

Confirmed: McDaniels returns as offensive coordinator and play-caller; the Patriots added A.J. Brown, Doubs, Vera-Tucker, Lomu, Van Roten, and Kiner, and traded Boutte. Analytical: whether the offense concentrates targets on Brown is the key structural question, pending charting.

## Reliability cautions

A continuous scheme with new personnel can still change its distribution and tempo. Do not assume 2025 usage patterns carry over now that the weapon hierarchy has changed, and do not read Week 1 backfield usage as evidence on the Stevenson–Henderson split. Establish target and backfield splits from early-season charting.
