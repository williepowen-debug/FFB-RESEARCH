# Mike Vrabel — New England Patriots

- Role: Head coach
- Joined team: 2025 (16th head coach in franchise history)
- Last verified: 2026-09-09

## Responsibilities

Vrabel enters his second season having led New England to a 2025 Super Bowl appearance (a loss to Seattle) in his first year, with Drake Maye finishing as an MVP runner-up. He controls whole-team standards, staff structure, and game management, and holds strong influence over roster construction. Offensive coordinator Josh McDaniels and special-teams coordinator Jeremy Springer return; the notable staff change is on defense, where Zak Kuhr was promoted to defensive coordinator on 2026-03-16 and 2025 coordinator Terrell Williams moved to assistant head coach. Per the official announcement, Kuhr had already been the de facto coordinator and play-caller for the final 20 games of 2025 during Williams's medical leave, and Vrabel described Williams's new role as "a strong role on the defense" with emphasis on the front.

This is a continuity-and-ascension situation, not a rebuild: the head coach, quarterback, and offensive play-caller all return, and the front office spent aggressively to build around Maye.

## Scheme and tendencies

Vrabel's teams are associated with physical, situationally disciplined football and an aggressive, multiple defense. With McDaniels running the offense, the offensive scheme is a veteran McDaniels system tailored to Maye's mobility and arm. The 2026 defensive question is whether Kuhr carries his 2025 playoff formula (more blitz and man coverage) into a full season; treat any specific front or pressure label as a hypothesis until charted.

## Personnel usage

The offseason was built around Maye: the Patriots signed receiver Romeo Doubs, traded for alpha receiver A.J. Brown, added guard Alijah Vera-Tucker, and drafted tackle Caleb Lomu in the first round to upgrade protection and weapons. The backfield remains a Rhamondre Stevenson–TreVeyon Henderson tandem. On defense, edge Dre'Mont Jones and safety Kevin Byard headline the additions.

Week 1 status (verified 2026-09-09): the staff kept the starting five (Campbell, Vera-Tucker, Wilson, Onwenu, Moses) set from before camp with Lomu a backup; traded Kayshon Boutte (2026-08-25) rather than carry a seventh receiver; traded for RB Corey Kiner (2026-08-28) over in-house RB3 options and released rookie Jam Miller; and opened Harold Landry III on reserve/PUP. Henderson (ankle, injured 2026-08-24) is OUT for Week 1, so Stevenson leads by availability with Kiner and elevated Lan Larison behind him.

## Pace and situational decisions

Vrabel has an established reputation for fourth-down aggression and situational sharpness. With a Super Bowl run already logged, the 2026 questions are whether the offense takes another step with upgraded weapons and whether the defense holds its level without Landry for at least four games.

## Fantasy implications

- This is the division's clearest "ascending contender" environment: a rising franchise quarterback with a genuinely upgraded supporting cast.
- The A.J. Brown trade gives New England a defined alpha receiver — a different structure than Buffalo's and Miami's more distributed rooms.
- The Stevenson/Henderson backfield is a live timeshare that caps each back's ceiling once both are healthy; Week 1 is a Henderson-absent sample.
