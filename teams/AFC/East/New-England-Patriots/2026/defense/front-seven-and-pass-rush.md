# Finding: New England added edge and interior help around a returning core, under a new play-caller

## Scope

- Unit: defensive line, edge rushers, and linebackers
- Area: pass-rush personnel, rotation, and pressure projection under a new coordinator
- Season: 2026
- Status: active preseason baseline
- Last verified: 2026-08-13

## Finding

New England reached the Super Bowl in 2025 with a Vrabel-built defense and returns its core into 2026, now called by promoted coordinator Zac Kuhr. The Patriots added edge rusher Dre'Mont Jones in free agency and drafted edge Gabe Jacas (Round 2, Illinois), reinforcing the pass rush around returning front-seven pieces. The unit's identity should be treated as continuous with 2025 but unproven under Kuhr's play-calling until charted.

## Evidence

### Facts

- Dre'Mont Jones signed a three-year, $39.5 million deal to add a proven interior/edge pass rusher.
- New England drafted edge Gabe Jacas (Round 2, No. 55) and linebackers Namdi Obiazor (Round 6, TCU) and Quintayvious Hutchins (Round 7) for front-seven depth.
- The 2025 defense reached the Super Bowl; its front-seven core returns.
- Zac Kuhr is the new defensive coordinator, promoted from within after Terrell Williams moved to assistant head coach.

### Inferences

- Dre'Mont Jones's versatility (interior and edge) gives Kuhr flexible pressure packages, but production depends on how he is deployed.
- Rookie Jacas is the depth swing that could keep the rush fresh; his rotational role is the early indicator.
- Because the core returns, the front seven carries less roster risk than a rebuilt unit; the variance is scheme (Kuhr's pressure design), not personnel churn.

## Fantasy implication

- New England's D/ST and takeaway profile benefit from a returning core plus added pass rush, contingent on Kuhr maintaining the unit's level.
- IDP value for Dre'Mont Jones and the returning edges depends on actual snap shares and Kuhr's blitz/coverage balance.
- A maintained or improved rush would raise New England's overall defensive matchup difficulty.

## Assessment

- Confidence: low
- Reason: personnel is known and the core returns, but Kuhr's scheme rates and the edge rotation are unestablished.
- Fact/inference boundary: signings, the draft, and the 2025 result are facts; the pressure and rotation projections are inference.
- What would invalidate this: a clear edge rotation and pressure identity emerging in the preseason, or a decline under the new coordinator.
- Next review: first two preseason games with pass-rush snap charting.
