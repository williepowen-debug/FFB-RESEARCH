# Finding: Chubb adds a proven edge opposite Rousseau, but production depends on health and Leonhard's pressure design

## Scope

- Unit: defensive line and edge rushers
- Area: pass-rush personnel, rotation, and pressure projection under a new coordinator
- Season: 2026
- Status: active preseason baseline
- Last verified: 2026-08-13

## Finding

Buffalo kept its front-seven anchors (Ed Oliver inside, Greg Rousseau on the edge, linebackers Terrel Bernard and Matt Milano) and added edge rusher Bradley Chubb from Miami on a three-year deal. Chubb gives Buffalo a proven complementary rusher opposite Rousseau, but his snap share and health after his Miami tenure, plus new coordinator Jim Leonhard's pressure design, will determine whether the rush improves. Rookie edge TJ Parker (Round 2, No. 35) and interior draftee Zane Durant add rotational depth.

## Evidence

### Facts

- Bradley Chubb signed a three-year deal on 2026-03-12, arriving from Miami after an 8.5-sack 2025.
- Ed Oliver and Dion Dawkins both remain on the roster; Oliver anchors the interior rush (Dawkins is offensive, listed here only to confirm no front-office fire-sale of veterans).
- Greg Rousseau returns as the primary edge rusher; Bernard and Milano anchor the linebacker level.
- Buffalo drafted edge TJ Parker (Round 2, No. 35, Clemson) and defensive tackle Zane Durant (Round 5) for front-seven rotation.
- Jim Leonhard is the new defensive coordinator; his scheme and blitz/simulated-pressure rates are not yet established for Buffalo.

### Inferences

- Chubb opposite Rousseau is the intended upgrade to a rush that leaned heavily on Rousseau and Oliver, but "proven addition" is not "guaranteed production" given Chubb's injury history.
- Rookie Parker's rotational role is the depth swing that could keep the top rushers fresh.
- Pressure rate will be a function of Leonhard's design as much as personnel; a coverage/disguise identity could produce pressure differently than a high-blitz approach.

## Fantasy implication

- Buffalo's takeaway and short-field creation — and therefore D/ST scoring — hinge on the rush pairing with the rebuilt secondary.
- IDP value for Chubb and Rousseau depends on actual pass-rush snaps and Leonhard's usage, not reputation.
- A healthy Chubb season would raise Buffalo's overall defensive matchup difficulty; his absence would expose the same edge-depth concern that predated the signing.

## Assessment

- Confidence: low
- Reason: the personnel is known, but rotation, health, and the coordinator's pressure scheme are unestablished.
- Fact/inference boundary: signings, returns, and the draft are facts; production and scheme projections are inference.
- What would invalidate this: a clear edge rotation and pressure identity emerging in the preseason, or a Chubb health/role setback.
- Next review: first two preseason games with pass-rush snap charting.
