# Finding: Buffalo reworked its secondary under a new coordinator, leaving nickel and second-safety roles open

## Scope

- Unit: secondary (cornerbacks and safeties)
- Area: personnel turnover, role competitions, and coverage projection under a new coordinator
- Season: 2026
- Status: active preseason baseline
- Last verified: 2026-08-13

## Finding

Buffalo changed defensive coordinators (Jim Leonhard replaces Bobby Babich) and substantially reworked its secondary. The Bills traded slot corner Taron Johnson, released safety Taylor Rapp, signed safeties C.J. Gardner-Johnson and Geno Stone plus corner Dee Alford, re-signed Damar Hamlin, and drafted three defensive backs. The nickel job and the second starting-safety spot alongside Cole Bishop are open competitions, and the unit's coverage identity under Leonhard is unestablished.

## Evidence

### Facts

- Taron Johnson, the longtime starting nickel, was traded to Las Vegas on 2026-03-11 for a sixth-round pick.
- Taylor Rapp was released with a failed-physical designation.
- Buffalo signed C.J. Gardner-Johnson (one-year, 2026-03-13), Geno Stone (2026-03-16; started all 17 games for Cincinnati in 2025), and Dee Alford (three-year, 2026-03-12, from Atlanta), and re-signed Damar Hamlin (one-year, 2026-03-27).
- The Bills drafted cornerback Davison Igbinosun (Round 2, No. 62), safety Jalon Kilgore (Round 5), and cornerback Toriano Pride Jr. (Round 7), investing heavily in the back end.
- Returning secondary pieces include Christian Benford and 2025 first-round cornerback Maxwell Hairston, plus safety Cole Bishop.
- New defensive coordinator Jim Leonhard arrived from Denver with a nearly all-new defensive staff, including cornerbacks coach Jay Valai.

### Inferences

- The nickel vacancy left by Taron Johnson is the most fantasy-relevant opening: whoever wins it (Alford, a returning corner, or a rookie) shapes slot-matchup exploitability.
- A rebuilt back end plus a new coordinator raises early-season variance; opposing passing games may find exploitable coverage until roles settle.
- Gardner-Johnson and Stone provide experienced-but-competing safety options; the box-vs-deep division is unresolved.

## Fantasy implication

- Opposing slot receivers and tight ends are worth monitoring against Buffalo early while the nickel and safety roles settle.
- Buffalo's D/ST value depends on how quickly the secondary coheres and how much pressure the front generates (see the pass-rush finding).
- IDP value in this secondary is unsettled; snap-share winners are the players to track, not preseason reputation.

## Assessment

- Confidence: low
- Reason: personnel moves are known, but roles, coverage rates, and the coordinator's scheme are not yet charted.
- Fact/inference boundary: transactions, the draft, and the coordinator change are facts; role and exploitability projections are inference.
- What would invalidate this: a quickly settled depth chart and coverage identity, or a nickel/safety winner clearly emerging in the preseason.
- Next review: first two preseason games with secondary snap and alignment charting.
