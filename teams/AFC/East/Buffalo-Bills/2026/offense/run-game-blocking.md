# Finding: the run-blocking system carries over almost entirely; the change is one guard spot and a new position coach hired from the same coaching tree

## Scope

- Unit: offensive line, run blocking
- Area: 2025-to-2026 delta in scheme, coaching, personnel, and measured performance
- Season: 2026
- Status: active
- Last verified: 2026-08-20

## Finding

Buffalo is not rebuilding the run game it just led the league with. Four of five
starting spots, the play-caller, the run-game coordinator, the running backs coach,
the assistant offensive line coach, and the scheme itself all carry forward. The real
2025-to-2026 changes are narrow: **left guard**, and a **new offensive line coach**
hired out of the same coaching tree as the man he replaced.

The 2025 baseline is the important context for how little needed changing. Buffalo
finished first in the NFL in rushing yards (2,714), rushing yards per game (159.6),
and rushing touchdowns (30), at 5.0 yards per attempt, and became the first offense in
league history to record 30-plus rushing and 30-plus passing touchdowns in the same
regular season. The line ranked top-three in yards before contact per rush (3.1) with
a 74.7 percent run-block win rate.

**The coaching change is a continuity hire, not a reset.** Aaron Kromer retired.
Buffalo replaced him with Pat Meyer, who coached under Kromer in Buffalo in 2015-2016,
comes from the Kromer coaching tree, and reportedly uses similar terminology
specifically to shorten the players' adjustment period. Meyer also worked with Joe
Brady on the Carolina staff in 2020-2021. Separately, Rob Boras — with Buffalo since
2017 — was promoted to a formalized **Run Game Coordinator** title alongside tight
ends. Running backs coach Kelly Skipper and assistant offensive line coach Austin Gund
were both retained. The run game arguably has *more* dedicated coaching authority in
2026 than it had in 2025.

**Where Meyer does change something, it is a philosophy of interior versatility.**
Kromer's line had a fixed left guard in David Edwards. Meyer is reported to want swing
linemen who move across the front, including tackles capable of kicking inside to
guard. That philosophy — not a simple best-player competition — is the most coherent
explanation for why Alec Anderson, a career sixth-man/swing lineman with only 542
career snaps, holds first-team left guard ahead of ninth-year veteran Austin Corbett.

**The scheme itself is unchanged.** Buffalo's core run menu under Brady is outside
zone and duo from heavy personnel, with inside zone as a changeup, including 13-personnel
inside-zone looks with tight ends capping both edges and 22-personnel outside-zone lead
concepts. Brady designed that menu and still calls it.

## Evidence

### Facts

- 2025 team rushing: 2,714 yards (1st), 159.6 per game (1st), 30 rushing touchdowns
  (1st), 5.0 yards per attempt. First NFL offense ever with 30-plus rushing and
  30-plus passing touchdowns in one regular season. Fourth in scoring at 28.3 points
  per game.
- 2025 line metrics: top-three in yards before contact per rush (3.1); 74.7 percent
  run-block win rate; 40 sacks allowed (tied 21st); 87.7 PFF pass-blocking efficiency
  (third).
- 2025 starting five and PFF grades: LT Dion Dawkins 72.4, LG David Edwards 66.1,
  C Connor McGovern 69.5, RG O'Cyrus Torrence 55.5, RT Spencer Brown 77.9.
- 2026 projected five per PFF: Dawkins, Anderson, McGovern, Torrence, Brown. PFF ranks
  the unit **sixth** entering 2026 (top five: Denver, Philadelphia, Tampa Bay,
  Indianapolis, Chicago).
- Personnel out: LG David Edwards to New Orleans on a reported four-year, $61 million
  deal; swing tackle Ryan Van Demark in free agency. Personnel in: G Austin Corbett and
  C Lloyd Cushenberry, both on one-year deals, both noted for positional flexibility.
- Coaching out: OL coach Aaron Kromer retired. In: Pat Meyer, most recently the
  Steelers' offensive line coach, previously a Bills assistant under Kromer in
  2015-2016, and on Brady's Carolina staff in 2020-2021.
- Rob Boras promoted to Run Game Coordinator / Tight Ends for 2026; he joined Buffalo
  in 2017. Kelly Skipper (RB) and Austin Gund (assistant OL) retained.
- PFF describes Dawkins and Brown as one of the league's best tackle duos, with Brown
  an elite run blocker at his position and Dawkins holding a pass-blocking grade above
  75.0 in all nine of his seasons.
- Health: C Connor McGovern out since 2026-08-08 (reported hamstring, two to four
  weeks, targeting Week 1). RT Spencer Brown disclosed offseason knee surgery that cost
  him the offseason program but reported ready for camp. LT Dawkins took veteran rest
  days during camp.
- Depth behind the starters is unsettled: with Van Demark gone, Tylan Grable and
  fourth-round rookie Jude Bowry are the swing-tackle candidates; both are described as
  having tackle/guard flexibility, which fits Meyer's stated preference.

### Inferences

- The scheme's dependence on outside zone and duo means **tackle play matters more to
  this run game than guard play**, and the tackles are the unit's documented strength.
  That materially softens the left-guard downgrade for rushing purposes, even though it
  does not eliminate it.
- Anderson leading Corbett is better read as a scheme-fit decision under a new position
  coach than as a pure talent verdict. It is a bet on versatility with 542 career snaps
  of evidence behind it — a genuine risk, but a coherent one.
- Losing Edwards (66.1) hurts less than his contract implies, but the interior is now
  the soft spot of the unit: the extended right guard posted the line's lowest 2025
  grade (55.5) and the left guard is a near-rookie in snap terms.
- The greater risk to the 2026 run game is not the guard change but **simultaneity**:
  a new position coach installing a versatility philosophy, a rehabbing center, an
  unresolved left guard, and a right tackle coming off knee surgery, all landing at the
  same time as the league's hardest early running-back schedule.
- Regression from a first-place, record-setting rushing season should be the base case
  regardless of personnel. A unit ranked sixth is very good; the 2025 output was
  historic.

## Fantasy implication

- The blocking environment for James Cook is a mild downgrade from 2025, not a
  structural break. Scheme, play-caller, run-game coordinator, position coaches, and
  both tackles carry over.
- Expect the interior to be the visible weak point early, especially in short yardage
  and duo concepts that ask guards to displace. Outside zone — Cook's best fit — leans
  on the part of the line Buffalo protected.
- The specific early-season risk window is Weeks 1-5, where a reshuffled interior meets
  the league's hardest RB schedule. Judge Cook on snaps and touches through that
  window; efficiency is the noisiest signal available.
- Treat any report that McGovern misses regular-season time as the single most
  actionable blocking-related update, because it would stack a new center on top of a
  new left guard.
- For team-level exposure, Buffalo remains a top-tier rushing environment. A drop from
  first to, say, top-eight in rushing efficiency is a normal outcome and does not by
  itself invalidate Cook's role-based value.

## Assessment

- Confidence: medium
- Reason: the 2025 measured performance, the coaching moves, the personnel moves, and
  the published depth ordering are all documented. The size of the run-blocking effect
  is inference, and no 2026 regular-season snap has been played.
- Fact/inference boundary: team rushing statistics, PFF grades and rankings, the
  Kromer retirement and Meyer hire, the Boras promotion, the Edwards and Van Demark
  departures, and the injury reports are facts. Meyer's versatility philosophy is
  reported rather than measured. The claim that tackle-dependent scheme softens the
  guard downgrade is inference.
- What would invalidate this: a left guard job still rotating in September; McGovern
  missing regular-season games; a visible shift away from outside zone and duo under
  Meyer; or early-season yards-before-contact falling well below the 2025 mark of 3.1
  while the tackles are healthy.
- Next review: preseason game two (2026-08-22 at Cleveland) for the interior
  configuration, then Weeks 1-4 with yards-before-contact and run-block win rate
  charting against the 2025 baselines recorded above.
