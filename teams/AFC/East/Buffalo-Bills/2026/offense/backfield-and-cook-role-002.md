# Finding: Cook's lead role is confirmed and the receiving lever is now stated intent, but the run game blocks behind an unsettled interior line

## Scope

- Unit: running backs, with interior offensive line as the dependent variable
- Area: workload, passing-down usage, goal-line share, and blocking context
- Season: 2026
- Status: active; supersedes the 2026-08-13 pre-preseason baseline
- Last verified: 2026-08-20 (one preseason game played; second is 2026-08-22 at Cleveland)

## Finding

James Cook enters 2026 as the reigning NFL rushing champion and an unambiguous
workhorse. Three things changed since the 2026-08-13 baseline, and they cut in
opposite directions.

**Resolved in Cook's favor.** Joe Brady is confirmed as the play-caller as head
coach, preserving the exact relationship that produced Cook's 2025. New offensive
coordinator Pete Carmichael — the open variable in the prior record — has stated an
intent to use Cook *more* in the passing game, not less. Carmichael's own open
question this preseason is only whether he works from the booth or the sideline; he
is preparing as though he could call plays, but Brady calls them.

**Unresolved, and now the primary risk.** The interior offensive line in front of
Cook is not the unit the prior record described. Pro Bowl center Connor McGovern
suffered a lower-body injury (reported as a hamstring strain) on 2026-08-08 and has
been sidelined since, missing the preseason opener; the reported timeline is two to
four weeks with McGovern targeting Week 1. Lloyd Cushenberry started at center in his
place. Separately, the left-guard competition is not the Corbett-versus-Cushenberry
battle recorded on 2026-08-13: Cushenberry is a center, and the left-guard job is
between incumbent Alec Anderson — who holds first-team status — and free-agent
addition Austin Corbett, who is second on the preseason depth chart.

**Unchanged.** Josh Allen still absorbs short-yardage and goal-line carries, and the
backfield behind Cook (Ray Davis, Ty Johnson, Frank Gore Jr.) carries defined,
non-conflicting roles rather than committee threat.

## Evidence

### Facts

- Cook's 2025: 309 carries, 1,621 rushing yards (led the NFL), 12 rushing touchdowns,
  95.4 rushing yards per game (led the NFL), 33 receptions, 291 receiving yards, 2
  receiving touchdowns, 14 total touchdowns, 17 games. Nine 100-yard rushing games in
  the regular season, ten including the playoffs; a career-high 216-yard, 2-touchdown
  game against Carolina in Week 8. Second-Team All-Pro; No. 20 on the NFL Top 100 of
  2026.
- Cook's receiving volume *declined* year over year: 33 receptions in 2025 against 44
  receptions for 445 yards in 2024. His 2025 target share was reported at 8.4% in an
  offense that ranked 25th in passing attempts (29.1 per game).
- Preseason opener, 2026-08-15 vs. Carolina (29-14 Buffalo): offensive starters played
  two full possessions totaling 15 snaps, both ending in points. Cook carried five
  times for 21 yards and left healthy. Josh Allen went 6-of-8 for 111 yards and a
  touchdown; DJ Moore had three catches for 61 yards.
- First-team offensive line in that game included Cushenberry at center, O'Cyrus
  Torrence at right guard, and Spencer Brown at right tackle. McGovern did not play.
- Brady confirmed from his first day as head coach that he is the offensive
  play-caller. Carmichael is testing the booth in one preseason game and the sideline
  in another to determine his game-day location.
- Carmichael has stated the team intends to use Cook more extensively in the passing
  game, with route concepts described as including angle routes and motion-adjusted
  deeper patterns. Cook's career receiving line is 130 receptions on 164 targets.
- Cook signed a contract extension ahead of the 2025 season. The 2025 hold-in is
  resolved; he is now widely described as underpaid relative to the running-back
  market. No 2026 contract dispute is reported.
- Camp injuries touching the run game: C Connor McGovern (lower body, since 2026-08-08)
  and RB Ty Johnson (lower body, reported 2026-08-13).
- No practice absence, injury, or role change for Cook is reported between 2026-08-15
  and 2026-08-20.

### Inferences

- The Carmichael receiving expansion is *stated intent*, not charted usage. It is the
  single largest swing factor in full-PPR scoring and it starts from an unusually low
  base (33 receptions), so it should be treated as upside to verify, not as a
  projection to bank.
- A replacement center plus an unsettled left guard is a genuine early-season run-game
  risk, and it compounds with Buffalo's schedule: this repository's own strength-of-
  schedule pass ranks Buffalo's first-five-game RB slate the hardest in the league
  (average opponent rank 26.4 across HOU, DET, LAC, NE, LAR) and its full-season RB
  schedule third-toughest.
- If McGovern returns for Week 1 as targeted, the risk is largely a preseason
  continuity cost rather than a regular-season one. If he slips, Cook runs behind two
  new interior starters into the league's hardest opening RB schedule.
- The absence of any reported RB transaction, draft pick, or free-agent addition means
  the backfield is a returning room. Committee risk remains low.

## Fantasy implication

- Cook is a workload-secure, role-secure early-round back whose 2025 floor was
  established behind a *healthier* interior line than the one he currently has.
- In full PPR his 33-reception 2025 is the weakest part of his profile and the reason
  his ceiling trails the tier's pass-catching backs. Carmichael's stated plan is the
  specific, named catalyst that would fix it — track receptions and route
  participation in the first four regular-season games.
- For the Gridiron Guru's Only 2026 format specifically: the pending 100-yard rushing
  game bonus is disproportionately valuable to Cook, who posted nine such games in
  2025. The 100-199 receiving-yard bonus is worth zero to him — his career single-game
  receiving high is 83 yards.
- Early-season production should be judged on snaps, touches, and route share rather
  than yardage, given the opening schedule. A poor Weeks 1-3 stat line behind a hard
  slate is a buy signal if the workload holds, not a sell signal.

## Assessment

- Confidence: high on role, workload, and the facts of the coaching arrangement;
  medium on the interior-line resolution; low on the size of any receiving expansion.
- Reason: the lead role, play-calling arrangement, and 2025 production are documented;
  the offensive-line configuration is actively changing and the receiving plan is an
  unquantified statement of intent.
- Fact/inference boundary: 2025 statistics, the preseason-opener usage, the McGovern
  injury, the left-guard depth ordering, and Carmichael's stated plan are facts. The
  magnitude of any receiving increase and the run-game cost of the line churn are
  inference.
- What would invalidate this: McGovern missing regular-season time, a left-guard job
  that keeps rotating into September, an unreported Cook injury, or a Weeks 1-4 route
  share that stays at or below the 2025 rate despite the stated plan.
- Next review: preseason game two (2026-08-22 at Cleveland) for interior-line
  configuration; Weeks 1-4 with snap, route, and target charting for the receiving
  question.
