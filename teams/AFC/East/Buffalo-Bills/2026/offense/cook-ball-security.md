# Finding: the direct scoring cost of Cook's fumbles is trivial; the coaching response is the real risk

## Scope

- Player: James Cook III
- Area: ball security, its trend, and the mechanism by which it could reduce his role
- Season: 2026
- Status: active
- Last verified: 2026-08-20

## Finding

Cook's 2025 fumble total was the worst in the NFL — six regular-season fumbles,
reportedly leading the league by two, with three lost, plus a further fumble in the
divisional-round loss to Denver. He led all running backs in both fumbles and fumbles
lost. PFF graded him 81.1 (10th among running backs) and, per that analysis, he would
have graded 90.1 and ranked second without the fumbles.

**This is a pattern, not a one-year spike.** Ball security has been attached to Cook
since he entered the league in 2022, and it was being written about during Buffalo's
2024 training camp. His per-touch fumble rate by season: 0.91 percent (2022), 1.42
(2023), 0.42 (2024), 1.75 (2025), against a career mark of 1.23 percent across 972
touches. The two worst rates came in his two heaviest-workload seasons.

**The direct fantasy cost is close to nothing.** Three fumbles lost at -2 points each
is -6 points against roughly 311 in this league's format — about 1.9 percent of his
season. Anyone modeling fumbles as a scoring drag is solving the wrong problem.

**The mechanism that matters is a coaching decision.** A back who leads the league in
fumbles is a back a coaching staff can decide to protect in specific situations —
two-minute, four-minute, or goal-line — and those are the highest-leverage snaps he has.
That is the single most plausible route by which Cook's role contracts in 2026, and it
is the risk flagged in his canonical profile.

The mitigating evidence is real. Cook publicly owned the problem and spent the 2026
offseason on it; head coach Joe Brady praised his commitment and work ethic publicly. No
2026 training-camp ball-security incident has been reported, and he handled his
preseason-opener work without issue.

## Evidence

### Facts

- 2025 regular season: six fumbles, three lost. Reported as the league high by two, and
  the most among running backs in both categories. A seventh fumble came in the
  divisional-round game against Denver.
- PFF season grade 81.1, 10th among running backs; the same analysis states the grade
  would have been 90.1 and second-ranked without the fumbles.
- Fumbles by season (regular season) against touches:

| Season | Rush att | Rec | Touches | Fumbles | Rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2022 | 89 | 21 | 110 | 1 | 0.91% |
| 2023 | 237 | 44 | 281 | 4 | 1.42% |
| 2024 | 207 | 32 | 239 | 1 | 0.42% |
| 2025 | 309 | 33 | 342 | 6 | 1.75% |
| Career | 842 | 130 | 972 | 12 | 1.23% |

- Ball security was documented as a training-camp concern in August 2024 and is
  described in coverage as an issue that has followed Cook since 2022.
- New England was reported to be preparing to attack the weakness ahead of their Week 15
  2025 meeting.
- 2026 offseason: Cook said his priority is to "protect the ball, most importantly,"
  acknowledged he did not do a good enough job in 2025, and said he had the full
  offseason to work on it. He skipped voluntary OTAs but trained at his South Florida
  home; Brady acknowledged his commitment in a press conference (reported 2026-06-18).
- No 2026 training-camp fumble incident has been reported as of 2026-08-20. Cook carried
  five times in the 2026-08-15 preseason opener without incident.

### Inferences

- The 2024 rate (0.42 percent) shows he is capable of a clean season, which argues
  against treating 1.75 percent as his true talent level. A regression toward his 1.23
  percent career rate is the reasonable 2026 expectation — roughly four fumbles on a
  similar workload.
- The absence of a 2026 camp report is weak positive evidence, not proof. It is also
  the kind of story that surfaces late in camp, so it remains worth monitoring through
  the second and third preseason games.
- Because the direct point cost is negligible, any bearish case built on fumbles must
  route through a role change. That makes the observable to watch a *usage* signal —
  two-minute and goal-line participation — rather than a fumble count.

## Fantasy implication

- Do not discount Cook's projection for fumbles directly; the arithmetic does not
  support it (-6 points in 2025).
- **Do** treat a documented in-season benching, or a visible loss of two-minute or
  goal-line work following a fumble, as a genuine downgrade signal. That is the specific
  event to watch for, and it would have knock-on effects on the red-zone distribution
  recorded separately.
- In this league's format, fumbles lost cost 2 points each, so the practical effect on
  a weekly score is roughly one bad reception's worth of variance.
- The upside framing is worth holding too: PFF's counterfactual puts a clean Cook near
  the top of the position. Ball security is the gap between a very good back and an
  elite one, which is precisely why the coaching staff is invested in it.

## Assessment

- Confidence: medium
- Reason: the fumble counts, PFF grades, and Cook's public comments are documented. The
  claim that a coaching response is the real risk is inference, unsupported by any
  actual 2025 benching that this research surfaced.
- Fact/inference boundary: the season fumble totals, per-touch rates, PFF grades, the
  2024 camp reporting, and the 2026 offseason quotes are facts. The projected regression
  toward the career rate and the role-contraction mechanism are inference.
- Source-dating note: one widely surfaced article about Cook's camp ball-security
  problems dates to **2024-08-07** and describes the 2024 camp, not 2026. It is cited
  here only as evidence that the pattern predates 2025 and must not be read as current
  reporting.
- What would invalidate this: a multi-fumble stretch early in 2026 accompanied by a
  visible usage change; or conversely a clean first month, which would push this record
  toward closure.
- Next review: preseason games two and three, then a check after four regular-season
  games on both fumble count and two-minute/goal-line participation.
