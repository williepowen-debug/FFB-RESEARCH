# Finding: Buffalo has become a run-first offense by degrees, and the 2026 levers on Josh Allen offset each other

## Scope

- Unit: offense, team-level pass/run identity
- Area: passing volume, its four-year trend, and the distribution consequences for Allen and the receivers
- Season: 2026
- Status: active
- Last verified: 2026-08-20

## Finding

Buffalo's transformation into a run-first offense is not a 2025 event. It is a
four-year, monotonic decline in passing volume that has been partially masked by
rising quarterback rushing production.

Josh Allen's pass attempts by season: **646 (2021), 567 (2022), 579 (2023), 483
(2024), 460 (2025)** — a 186-attempt, 28.8 percent reduction. Passing touchdowns fell
in the same direction every year: 36, 35, 29, 28, 25. In 2025 he set six-year lows in
passing yards per game (215.8) and passing touchdowns while posting a career-high
69.3 percent completion rate. The offense became more efficient and much smaller.

What replaced it is rushing — Allen's own and the backfield's. Allen has now recorded
double-digit rushing touchdowns in three consecutive seasons (15, 12, 14), and Buffalo
in 2025 led the NFL in rushing yards, rushing yards per game, and rushing touchdowns
while ranking 25th in pass attempts at 29.1 per game.

**The 2026 question is whether this reverts, and the honest answer is that the two
levers cancel.** The bullish case is real: DJ Moore was acquired in March to address a
receiver room that has not produced a 900-yard receiver since Stefon Diggs left after
2023 (Khalil Shakir led 2025 with 719 yards), Pete Carmichael arrives from a passing
tradition, and public projections have Allen back up near 509 attempts. But the
bearish case is that the man who designed and called this run-first offense, Joe Brady,
is now the head coach and still calls the plays. Two consecutive years of this identity
under the same designer is a system, not a slump.

## Evidence

### Facts

| Season | Att | Cmp | Cmp% | Pass yds | Pass TD | INT | Rush att | Rush yds | Rush TD |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2021 | 646 | 409 | 63.3 | 4,407 | 36 | 15 | 122 | 763 | 6 |
| 2022 | 567 | 359 | 63.3 | 4,283 | 35 | 14 | 124 | 762 | 7 |
| 2023 | 579 | 385 | 66.5 | 4,306 | 29 | 18 | 111 | 524 | 15 |
| 2024 | 483 | 307 | 63.6 | 3,731 | 28 | 6 | 102 | 531 | 12 |
| 2025 | 460 | 319 | 69.3 | 3,668 | 25 | 10 | 112 | 579 | 14 |

- 2025 team context: first in the NFL in rushing yards (2,714), rushing yards per game
  (159.6), and rushing touchdowns (30); 25th in pass attempts (29.1 per game); fourth
  in scoring (28.3 points per game).
- Allen was still the overall QB1 in fantasy in 2025 — the fourth time in six seasons —
  at 22.0 points per game in conventional scoring, a six-year low.
- Allen is the consensus 2026 QB1, the top choice of seven of eight ESPN panelists.
- He holds the NFL record for career rushing touchdowns by a quarterback (79) and
  shares the single-season record (15, 2023). 2024 NFL MVP; four Pro Bowls.
- Receiver context: no Buffalo receiver has reached 900 yards since Diggs departed
  after 2023. Shakir led 2025 with 719 yards. DJ Moore was acquired from Chicago in
  March 2026.
- ESPN projects 509 pass attempts and 3,945 passing yards for Allen in 2026; another
  public forecast has 3,835 yards, 27 passing touchdowns, 563 rushing yards, and 11
  rushing touchdowns.

### Inferences

- The decline is structural, not injury- or personnel-driven noise: it is monotonic
  across four seasons and survives a coordinator change, a head-coach change, and
  wholesale receiver turnover.
- Efficiency rising while volume falls (career-high completion rate at the lowest
  attempt total) is the signature of an offense choosing fewer, better passing
  situations — consistent with leading the league in rushing.
- Because Brady designed this offense and retains play-calling as head coach, the
  prior should be continuity. Carmichael's arrival and the Moore trade are reasons to
  expect modest reversion, not a return to the 2021-2022 shape.
- A meaningful passing-volume recovery and continued 14-touchdown quarterback rushing
  are, to some degree, mutually exclusive: both compete for the same red-zone snaps.

## Fantasy implication

**For scoring formats that pay per completion and per passing yard**, this trend is the
single most important fact about Buffalo. Scored in the Gridiron Guru's Only 2026
format (0.1 per completion, 0.04 per passing yard, 6-point passing touchdowns), Allen's
season totals have fallen every year for four years:

| Season | Pass pts | Rush pts | Total | Per game |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2021 | 403.2 | 112.3 | 515.5 | 30.3 |
| 2022 | 389.2 | 118.2 | 507.4 | 31.7 |
| 2023 | 348.7 | 142.4 | 491.1 | 28.9 |
| 2024 | 335.9 | 125.1 | 461.0 | 27.1 |
| 2025 | 308.6 | 141.9 | 450.5 | 26.5 |

(Excludes big-play and yardage-game bonuses, which add to every row.)

- **The 2025 floor is high.** Allen's worst passing season in six years still produced
  roughly 26.5 points per game in this format. That is the downside case, not the
  projection.
- **But the upside is narrower than it looks.** Applying the public 2026 projection
  (509 attempts, 3,945 yards, 27 passing touchdowns, 11 rushing touchdowns) yields
  about 457 points, or 26.9 per game — essentially flat against 2025. Passing-volume
  reversion is worth approximately what rushing-touchdown regression costs. A realistic
  band is 26-28 points per game, and it takes *both* volume recovery *and* retained
  rushing scores to exceed it.
- **Allen's floor is built from the carries that cap James Cook's ceiling.** Rushing
  touchdowns supply 19 to 31 percent of Allen's format value depending on the season.
  A manager cannot simultaneously hold a bullish Cook touchdown projection and a
  bullish Allen floor — they are claims on the same goal-line snaps. See
  [red zone and touchdown share](red-zone-and-touchdown-share.md).
- **For the receivers, this is the structural drag.** A 25th-ranked pass-attempt volume
  is the reason no Buffalo receiver has cleared 900 yards in two seasons, and it caps
  DJ Moore's realistic ceiling regardless of his target share. Price Bills pass-catchers
  off roughly 460-510 team attempts, not off Moore's Chicago volume.

## Assessment

- Confidence: high on the trend and its magnitude; medium on the 2026 direction.
- Reason: five seasons of attempt, yardage, and touchdown data are unambiguous and
  independently verifiable. The 2026 projection depends on unresolved questions about
  Carmichael's influence and Moore's integration.
- Fact/inference boundary: the statistical table, the team rankings, the receiver
  context, and the public projections are facts. The claim that the decline is
  structural, and that the 2026 levers offset, is inference.
- What would invalidate this: Buffalo throwing above roughly 33 attempts per game
  across the first four weeks, which would break a four-year pattern and require
  repricing both Allen and the receivers upward; or a collapse below 25 attempts per
  game, which would push Allen's value almost entirely onto rushing.
- Next review: after four regular-season games, charting team pass attempts per game,
  Allen's completion total, and the rushing-touchdown split with Cook.
