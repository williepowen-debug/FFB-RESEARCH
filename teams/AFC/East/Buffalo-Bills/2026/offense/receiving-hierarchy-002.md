# Finding: the distributed-targets thesis is confirmed by 2025 data, and the binding constraint is team pass volume rather than the pecking order

## Scope

- Unit: wide receivers and tight ends
- Area: target distribution, hierarchy after the DJ Moore trade, and the volume ceiling
- Season: 2026
- Status: active; supersedes the 2026-08-13 pre-preseason baseline
- Last verified: 2026-08-20

## Finding

The prior record hypothesized that Buffalo distributes targets rather than producing a
receiver above a 25 percent share. **The 2025 data confirms it.** Khalil Shakir led the
team with 95 targets against roughly 460 team pass attempts — a share near 20.7 percent
— and no other pass-catcher cleared 60. That is a genuinely flat hierarchy.

The more important finding is that the hierarchy question has been the wrong question.
Buffalo ranked **25th in pass attempts** in 2025 at 29.1 per game, the fourth
consecutive year of declining passing volume. Shakir's team-leading line was 72
receptions for 719 yards — and no Buffalo receiver has reached 900 yards since Stefon
Diggs departed after 2023. **The ceiling on every Bills pass-catcher is set by the
denominator, not by their rank within it.** A 25 percent share of 460 attempts is 115
targets; the same share of a 2021-era 646 attempts would have been 162.

DJ Moore was acquired in March 2026 to address exactly this, and public projections have
Allen back near 509 attempts. But the offense's designer, Joe Brady, is now the head
coach and still calls plays, and this is the offense he built — one that led the NFL in
rushing while ranking 25th in passing volume.

## Evidence

### Facts

2025 Buffalo receiving, regular season:

| Player | Targets | Rec | Yards | TD |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Khalil Shakir | 95 | 72 | 719 | 4 |
| Keon Coleman | 59 | — | 404 | — |
| Dalton Kincaid | 49 | — | 571 | — |
| James Cook | ~40 | 33 | 291 | 2 |

- Buffalo attempted 460 passes in 2025 (29.1 per game, 25th in the NFL) while leading
  the league in rushing yards, rushing yards per game, and rushing touchdowns.
- Shakir's 95 targets represent roughly a 20.7 percent share — below the 25 percent
  threshold the prior record set as its disconfirming condition.
- No Buffalo receiver has posted a 900-yard season since Diggs left after 2023.
- DJ Moore was acquired from Chicago in March 2026, reuniting with Brady from Carolina,
  and is the projected perimeter WR1.
- Dawson Knox was re-signed through 2028 as the blocking and red-zone tight end
  alongside Kincaid.
- Preseason opener (2026-08-15): Moore three catches for 61 yards, Shakir two for 41,
  Keon Coleman a nine-yard touchdown, across two starter possessions.
- ESPN projects 509 pass attempts and 3,945 passing yards for Allen in 2026.

### Inferences

- Moore should lead the team in targets, but "leads a distributed offense with 460-510
  attempts" is a materially smaller outcome than his Chicago usage implies. Price him
  off Buffalo's denominator.
- Shakir's 2025 line is the realistic template for Buffalo's target leader under current
  volume: roughly 90-100 targets, 700-800 yards. That is a WR3/flex outcome in PPR, not
  a WR1 one, and it was the *best* case on the roster.
- Kincaid's 571 yards on only 49 targets is the most efficient line in the group and the
  clearest candidate for positive regression if volume rises — though tight-end target
  competition from Knox caps it.
- Coleman at 59 targets for 404 yards is the weakest efficiency in the group and the
  most exposed to Moore's arrival.
- The distributed pattern plus low volume means Buffalo is a poor source of weekly WR1
  outcomes but a reasonable source of touchdown-dependent flex production.

## Fantasy implication

- **Downgrade all Bills pass-catchers relative to their raw talent.** The constraint is
  structural and four years old. See
  [passing-volume decline](passing-volume-decline.md).
- DJ Moore is the correct first Bills receiver off the board, but treat WR2/WR3 pricing
  as fair rather than a discount. His ceiling requires Buffalo's pass volume to rise,
  which is the same unresolved bet as Allen's ceiling.
- Shakir is the floor play: a proven 95-target role in a low-volume offense.
- Kincaid is the leverage play in PPR and tight-end-premium formats — the efficiency is
  there, the volume is not, and he is the cheapest way to buy the volume-reversion
  thesis.
- Coleman is the most avoidable of the group given Moore's arrival and his 2025
  efficiency.
- In this league's full-PPR scoring with a 100-199 receiving-yard game bonus, only Moore
  and Shakir have a realistic path to collecting that bonus with any frequency, and
  neither often.

## Assessment

- Confidence: medium
- Reason: the 2025 target and yardage data are documented and the volume trend is
  strongly established, but no 2026 regular-season target distribution exists and
  Carmichael's practical influence is unmeasured.
- Fact/inference boundary: the 2025 receiving lines, team pass attempts and rank, the
  Moore trade, the Knox extension, and the preseason-opener lines are facts. The
  projected hierarchy and per-player advice are inference.
- Data note: an aggregator earlier attributed a "72 receptions, 719 yards, 4 touchdowns"
  line to James Cook. That is **Khalil Shakir's** 2025 line. The conflict flagged in the
  [Cook profile](../../../../../../players/james-cook/profile.md) is therefore resolved:
  Cook's correct 2025 receiving line is 33 receptions for 291 yards and 2 touchdowns.
- What would invalidate this: Buffalo throwing above roughly 33 attempts per game in the
  first month, or any receiver exceeding a 25 percent target share across four
  representative games.
- Next review: after four regular-season games, with target share and team pass attempts
  per game charted.
