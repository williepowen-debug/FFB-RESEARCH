# Finding: Allen still caps the backfield's scoring, but the cap loosened measurably in 2025

## Scope

- Unit: offense, red-zone and goal-line personnel
- Area: touchdown distribution between Josh Allen and the running backs
- Season: 2026, projected from 2025 distribution
- Status: active
- Last verified: 2026-08-20

## Finding

The standing view in this repository — that Josh Allen's short-yardage and goal-line
rushing caps the backfield's touchdown ceiling — remains directionally correct but is
now too strong as stated. Allen rushed for 14 touchdowns on 112 carries in 2025 and is
still the offense's primary goal-line runner. But James Cook took 24 goal-line carries
(eighth in the NFL) and his red-zone touch share is reported near 40 percent, both
materially above his earlier seasons.

The more accurate framing is that Buffalo runs a genuinely *shared* red zone in which
Allen holds the highest-leverage snaps but the lead back is no longer a bystander.
Cook has now scored 12 and 16 rushing touchdowns in the last two seasons — he is not a
touchdown-starved back, and treating him as one misprices him.

The countervailing fact is that Cook's touchdown output does not track his workload.
He scored 2 rushing touchdowns on 237 carries in 2023 and 16 on 207 carries in 2024.
Volume does not predict scoring in this offense; goal-line deployment does, and that
deployment is a coaching choice made fresh each season.

## Evidence

### Facts

- Josh Allen 2025: 112 carries, 579 rushing yards, 14 rushing touchdowns; 319-of-460
  passing for 3,668 yards, 25 passing touchdowns, 10 interceptions.
- James Cook 2025: 24 goal-line carries, eighth-most in the NFL; reported red-zone
  touch share near 40 percent; 12 rushing touchdowns and 2 receiving touchdowns.
- Cook's rushing touchdowns by season: 2 (2022), 2 (2023), 16 (2024), 12 (2025), on
  carry totals of 89, 237, 207, and 309 respectively.
- Buffalo's 2025 offense ranked fourth in total offense, fourth in points per game,
  and third in red-zone efficiency, and led the NFL in rushing attempts and rushing
  production.
- Joe Brady, who designed the 2025 red-zone menu as coordinator, is the 2026 head
  coach and confirmed play-caller. Pete Carmichael is the new offensive coordinator.
- Buffalo re-signed blocking and red-zone tight end Dawson Knox through 2028 and added
  fullback usage in early preseason run formations.

### Inferences

- The 2024-to-2025 shift (16 to 12 rushing touchdowns for Cook, alongside a rise in
  goal-line carries) suggests Buffalo is distributing goal-line work more evenly
  rather than moving decisively toward either player.
- Because the play-caller is unchanged, the 2025 distribution is the best available
  prior for 2026 — a better prior than the 2024 split, which came under the same
  coordinator but a different head coach and roster.
- A materially higher Cook touchdown projection requires evidence of Allen's goal-line
  carries declining, which no current reporting supports. A materially lower one
  requires the 2023 pattern to return, which the 2025 goal-line carry count argues
  against.
- Touchdown variance, not touchdown suppression, is the correct risk to price for
  Cook.

## Fantasy implication

- Price Cook's touchdown expectation off the two-year 12-16 range rather than off the
  2023 two-touchdown season, but treat the variance band as wide.
- In formats with 6-point rushing touchdowns and big-play touchdown bonuses, this
  distribution question is worth more to Cook's valuation than his yardage projection.
- Allen's 14 rushing touchdowns are a large share of his own fantasy value; any
  reporting that Buffalo intends to protect him with fewer designed goal-line runs is
  simultaneously bullish for Cook and bearish for Allen. Track it as a single linked
  question, not two independent ones.
- The red-zone share is the leading indicator to chart in Weeks 1-4 — it moves before
  touchdown totals do.

## Assessment

- Confidence: medium
- Reason: the underlying counts (Allen's rushing touchdowns, Cook's goal-line carries)
  are documented, but the red-zone touch share is a single reported figure and no 2026
  usage exists yet.
- Fact/inference boundary: the 2025 statistics, rankings, and coaching arrangement are
  facts. The 2026 distribution and the claim that the cap has "loosened" are inference
  from a one-season shift.
- What would invalidate this: a 2026 red-zone split that reverts to the 2023 pattern,
  a stated plan to reduce Allen's designed runs, or a goal-line package built around
  a different back or a tight end.
- Next review: after four regular-season games, with red-zone and goal-line carry
  charting for both Allen and Cook.
