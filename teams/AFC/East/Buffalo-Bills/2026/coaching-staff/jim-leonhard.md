# Jim Leonhard — Buffalo Bills

- Role: Defensive coordinator
- Joined team: 2026 (from Denver)
- Last verified: 2026-08-13

## Responsibilities

Leonhard is Buffalo's new defensive coordinator, arriving from Denver alongside offensive coordinator Pete Carmichael as part of Joe Brady's first staff. He inherits a unit that kept its front-seven anchors (Ed Oliver, Greg Rousseau, Terrel Bernard, Matt Milano) but substantially reworked its secondary and added edge rusher Bradley Chubb. Buffalo also replaced 2025 defensive coordinator Bobby Babich and brought in a nearly all-new defensive position-coach staff.

## Scheme and tendencies

Leonhard's history — as a long-time NFL safety and as Wisconsin's defensive coordinator — is associated with disguise, split-safety and zone structures, and simulated/creative pressure rather than one fixed front. Buffalo's exact 2026 scheme should be described as to-be-charted: the reasonable prior is a coverage-and-disguise emphasis with situational pressure, but no fixed front or blitz rate should be assigned before preseason and early-season evidence. Treat any "aggressive" or "multiple" label as a hypothesis.

## Personnel usage

The rebuilt secondary is the story. Taron Johnson was traded and Taylor Rapp released; Buffalo signed safeties C.J. Gardner-Johnson and Geno Stone and corner Dee Alford, re-signed Damar Hamlin, and drafted three defensive backs (Igbinosun, Kilgore, Pride). The nickel and second-safety roles are open competitions. On the edge, Chubb's snap share and health opposite Rousseau, and rookie TJ Parker's rotational role, are the early indicators.

## Pace and situational decisions

No Buffalo game sample exists. Chart coverage shell rates (single-high vs. split), blitz and simulated-pressure frequency by down and distance, and how Leonhard deploys the new safeties in the box vs. deep. His handling of the nickel vacancy left by Taron Johnson is the most fantasy-relevant early decision for opposing slot receivers.

## Fantasy implications

- Opponent passing-game and IDP value will depend on actual coverage and pressure rates, not the coordinator's reputation.
- A rebuilt secondary carries early-season variance: matchup exploitability could be higher until the back end settles.
- Chubb's pressure production directly affects Buffalo's ability to generate takeaways and short fields, which feeds the D/ST projection.
