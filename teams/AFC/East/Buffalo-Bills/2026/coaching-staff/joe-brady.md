# Joe Brady — Buffalo Bills

- Role: Head coach; reported offensive play-caller
- Joined team: 2022 (quarterbacks coach); offensive coordinator 2023–2025; head coach 2026
- Last verified: 2026-08-13

## Responsibilities

Brady controls whole-team standards, staff management, game management, and — per reporting — continues to call the offensive plays he called as coordinator. He was promoted from offensive coordinator to head coach on 2026-01-27, signing a five-year deal, after Buffalo dismissed Sean McDermott following a 33-30 overtime wild-card loss to Denver. General manager Brandon Beane remains in place, so this is a coaching-succession change rather than a full front-office reset.

Brady's promotion is the opposite of a scheme reset: he was the incumbent play designer, so the offensive system carries forward. The new variable is his expanded head-coaching workload and how much of the offensive operation he delegates to new coordinator Pete Carmichael.

## Scheme and tendencies

The offense should be treated as broadly continuous with the 2024–2025 Brady offense: quarterback-driven, tempo-capable, and built to move Josh Allen inside and outside structure. Do not assume a new identity simply because the title changed.

The open questions are additive rather than foundational. Carmichael brings a long Saints coordinator history (he called New Orleans plays in 2022–2023) and joins from Denver. His influence on early-down structure, dropback passing detail, and situational calls is the thing to chart, not a wholesale philosophy change. Buffalo is publicly experimenting in the preseason with whether Carmichael operates from the booth or the sideline.

## Personnel usage

Brady's usage of James Cook — expanding Cook's role as both a runner and receiver — is the clearest established pattern to carry into projections. The pass-catching hierarchy changed materially with the DJ Moore trade, so target distribution among Moore, Khalil Shakir, Keon Coleman, and tight end Dalton Kincaid must be charted rather than assumed. Whether Brady's head-coaching duties dilute his week-to-week offensive attention is a genuine unknown for the first month.

## Pace and situational decisions

No head-coaching sample exists yet. Track fourth-down aggression, timeout and challenge management, end-of-half decisions, and whether calling plays while running the whole team affects tempo or late-game clock control. His decision to retain play-calling makes communication load a specific early-season watchpoint.

## Fantasy implications

- Josh Allen's environment is stable-to-improved: same play-caller, upgraded perimeter target (Moore). This is a continuity story, not a rebuild — the opposite of Miami's situation in the same division.
- James Cook's dual-role usage under Brady supports his floor; Carmichael's arrival is the variable that could reshape early-down and passing-down back usage.
- The receiver room gained a proven WR1 archetype in Moore but became more crowded; no single receiver's target share should be treated as settled.
- A first-time head coach who also calls plays introduces operational risk that does not show up in talent-based projections.

## Statements and reporting

Confirmed: Brady was promoted from OC to HC and signed a five-year deal; the staff added Carmichael (OC) and Leonhard (DC). Reported: Brady will call the offensive plays in 2026, with Carmichael important to the process. Analytical: "continuity offense under a new head coach" is the working frame until game evidence shows Carmichael materially altering the system.

## Reliability cautions

Coordinator success does not guarantee head-coaching success, and a retained scheme can still change when the designer's attention is split across three phases. Public offseason statements are not observed in-season behavior. Establish Carmichael's real influence and Brady's game-management tendencies from preseason and early-season charting.
