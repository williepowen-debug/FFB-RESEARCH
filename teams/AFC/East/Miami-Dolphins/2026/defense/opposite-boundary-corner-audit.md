# Finding: Miami has boundary candidates, but no dependable CB2 baseline

## Scope

- Team: Miami Dolphins
- Primary candidates: JuJu Brents, Darrell Baker Jr., Alex Austin, Marco Wilson, Ethan Bonner, Jason Marshall Jr.
- Depth candidates: A.J. Green III, Storm Duck, Miles Battle, and other retained corners
- Area: availability, experience, alignment, coverage role, safety-help cost, and opponent targeting
- Status: active preseason audit
- Last verified: 2026-08-05

## Finding

Miami's opposite-boundary competition is not a choice among equivalent starting corners. Each leading candidate carries a different unresolved condition:

- JuJu Brents has length, physicality, second-round pedigree, and 11 NFL starts, but only 18 games in three seasons.
- Darrell Baker Jr. has the largest recent boundary workload and matchup experience, but he began 2026 camp on the physically unable to perform list.
- Alex Austin has supplied competent injury-replacement starts and ball disruption in a limited sample, but has never held a full-time role.
- Marco Wilson has by far the most starting experience and elite athletic testing, but recent teams did not retain him as a starter.
- Ethan Bonner has continuity and traits but only nine tackles and three passes defensed across 21 games.
- Jason Marshall Jr. has developmental upside and some 2025 production, but his strongest current competition may be at nickel rather than boundary.

Brents is a reasonable roster-based projection, not a settled starter. Baker may have the best evidence for handling a recurring outside assignment if healthy. Austin may offer the safest spot-start alternative. None has established the combination of availability, coverage stability, and role continuity required to protect Miami from a target funnel opposite Chris Johnson.

### JuJu Brents

Indianapolis drafted Brents 44th overall in 2023. At 6-foot-3 and 198 pounds, he supplies the size Miami otherwise lacks among its leading veteran corners.

His rookie season produced 43 tackles, six passes defensed, one interception, one forced fumble, and one recovery across nine games and eight starts. The Colts also documented repeated interruption:

- pre-draft wrist recovery;
- a training-camp hamstring injury;
- six missed games after a Week 7 quad injury; and
- another hamstring injury in Week 18.

Brents tore his meniscus and MCL in the 2024 opener and missed most of the season. A 2025 training-camp hamstring problem preceded Indianapolis waiving him. Miami claimed him, used him in seven games with two starts, and placed him on injured reserve in November.

Brents has demonstrated physical outside-corner play in fragments. He has not demonstrated season-long availability or enough recent coverage volume to make his starting projection secure.

### Darrell Baker Jr.

Miami's official profile credits Baker with 34 games, 19 starts, 1,349 cornerback snaps, 12 passes defensed, and 22 successful-play tackles over two Tennessee seasons. It says he matched top opposing receivers.

That is the most relevant recent workload in the room. Miami also describes:

- press experience;
- zone vision and downhill tackling;
- 4.43 speed;
- a 41.5-inch vertical; and
- an 11-foot-4 broad jump.

Those are team-presented traits, not an independent coverage grade. Baker's placement on PUP prevents the workload from becoming an immediate camp advantage. His candidacy depends first on return timing, then on whether he receives first-unit outside snaps rather than merely rejoining the rotation.

### Alex Austin

Austin has started games for three different New England coaching staffs. In 2025, he started twice for Christian Gonzalez, then shifted toward special teams after Gonzalez returned.

His career includes:

- 29 games;
- six NFL starts by the Patriots' seasonal accounting;
- eight passes defensed;
- one interception; and
- a full-snap start against Kansas City as a rookie.

Austin also spent time on injured reserve in both 2024 and 2025. His profile supports functional replacement value and roster versatility. It does not establish that he can hold a boundary job after opponents accumulate film and target him weekly.

### Marco Wilson

Wilson offers a different wager. Miami credits him with 64 games and 37 starts, all three of his interceptions, 20 passes defensed, and exceptional testing:

- 4.34 forty;
- 43.5-inch vertical;
- 11-foot-4 broad jump;
- 26 bench repetitions;
- 4.09 shuttle; and
- 6.80 three-cone.

Most of the starting experience came in his first three Arizona seasons. His movement through New England and Cincinnati afterward is evidence that athletic ability did not lock in a starting role. Miami should test whether Hafley's zone spacing and technique coaching can convert those traits into more consistent play.

### Ethan Bonner and developmental alternatives

Miami tendered Bonner after three seasons in which he appeared in 21 games and recorded nine tackles, three passes defensed, and one interception. His 6-foot-1 frame and continuity merit competition, but his official production does not support assuming a major defensive role.

Marshall played 12 games with one start as a rookie, recording 23 tackles, four passes defensed, and one interception. His college record includes 27 passes defensed over four Florida seasons. He should be tested outside, but moving him there may reopen nickel and reduce continuity at two positions.

A.J. Green has 36 career games and two starts through the point Miami acquired him in November 2025. Storm Duck and Miles Battle retain developmental or special-teams paths. None should be projected over the leading group without repeated first-unit deployment.

## Functional requirements

Miami does not merely need the second-best practice corner. The winner must:

1. remain available;
2. align outside in base and nickel personnel;
3. survive vertical and intermediate isolation when safety rotation moves elsewhere;
4. tackle perimeter runs and screens;
5. execute zone exchanges and late rotations;
6. communicate through motion, stacks, and bunches;
7. avoid penalties in press or recovery situations; and
8. remain viable enough that quarterbacks cannot automatically target away from Johnson.

Special teams can decide the final roster spots, but it should not be mistaken for evidence of starting coverage quality.

## Coverage and protection consequences

### If Brents wins

Miami gains length and physical matchup potential. The unit accepts elevated availability risk and must identify a ready game-day replacement.

### If Baker wins

Miami gains the most recent sustained outside workload. The key questions become health, whether his Tennessee matchup role was effective rather than merely necessary, and whether he can execute Hafley's disguise rules.

### If Austin wins

Miami gains a defender with repeated spot-start experience and special-teams value. It would still need evidence that his performance holds over continuous starting exposure.

### If Wilson wins

Miami has likely converted rare athletic traits into a rebound. It must verify that the result reflects repeatable technique rather than isolated camp plays.

### If Johnson moves outside full time

That settles only one boundary. Miami still needs the opposite starter and a nickel. If Johnson instead moves inside on passing downs, two other corners must remain viable outside simultaneously.

## Opponent attack plan to anticipate

- formation strength away from Johnson;
- vertical isolation of the second corner;
- deep comebacks and outs that stress transition;
- back-shoulder targets against late-phase positioning;
- bunch and motion to force communication;
- screens and perimeter runs to test tackling;
- tempo to trap a replacement or unfavorable package;
- repeated targets after the first loss or penalty; and
- no-huddle attacks after Johnson moves inside and the second boundary pair changes.

The target funnel matters as much as raw completion percentage. A corner can avoid catastrophic efficiency while still concede valuable volume, first downs, and red-zone targets.

## Miami-facing test plan

### Camp and preseason

- Record first-unit outside corners on every base, nickel, and dime snap.
- Treat return from PUP and return to first-unit work as separate Baker milestones.
- Track Brents's consecutive practice and game availability.
- Chart press, off, bail, and zone technique.
- Record safety leverage and whether each corner is truly isolated.
- Tag targets by depth, route, separation, and result.
- Track motion, bunch, switch, and communication errors.
- Record penalties and near-penalties.
- Grade screen and perimeter-run tackling.
- Identify which corners remain outside when Johnson moves to nickel.

### Regular season

- Compare target share between Johnson and the opposite boundary.
- Track explosive targets, first downs, touchdowns, and penalties—not only passer rating.
- Split isolated coverage from snaps with top-down safety help.
- Measure performance before and after opponents begin deliberately targeting the winner.
- Reassess immediately after any starter absence because availability is part of the unit forecast.

## Fantasy implication

- Until the job stabilizes, opposing boundary receivers should retain target and explosive-play upside.
- If Johnson moves to nickel on passing downs, the two remaining outside corners may become the preferred fantasy matchup.
- A persistent target funnel can support an opposing WR even without terrible per-target efficiency.
- Safety help tilted toward the second corner can open seams, crossers, or the opposite half of the field.
- Brents or another physical corner could produce tackles in corner-required IDP formats, but unstable availability and rotation prevent a draftable baseline.
- Miami D/ST cannot be upgraded from Johnson alone; the offense can choose the weakest remaining matchup.

## Registered hypotheses

Five audit-specific hypotheses are registered in [`hypotheses.csv`](hypotheses.csv):

1. Brents's availability, rather than talent, is the main constraint on winning and holding the job.
2. Baker joins the first-unit boundary competition after leaving PUP.
3. Austin is the first spot-start replacement if the initial winner is unavailable.
4. Opponents create a material target funnel away from Johnson.
5. Miami supplies above-average safety help to the opposite boundary until that position stabilizes.

Existing hypothesis `mia-2026-def-q02` already tests whether Brents opens as the other starter. The new hypotheses test availability, succession, opponent behavior, and structural cost.
