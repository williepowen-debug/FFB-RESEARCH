# Linebacker roles

## Finding

Jordyn Brooks is Miami's every-down defensive foundation. The unresolved question is how Tyrel Dodson, second-round rookie Jacob Rodriguez, Willie Gay Jr., fourth-round rookie Kyle Louis, and hybrid Ronnie Harrison Jr. divide base, nickel, coverage, pressure, and special-teams roles.

Miami can use Brooks, Dodson, and Rodriguez together in four-linebacker-front presentations, but Duggan has described the combination as a competition rather than a settled lineup. Nickel deployment will determine which secondary linebacker has usable IDP volume.

## Evidence

| Player | Best current hypothesis | Confidence | Decisive evidence |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Jordyn Brooks | green-dot candidate, every-down linebacker, tackle leader | high | preseason communication and subpackage retention |
| Tyrel Dodson | incumbent starter with base and possible nickel work | medium | whether Rodriguez replaces him on passing downs |
| Jacob Rodriguez | immediate rotational role with every-down upside | low | first-unit nickel, third-down, and two-minute snaps |
| Willie Gay Jr. | veteran coverage and depth option | low | recurring defensive package versus special-teams concentration |
| Kyle Louis | developmental coverage/subpackage candidate | low | role beyond special teams |
| Ronnie Harrison Jr. | hybrid linebacker/coverage defender | low | matchups, alignment, and roster usage |

Brooks's extension is strong organizational evidence. It does not settle who plays beside him or whether Miami consistently uses two or three off-ball linebackers.

## Role tests

- Who wears the communication helmet and sets the front?
- Does Rodriguez play in nickel or only base?
- Which linebacker carries backs, tight ends, and vertical routes?
- Who blitzes, spies mobile quarterbacks, and replaces an edge in simulated pressure?
- Does Harrison function as a true linebacker, big nickel, or package defender?
- Does special-teams responsibility limit a rookie's defensive opportunity?

## Fantasy implication

- Brooks is the safest Miami IDP and should retain a strong tackle floor.
- Rodriguez has the largest contingent upside; draft capital alone does not establish an every-down role.
- Dodson becomes volatile if he leaves the field in nickel.
- A deep rotation may help Miami while eliminating a second dependable linebacker for fantasy.

## Assessment

- Confidence: high on Brooks; low-to-medium on every other linebacker role.
- Fact/inference boundary: contracts, roster status, and Duggan's competition statement are evidence. Package hierarchy is projected.
- What would invalidate this: Brooks leaves subpackages, Rodriguez immediately wins every-down work, or Miami primarily uses one off-ball linebacker.
- Next review: first-unit base, nickel, third-down, and two-minute preseason snaps.
