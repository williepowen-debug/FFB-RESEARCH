# Defensive fantasy impact

## Finding

Miami should enter fantasy preparation as a matchup-dependent streaming defense with a wide outcome range. Brooks, Robinson, and Sieler provide credible individual anchors. The unit-level ceiling depends on four-man pressure and secondary stabilization; the floor is threatened by coverage turnover and explosive plays.

## Evidence

The known front-seven anchors, documented personnel losses, unsettled secondary, and multiple scheme menu support scenario analysis. No preseason or regular-season rates yet support a confident unit ranking.

## Fantasy implication

### Miami D/ST

Upgrade when:

- the opponent has weak protection or a high pressure-to-sack quarterback;
- Miami's four-man rush is creating pressure;
- the starting secondary is stable;
- game script permits opponent dropback volume;
- Sieler, Brooks, and Robinson are healthy.

Downgrade when:

- the opponent can force Miami to defend the run from light boxes;
- the quarterback punishes blitzes or extends plays without taking sacks;
- corner or safety injuries create new communication pairings;
- Miami's sack production depends on sending five or more;
- the opponent can isolate backs or tight ends on unstable coverage roles.

### IDP hierarchy

| Tier | Player | Current fantasy case | Primary risk |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | Jordyn Brooks | every-down role and tackle volume | unexpected rotation or assignment change |
| 1 | Chop Robinson | lead-rusher volume and sack ceiling | protection attention and sack volatility |
| 2 | Zach Sieler | stable interior role | format dependence and rotation |
| 3 | Chris Johnson | likely high snaps and playmaking opportunity | alignment, target volume, rookie volatility |
| 3 | Jacob Rodriguez | every-down upside | base-only or rotational deployment |
| watch | Uche, Dodson, Grant, Trader, Anderson | package or role-based upside | insufficient snap concentration |

### Opponent positions

- **Quarterbacks:** ceiling remains available if the secondary allows explosives; downgrade only after Miami proves repeatable pressure without blitz dependence.
- **Running backs:** evaluate box count and interior health. Receiving backs may benefit if linebacker/safety coverage roles remain unsettled.
- **Wide receivers:** do not assign a blanket shutdown matchup before Johnson's role and the opposite corner stabilize.
- **Tight ends:** track Brooks, Rodriguez, safety, and big-nickel assignments before making matchup claims.

## Projection rules

1. Separate sacks from pressure quality and coverage result.
2. Separate takeaways from turnover-worthy opportunities.
3. Do not raise an IDP solely from a preseason splash play; require snap and assignment stability.
4. Apply injuries through the dependency chain rather than one-for-one replacement.
5. Reassess opponent positions weekly once coverage assignments become known.
