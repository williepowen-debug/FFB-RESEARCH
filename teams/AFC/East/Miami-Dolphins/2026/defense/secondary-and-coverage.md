# Secondary and coverage

## Finding

Miami's secondary is the defense's largest uncertainty. Chris Johnson projects as a full-time starter but is cross-training outside and at nickel. JuJu Brents is the roster-based opposite-boundary projection without strong camp confirmation. Jason Marshall Jr. was used inside as a rookie, but the current staff views him primarily as an outside corner. Dante Trader has the strongest returning safety claim, while Lonnie Johnson Jr., Zayne Anderson, Michael Taaffe, and others compete for the remaining roles.

Hafley and Duggan have confirmed both man and zone, press and off coverage, and personnel-driven deployment. No stable coverage rate or alignment hierarchy is established.

## Evidence

### Competition map

| Position | Current leaders | Central question |
|---|---|---|
| Outside corner | Chris Johnson; JuJu Brents | does Johnson remain outside, and who handles the other boundary? |
| Nickel | Chris Johnson; third-corner or big-nickel alternatives unresolved | can one defender handle every down, or does Miami split the role by package? |
| Safety | Dante Trader; Lonnie Johnson Jr.; Zayne Anderson | who plays deep, box, slot, and communication roles? |
| Developmental depth | Michael Taaffe; Ethan Robinson; Alex Austin; others | who earns defensive snaps versus special-teams-only work? |

Johnson has worked at outside corner and nickel. Zayne Anderson has two reported camp interceptions and active run-defense evidence. Lonnie Johnson was described as day-to-day. These facts sharpen the competition without resolving it.

The dedicated [`chris-johnson-role-audit.md`](chris-johnson-role-audit.md) finds that Johnson's strongest quantified college evidence came in zone coverage and documents Miami's proposed early-down outside and passing-down nickel split. It also isolates press, vertical, high-point, slot-option, and cross-training questions that require game evidence.

The [`opposite-boundary-corner-audit.md`](opposite-boundary-corner-audit.md) finds no dependable CB2 baseline: Brents carries the highest availability risk, Baker the largest recent workload but current PUP constraint, Austin the clearest spot-start case, and the remaining candidates narrower or less-established claims.

The [`nickel-competition-audit.md`](nickel-competition-audit.md) finds a package plan rather than a winner. Johnson has the strongest inside case, Marshall is currently projected outside by Duggan, and Hafley has allowed for separate early-down, big-nickel, and passing-down occupants.

### Coverage questions

- What is the single-high versus split-safety rate?
- Who plays the post, who rotates down, and who carries the communication burden?
- Does Miami use true man, match principles, spot zone, or a mixed family by situation?
- Can the corners press without safety protection?
- Does Johnson shadow, stay on one side, or move by package?
- Can the defense disguise late without creating assignment errors?
- Do takeaways reflect repeatable disruption or volatile finishing?

## Failure modes

1. Johnson's cross-training prevents mastery rather than increasing flexibility.
2. The opposite boundary corner requires persistent safety help.
3. Safety rotation or communication errors create explosives.
4. Pressure packages leave inexperienced defenders isolated.
5. Camp interceptions produce unjustified confidence in coverage quality.

## Fantasy implication

- Opposing wide receivers retain ceiling until boundary and nickel roles stabilize.
- Tight-end and running-back matchups depend on linebacker/safety coverage assignments.
- Johnson could earn useful tackle and playmaking volume, but cornerback-required IDP value depends on alignment and target frequency.
- Miami D/ST may combine turnovers with explosive plays allowed, producing volatility rather than a stable floor.
