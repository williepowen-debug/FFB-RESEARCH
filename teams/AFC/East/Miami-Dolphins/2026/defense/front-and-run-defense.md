# Defensive front and run defense

## Finding

Miami has enough interior talent to support Hafley and Duggan's multiple front menu, but the unit's structure depends on Kenneth Grant making a second-year step beside Zach Sieler. Sieler supplies the stable baseline. Grant, Jordan Phillips, Zeek Biggers, Matthew Butler, Alex Huntley, and Keith Cooper Jr. determine whether Miami can rotate without losing gap control.

The decisive question is whether the front can defend the run while remaining square and sound without routinely adding a safety. Success preserves coverage flexibility; failure exposes the secondary to play action and isolated coverage.

## Evidence

### Personnel baseline

| Player/group | Best current role hypothesis | Confidence | Required evidence |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Zach Sieler | primary interior anchor and penetrator | high | first-unit deployment and passing-down retention |
| Kenneth Grant | starting interior defender with nose and shaded-front value | medium | double-team response, anchor, and snap concentration |
| Jordan Phillips / Zeek Biggers | first-wave rotational size | low | position, down, and front usage |
| Matthew Butler / Alex Huntley / Keith Cooper Jr. | depth competition | low | preseason order and special-teams value |

Miami's official OTA reporting identified Grant, Phillips, and Biggers as second-year players expected to develop. That is organizational expectation, not proof of performance.

### Front questions

- How often does Miami use four-down versus three-down spacing?
- Who aligns over the center in odd fronts?
- Does Wide-9 spacing appear only on passing downs, or can the interior protect the widened edges against the run?
- Which linebacker fits first against zone, gap, counter, and quarterback-read concepts?
- Can the front force runs laterally without creating cutback lanes?
- Does Sieler need excessive snaps because the reserve floor is unstable?

## Failure modes

1. Grant does not anchor consistently, forcing Sieler into less favorable assignments.
2. Wide edge alignments widen natural run lanes without disciplined inside fits.
3. Rotation snaps create a material decline in gap control.
4. The defense adds a safety to stop the run and becomes vulnerable to explosives.
5. Mobile quarterbacks exploit rush-lane or read-option discipline.

## Fantasy implication

- Strong early-down run defense creates third-and-long pressure opportunities and raises Miami D/ST's sack ceiling.
- A leaky front increases opponent running-back volume, play-action efficiency, and red-zone trips.
- Sieler has the safest defensive-line floor; Grant requires snap and production evidence.
- Heavy rotation can help the team while limiting individual IDP volume.

## Assessment

- Confidence: medium in the personnel hierarchy; low in front rates and run-defense quality.
- Fact/inference boundary: roster and coach-stated principles are evidence. Alignment, rotation, and performance are hypotheses.
- What would invalidate this: another interior defender displaces Grant, Miami uses a narrow front family, or the front succeeds without the expected personnel concentration.
