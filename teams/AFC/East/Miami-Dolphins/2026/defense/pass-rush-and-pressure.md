# Pass rush and pressure

## Finding

Chop Robinson is Miami's clear lead edge, but the defense has not established how it will replace the volume and attention lost with Bradley Chubb and Jaelan Phillips. Josh Uche has the best early evidence for a complementary passing-down role. Clelin Ferrell offers an early-down profile, while David Ojabo, Trey Moore, Cameron Goode, Robert Beal Jr., Max Llewellyn, and others compete for specialized work.

The healthy outcome is a role-based rotation that generates pressure with four and uses simulated or heavier pressure selectively. The failure outcome is blitz dependence that exposes an unsettled secondary.

## Evidence

| Role | Current leader | Alternatives | Open question |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lead edge | Chop Robinson | none with equivalent current claim | can he sustain production as the offense's primary protection focus? |
| Passing-down complement | Josh Uche | Ojabo; Moore; Goode | does the role include enough snaps for stable production? |
| Early-down edge | Clelin Ferrell | Goode; Beal; Moore | who sets the edge without telegraphing pass packages? |
| Developmental rush | Ojabo; Moore; Llewellyn | Beal; Coleman | which player earns recurring third-down work? |

Camp reports describe Robinson winning against run and pass, Uche reaching the backfield and winning one-on-ones, and Moore setting an edge on a notable rep. Those observations open role hypotheses; they do not establish regular-season pressure rates.

The dedicated [`complementary-pass-rush-audit.md`](complementary-pass-rush-audit.md) tests each candidate's historical production and constraints, the interior contribution, plausible four-man combinations, and whether the rush can survive opponent counters directed at Robinson.

## Pressure diagnosis

Chart pressure by:

- number of rushers;
- edge and interior alignment;
- true four-man rush versus simulated pressure;
- game, stunt, or straight rush;
- time to pressure;
- first pressure and finishing defender;
- quarterback escape or throw effect;
- coverage consequence;
- whether the pressure repeats after opponent adjustment.

Sacks alone are insufficient. A free rusher created by design is different from an individual win, and a high pressure rate paired with explosive completions can still be a losing outcome.

## Fantasy implication

- Robinson has the strongest edge IDP ceiling, but protection attention may make weekly sacks volatile.
- Uche can be efficient without becoming a dependable IDP if he is limited to passing downs.
- Miami D/ST needs repeatable pressure with four to become more than a matchup streamer.
- Opposing quarterbacks gain ceiling if Miami must send extra rushers and cannot disguise the coverage behind them.

## Assessment

- Confidence: medium that Robinson leads and Uche owns the best passing-down claim; low in snap and pressure distribution.
- Fact/inference boundary: departures and dated camp observations are evidence. Package roles and pressure sustainability are hypotheses.
- What would invalidate this: another edge wins the complementary role, Miami concentrates snaps differently, or interior pressure drives the unit.
- Next review: every preseason third down and two-minute series; regular-season review after 50 opponent dropbacks.
