# Miami Dolphins 2026 defense

## Finding

Miami is building a multiple, personnel-responsive defense around established interior defender Zach Sieler, every-down linebacker Jordyn Brooks, and ascending edge Chop Robinson. Jeff Hafley will call the defense while coordinator Sean Duggan manages installation and operation. Their stated menu includes even and odd fronts, situational Wide-9 alignments, four-man and simulated pressure, heavier pressure, press and off coverage, and man and zone.

The front seven has a credible disruptive ceiling. The secondary creates the larger floor risk after the departures of Minkah Fitzpatrick, Rasul Douglas, and Kader Kohou. First-round corner Chris Johnson is cross-training outside and at nickel, the opposite boundary job lacks strong confirmation, and the safety division remains unsettled.

**Preseason grade: high variance, with the front ahead of the coverage unit.** Miami can become a useful matchup defense if it controls the run and pressures with four. If those conditions fail, Hafley may need heavier boxes or additional rushers that expose an inexperienced secondary.

## Evidence

| Unit | Established evidence | Central uncertainty |
|---|---|---|
| Interior line | Sieler is the proven anchor; Kenneth Grant returns after a developmental rookie season | Grant's second-year growth and reserve gap control |
| Edge | Robinson is the lead rusher and has produced positive camp evidence | complementary early-down and passing-down roles |
| Linebacker | Brooks is extended through 2029 and projects as the every-down foundation | Dodson–Rodriguez–Gay package distribution |
| Cornerback | Johnson projects to start and is learning two positions | his primary alignment, the other boundary corner, and nickel |
| Safety | Dante Trader has the strongest returning claim; Zayne Anderson has camp ball production | deep/box division, communication, and Lonnie Johnson's availability |

Hafley and Duggan have described a flexible system rather than fixed rates. Those statements establish the available menu, not Miami's base front, blitz rate, coverage family, or snap concentration.

## Defensive dependency chain

1. Sieler, Grant, and the rotation control the run without routine extra support.
2. Hafley preserves split-safety and coverage flexibility.
3. Robinson and the complementary rush create pressure without blitz dependence.
4. The young secondary receives manageable assignments.
5. Disruption creates takeaway opportunities without requiring unsustainable turnover luck.

A failure early in that chain transfers stress to every later layer.

## Fantasy implication

- **Miami D/ST:** matchup-dependent streamer with sack and takeaway upside, not yet an automatic weekly starter.
- **Jordyn Brooks:** safest IDP snap and tackle projection.
- **Chop Robinson:** strongest sack ceiling; pressure must convert without Chubb or Phillips drawing attention.
- **Zach Sieler:** stable interior role, most useful in defensive-line-required formats.
- **Chris Johnson and Jacob Rodriguez:** rookie upside depends on alignment and package concentration.
- **Opponents:** receivers retain upside until Miami proves it can pressure with four and stabilize its secondary.
