# Bobby Slowik — Miami Dolphins

- Role: Offensive coordinator; offensive designer and play-caller
- Joined team: 2025; promoted for 2026
- Last verified: 2026-08-05

## Responsibilities

Slowik controls the offensive system and play-calling. Kevin Patullo is the experienced passing-game coordinator, Bush Hamdan handles quarterback development, and the position coaches shape personnel execution, but no source indicates that Slowik has ceded final offensive authority.

## Scheme and tendencies

Slowik said the offense shares the same “bones” and roots as Mike McDaniel's because both descend from the Shanahan tree: a strong run-game foundation, trench play, precision passing, and methods that keep a defense off balance. He also emphasized that the system must change with personnel. Miami should therefore retain recognizable concepts without being labeled the same offense.

Extensive pre-snap motion is a confirmed design commitment. On August 4, Slowik said Miami had increased on-the-move motion work, acknowledged assignment and snap-point strain, and said abandoning a tool Miami had used effectively would be foolish. Execution quality is unresolved.

Malik Willis adds designed runs, scrambles, keepers, and movement passes. Slowik said those elements will vary by opponent. Their availability changes box counts and rushing efficiency, but may also compete with running backs for carries and goal-line work.

## Personnel usage

Historical tendencies create questions rather than settled roles. A CBS analysis calculated a 16% running-back target rate for Slowik's Houston offenses and 10.2% on Willis's Green Bay attempts, versus 18% as its comparison point. Slowik has publicly said Miami's backs remain part of the passing game. De'Von Achane's route participation, first-read targets, checkdowns, two-minute use, and goal-line share must be measured.

Receiver alignment and tight-end personnel are also unsettled. The roster no longer has the Hill–Waddle structure that defined McDaniel's offense, and the staff must build around a young receiver room, multiple tight-end candidates, and Willis's different skill set.

## Pace and situational decisions

Slowik's first Houston season produced a top-12 offense and the seventh-ranked passing attack. In 2024 Houston fell to 22nd in total yards and 19th in scoring amid line problems, injuries, quarterback regression, and criticized schematic choices. Track early-down pass rate, third-down sequencing, protection answers, red-zone efficiency, and whether motion creates leverage without generating operational penalties.

## Fantasy implications

- Achane's receiving role is the highest-leverage uncertainty; receiving volume should not be assumed from prior Miami usage.
- Willis's designed rushing can raise his floor while redistributing red-zone opportunity.
- Motion and play action may support efficiency, but protection and assignment errors can suppress volume.
- A new target hierarchy creates opportunity, but camp praise is weaker evidence than routes, first-read targets, and two-minute work.

## Statements and reporting

Confirmed intentions: retain extensive motion, run the ball effectively, build from the line of scrimmage, use precision in the pass game, and deploy Willis's mobility when the matchup supports it.

Projection: the offense will resemble the broader Shanahan family while changing materially from Miami 2025. Specific personnel rates remain unverified.

## Reliability cautions

Slowik's 2023 and 2024 Houston seasons pull in opposite directions. Do not select only the successful season or treat the failure as solely coaching. Public descriptions also do not establish actual Miami rates. Preseason and early-season charting must update this profile.
