# Miami Dolphins — 2026 Regime Change and Roster Reset

- Conference/division: AFC East
- Last verified: 2026-08-05
- Scope: changes from the 2025 team to the 2026 preseason roster and staff

Miami dismantled the central structure of the Mike McDaniel–Tua Tagovailoa era and began a younger roster build under general manager Jon-Eric Sullivan and head coach Jeff Hafley. This is a regime change plus roster reset, not a routine offseason refresh.

## Fantasy-relevant snapshot

### Confirmed baseline

- New general manager, head coach, offensive coordinator, defensive coordinator, and special-teams coordinator.
- Starting quarterback Tua Tagovailoa was released; Miami signed Malik Willis.
- Tyreek Hill was released and Jaylen Waddle was traded, leaving Malik Washington as the leading returning wide receiver.
- De'Von Achane, center Aaron Brewer, and linebacker Jordyn Brooks received extensions through at least 2029.
- Miami made 13 draft selections, including first-round offensive lineman Kadyn Proctor and cornerback Chris Johnson.
- The veteran edge and secondary hierarchy was stripped down through the departures of Bradley Chubb, Jaelan Phillips, Minkah Fitzpatrick, Rasul Douglas, and Kader Kohou.

### Working fantasy hypotheses

These are projections to test, not established 2026 outcomes:

- Achane enters camp as the offense's clearest established skill-position centerpiece, but Willis may reduce checkdowns and claim some quarterback rushing or goal-line work.
- The departures of Hill and Waddle create a major target vacuum; first-team two-receiver sets and routes per dropback matter more than camp praise.
- Multiple drafted tight ends and the loss of fullback Alec Ingold may produce a personnel change, but multiple-TE usage must be observed before it is modeled.
- Willis's mobility may increase boot action, movement passes, read-option concepts, and designed quarterback runs.

## Offensive identity

### Confirmed changes

The quarterback profile changed from Tagovailoa's timing, anticipation, and quick-game strengths to Willis's mobility, arm strength, and off-structure ability. The offense also lost Hill, Waddle, Ingold, and several 2025 offensive linemen. Miami added veteran receivers Tutu Atwell, Jalen Tolbert, and Jalen Reagor; drafted three receivers and two tight ends; and invested a first-round pick in Proctor.

### Questions to resolve

- Willis's designed-run, read-option, and boot-action rates.
- Willis's pressure-to-sack rate and intermediate accuracy.
- Quinn Ewers's share of first-team work.
- The first-team receiver combination, slot/boundary alignments, third-down targets, and red-zone packages.
- Chris Bell's return from his late-2025 ACL tear and active/NFI placement.
- Whether Proctor starts at guard or tackle, plus the starting guard combination.
- Patrick Paul's development and Austin Jackson's availability.
- Whether the offense actually increases multiple-tight-end personnel.

## Defensive matchup profile

### Confirmed changes

Brooks and defensive lineman Zach Sieler remain established anchors. Chop Robinson becomes the leading young edge after the exits of Chubb and Phillips. Miami added several low-cost veteran edge options and drafted Trey Moore and Max Llewellyn. The linebacker room added second-round pick Jacob Rodriguez and fourth-round pick Kyle Louis.

The secondary lost Fitzpatrick, Douglas, and Kohou. First-round cornerback Chris Johnson enters a competition-heavy group of young players and inexpensive veterans.

### Questions to resolve

- The edge rotation and pressure production behind Robinson.
- Whether Rodriguez immediately earns an every-down role.
- Whether Johnson opens as CB1 and who wins the nickel job.
- The deep-safety versus box-safety division.
- Hafley's use of single-high, split-safety, press-man, and zone structures.

These items affect opponent passing-game and protection matchups, but no final schematic profile should be assigned before preseason and early-season evidence.

## Coaching and scheme

Chris Grier and Miami parted ways on 2025-10-31, with Champ Kelly serving as interim general manager. Miami hired Sullivan on 2026-01-09. Mike McDaniel was dismissed on 2026-01-08 after a 35–33 regular-season record and two playoff appearances. Hafley became the 12th head coach in franchise history.

The coordinator changes were:

| Unit | 2025 | 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Offense | Frank Smith | Bobby Slowik |
| Defense | Anthony Weaver | Sean Duggan |
| Special teams | Craig Aukerman | Chris Tabor |

Six 2025 assistants were retained: Slowik, Joe Barry, Austin Clark, Lemuel Jeanpierre, Jonathan Krause, and DeShawn Shead. Slowik's promotion from senior pass-game coordinator creates some terminology and personnel continuity, but McDaniel was the former designer and play-caller. The offense should not be treated as unchanged.

The complete role-level staff and retention flags are in [staff.csv](coaching-staff/staff.csv). Authority, staff connections, coach profiles, and measurable scheme hypotheses are organized in the [coaching intelligence module](coaching-staff/README.md).

### Analytical projection

Sullivan publicly emphasized a resilient, physical, and tough roster. The personnel moves are consistent with a younger and more competition-driven build, but “more physical,” “less quarterback-dependent,” and any specific run/pass or coverage tendencies remain hypotheses. Verify them through preseason personnel groupings, play-action and movement rates, designed quarterback runs, defensive fronts, coverage shells, and early-down play calling.

## Special teams

Kicker Jason Sanders and punter Jake Bailey departed. Zane Gonzalez and Riley Patterson entered camp as the named kicker options, Bradley Pinion as the leading punter, and Tucker Addington as the current long-snapper addition captured in the transaction data.

Track the full snap operation, kickoff role, field-goal attempts by distance, and return duties. Atwell, Reagor, and rookie Kevin Coleman Jr. have return backgrounds, but preseason usage should determine the hierarchy.
