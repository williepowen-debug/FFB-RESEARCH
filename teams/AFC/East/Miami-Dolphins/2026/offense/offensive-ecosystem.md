# Finding: Miami's offense must be evaluated as one connected transition

## Scope

- Team: Miami Dolphins
- Area: quarterback, skill-position hierarchy, running-back usage, offensive line, and play calling
- Season: 2026
- Status: active preseason baseline
- Last verified: 2026-08-05

## Finding

Miami has plausible paths to efficient rushing and explosive passing, but unusually little evidence about sustainable volume. Malik Willis has been highly efficient in a small Green Bay sample; De'Von Achane is a proven high-volume centerpiece; the projected offensive line is bigger and more flexible; and Malik Washington and Greg Dulcich have early claims on targets. None of those facts establishes how a full-season Slowik–Willis offense will allocate dropbacks, checkdowns, designed targets, carries, or red-zone chances.

The correct baseline is therefore **conditional optimism with wide role uncertainty**, not a simple upgrade or downgrade from the 2025 offense.

## System map

| Driver | Direct effect | Downstream fantasy effect | Decisive evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Willis designed runs and scrambles | Adds a gap and changes box math | Raises Willis's floor; can help rushing efficiency; can remove RB carries and touchdowns | designed-run share; scramble rate; carries inside the 10 |
| Willis's progression and checkdown behavior | Determines where incomplete downfield concepts end | Can preserve or reduce Achane's receiving floor | RB route rate; first-read targets; checkdowns under pressure |
| Protection continuity | Controls whether deeper concepts develop | Changes Willis's passing floor and WR/TE target quality | pressure rate by position; time to pressure; five-man continuity |
| Receiver separation and blocking | Determines who earns full route participation | Selects the target hierarchy and personnel packages | 11-personnel routes; third-down snaps; red-zone targets; run-blocking trust |
| Tight-end multiplicity | Allows similar looks to produce runs or passes | Can increase 12 personnel and create Dulcich/Kacmarek role splits | inline/slot alignments; routes; pass-block and run-block assignments |

## Evidence

### Facts

- Willis completed 70 of 89 passes for 972 yards, six touchdowns, and no interceptions across the 2024 and 2025 Green Bay regular seasons. He also rushed 42 times for 261 yards and three touchdowns. The sample contains only three starts.
- Achane produced 1,350 rushing yards on 238 carries and caught 67 of 85 targets for 488 yards and four touchdowns in 2025. His 305 touches were far ahead of the other Miami backs.
- The projected line is Patrick Paul, Kadyn Proctor, Aaron Brewer, Jonah Savaiinaea, and Austin Jackson. Proctor is moving from college left tackle to left guard; Savaiinaea opened camp at right guard; Jackson was day-to-day on August 4.
- Washington entered camp with 540 career receiving yards. Tolbert and Atwell supply the most established outside-WR production, while Dulcich produced 222 yards on 16 catches over the final five games of 2025.
- Slowik has confirmed extensive motion, a run-game foundation, precision passing, and situational use of Willis's mobility. He has not established 2026 personnel or target rates.

### Inferences

- Miami can create favorable rushing numbers through quarterback conflict, motion, and a line built to run outside zone, inside zone, and gap concepts.
- Washington currently has the most stable claim on route volume because his offseason work with Willis and improvement at route breaks are independently supported by the coaching staff and camp reporting.
- The offense may use more multiple-tight-end structures than the prior Miami identity because Dulcich supplies receiving ability while Will Kacmarek and Ben Sims supply blocking. That is a personnel-fit hypothesis, not a measured package rate.
- Protection failure would probably change more than sack totals: it could push Willis toward scrambling, reduce deeper receiver opportunities, and either create Achane checkdowns or bypass them entirely.

## Fantasy implication

- **Willis:** attractive superflex rushing floor, but his full-season passing volume and pressure response remain unproven.
- **Achane:** still the offensive centerpiece. His key risk is the quality and type of touches, especially targets and carries near the goal line, rather than loss of the nominal RB1 role.
- **Washington:** best current bet for stable receiver volume, but not yet an established fantasy alpha.
- **Dulcich:** credible late-round receiving bet if he earns routes in passing situations; blocking-oriented tight ends can also create package competition.
- **Tolbert, Atwell, and Douglas:** opportunity exists, but preseason routes and situational use matter more than depth-chart labels.

## Assessment

- Confidence: medium
- Reason: roster and historical production are confirmed, and the coaching intentions are documented; the decisive Miami usage rates do not yet exist.
- Fact/inference boundary: personnel, historical totals, and public statements are facts. Role hierarchy, package frequency, and fantasy value are inferences.
- What would invalidate this: sustained preseason usage that establishes a materially different quarterback order, target hierarchy, backfield split, or line combination.
- Next review: after the first preseason game with chartable first-unit snaps.
