# Finding: Achane's role is secure, but his high-value touch mix is not

## Scope

- Player: De'Von Achane
- Area: workload, receiving, two-minute use, goal-line work, and backfield competition
- Season: 2026
- Status: active preseason baseline
- Last verified: 2026-08-05

## Finding

Achane is the clear centerpiece and RB1. The material fantasy uncertainty is not whether he starts; it is whether Miami preserves his targets, routes, and scoring opportunities while adding Willis's rushing and retaining Ollie Gordon II and Jaylen Wright.

## Evidence

### Facts

- Achane recorded 238 carries for 1,350 yards and caught 67 of 85 targets for 488 yards and four receiving touchdowns in 2025. He totaled 305 touches and 1,838 yards from scrimmage despite missing the finale.
- Gordon and Wright each received 70 carries. Miami described Gordon as the more frequent short-yardage option and Wright as the player who filled in for Achane.
- Achane signed a long-term extension in May 2026. The transaction and roster usage support a stable lead role.
- CBS calculated that Green Bay targeted running backs on 10.2% of Willis's 89 attempts in 2024–25 and that Slowik's Houston offenses targeted running backs at a 16% rate. Those are historical priors, not Miami rates.
- The same analysis found that Green Bay ran on 66.9% of plays with Willis and that Willis accounted for 20.8% of team carries, including scrambles.
- On August 4, official camp reporting described Achane creating explosive gains as both a runner and receiver, including a 25-yard catch-and-run. This confirms involvement, not share.

### Usage scenarios

| Scenario | Receiving | Rushing/scoring | Fantasy effect | Evidence required |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Role preserved | designed routes and targets remain central | clear lead with enough inside-10 work | elite ceiling remains intact | high route share; two-minute role; designed targets; majority of high-value carries |
| Efficiency rises, targets fall | Willis and line improve run efficiency | strong rushing but fewer catches/checkdowns | standard/half-PPR holds better than full PPR | lower RB target rate with stable carries and explosive rushing |
| Touch-quality compression | targets fall and Willis/Gordon take scoring work | Achane remains lead between the 20s | meaningful PPR and touchdown downside | limited two-minute routes; low inside-10 share; QB/Gordon goal-line dominance |
| Workload managed | Wright earns independent series and Gordon keeps short yardage | Achane emphasized in space | lower volume but potentially preserved efficiency | recurring series rotation rather than pure backup substitution |

### Inferences

- Willis's low historical RB target share matters, but the denominator is small and Green Bay did not roster an Achane-equivalent receiving centerpiece. Miami can preserve Achane's targets through designed concepts even if Willis rarely checks down.
- The most important distinction is **designed target versus bailout target**. A reduction in checkdowns is less damaging if screens, option routes, slot alignments, and first-read concepts remain.
- Willis can help Achane's rushing efficiency by holding backside defenders. That benefit does not automatically offset a loss of receptions or touchdowns in PPR scoring.
- Gordon is the clearest threat to short-yardage and goal-line work; Wright is the clearest threat to backup and relief volume. Their roles should not be combined into one generic committee forecast.

## Fantasy implication

- Preserve Achane as the clear top Miami running back while widening the confidence interval around his PPR ceiling.
- Do not apply Willis's 10.2% Green Bay RB-target rate directly to Miami. Track Achane's share of a potentially different team rate.
- The decisive metrics are route participation, targets per route, two-minute snaps, carries inside the 10 and 5, and the identity of the back paired with or replacing Achane.
- Gordon has contingent touchdown value if his short-yardage role expands. Wright has contingent volume value if Miami protects Achane or if Achane misses time.

## Assessment

- Confidence: medium
- Reason: Achane's lead status and prior workload are strong facts, but Slowik–Willis target behavior and scoring allocation are unmeasured.
- Fact/inference boundary: historical touch totals and source-calculated tendency rates are facts attributed to their sources. Projected 2026 effects are scenarios.
- What would invalidate this: Achane loses first-unit routes or the lead rushing role, or Miami clearly designs a higher RB target rate than the historical priors imply.
- Next review: first preseason usage with first-unit backfield snaps; regular-season review after two games.
