# Finding: Washington is the best current volume anchor, but no target alpha is established

## Scope

- Unit: wide receivers and tight ends
- Area: alignment, route participation, situational usage, and target hierarchy
- Season: 2026
- Status: active preseason baseline
- Last verified: 2026-08-06

## Finding

Malik Washington currently has the strongest claim on stable receiver volume, while Greg Dulcich has the strongest receiving claim at tight end. That does not establish either player as a conventional high-volume No. 1 target. The outside jobs, third-down hierarchy, red-zone preferences, and 11-versus-12-personnel distribution remain open.

## Evidence

### Facts

- Washington entered camp with 540 career receiving yards and is tied for the longest Dolphins tenure in the receiver room. Slowik said his footwork and separation at route breaks had visibly improved and that his offseason work with Willis was evident.
- Jalen Tolbert's best NFL season produced 49 catches, 610 yards, and seven touchdowns in 2024. Tutu Atwell's best produced 42 catches for 562 yards; his career average is 14.6 yards per reception.
- Miami drafted three stylistically different receivers: Caleb Douglas played flanker, Chris Bell played X, and Kevin Coleman Jr. worked primarily from the slot in college. Bell had not practiced by August 4 while rehabilitating an ACL injury.
- Douglas has been moved among multiple receiver positions and has flashed in red-zone practice. Slowik also emphasized that his technique and ball security remain developmental.
- Miami's official August 6 roster lists 12 receivers: 11 active and Bell on active/NFI.
- Dulcich caught 16 of 18 targets for 222 yards and a touchdown over the final five games of 2025. Miami added blocking-oriented tight ends Will Kacmarek and Ben Sims plus receiving prospect Seydou Traore, then expanded the room to six by claiming rookie Jeremiah Franklin on August 5.
- Early camp reporting identifies Washington as the most consistent receiver, but reporting restrictions have limited public detail about personnel groupings and alignments.
- The official August 6 practice notebook recorded another deep target to Washington and a 30-yard back-shoulder catch by Tolbert from Quinn Ewers. Those plays support broader route possibilities without establishing route or target shares.

### Inferences

- Washington's safest path is not necessarily a traditional X-receiver role. Movement, slot work, option routes, and yards-after-catch opportunities can produce usable volume without making him the offense's dominant boundary target.
- Atwell may create space and formation stress without earning a large target share. Fantasy projections should not equate starting alignment with first-read volume.
- Tolbert and Douglas have the clearest physical profiles for contested and red-zone work, but Miami has not established a preferred red-zone receiver.
- Dulcich can benefit if Willis continues to target tight ends more often than running backs, but that historical Willis distribution is based on only 89 Green Bay attempts.
- Kacmarek's blocking could increase 12-personnel usage while also making offensive tendencies less obvious. More tight-end snaps do not automatically mean more Dulcich routes.

## Fantasy implication

- Washington is the first receiver to monitor because his route-floor evidence is stronger than his competitors', not because his target ceiling is settled.
- Dulcich is the most plausible inexpensive breakout, but route participation and health matter more than the five-game 2025 production spike.
- Tolbert, Atwell, and Douglas should remain separate archetypes in projections: possession/red-zone, speed/space, and developmental size respectively.
- No receiver should receive a confident season-long volume projection until first-unit 11-personnel, two-minute, third-down, and red-zone work is charted.

## Assessment

- Confidence: low
- Reason: camp has supplied direction but not the route, alignment, and first-read data required to establish a hierarchy.
- Fact/inference boundary: roster history, production, and coach statements are facts. The ordering above is an explicitly provisional inference.
- What would invalidate this: first-unit preseason routes that place another receiver or tight end clearly ahead of Washington or Dulcich.
- Next review: first preseason game with alignment and route charting.
