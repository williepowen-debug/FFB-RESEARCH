# Tight-end personnel and roster paths

## Finding

Miami has two players with differentiated leading claims—Greg Dulcich as the receiver and Will Kacmarek as the attached blocker—followed by four candidates whose paths depend on special teams, redundancy, and availability. A four-tight-end opening roster is plausible if Miami commits to multiple-TE structures, but no public evidence yet establishes the final count.

## Evidence

### Greg Dulcich

- **NFL evidence:** 67 catches for 799 yards and three touchdowns across 31 career games. He produced 26 catches for 335 yards and one touchdown in 10 Miami games in 2025.
- **Role evidence:** Miami retained him on a one-year deal after his late-season production, and Sullivan identified him as a 2026 riser.
- **Best path:** route leader, movement tight end, middle-field target, and designed YAC option.
- **Constraint:** an injury-interrupted NFL history and an unproven complete inline role.

### Will Kacmarek

- **College evidence:** 65 catches for 761 yards and four touchdowns in 55 games; 24 starts; 2025 honorable-mention All-Big Ten.
- **Role evidence:** Miami selected him 87th overall and publicly emphasized his point-of-attack blocking. Ohio State used him attached, off the ball, in the backfield, and split out.
- **Best path:** primary Y, first-unit run blocker, play-action leak/seam receiver, and multi-TE formation anchor.
- **Constraint:** outside evaluators project sparse receiving production and identify pad-level and hand-placement work.

### Ben Sims

- **NFL evidence:** 45 games and eight starts over three seasons with Green Bay and Minnesota; 11 catches for 93 yards and one touchdown.
- **Role evidence:** Green Bay used him primarily as an inline blocker in two-TE packages as a rookie. Packers coaches documented improvement in his run-game technique, and he contributed on special teams.
- **Miami evidence:** a 40-yard minicamp touchdown and a later red-zone score show that his camp role is not blocking-only.
- **Best path:** assignment-reliable veteran, second blocking tight end, short-yardage/red-zone package player, and special teamer.
- **Constraint:** limited target history and direct functional overlap with Kacmarek.

### Cole Turner

- **NFL evidence:** 13 catches for 143 yards in 23 games, with three starts.
- **College evidence:** converted from wide receiver at Nevada and produced 111 of his 117 college catches plus 19 of his 20 touchdowns after the position change.
- **Best path:** height/catch-radius receiving depth and red-zone package competitor.
- **Constraint:** minimal recent NFL production, limited established blocking value, and overlap with Dulcich/Traore as a receiving-first option.

### Seydou Traore

- **College evidence:** 131 catches for 1,480 yards and 10 touchdowns at Arkansas State and Mississippi State.
- **Athletic evidence:** Miami reported a 4.59-second 40-yard dash, 40-inch vertical, and 10-foot-7 broad jump; NFL's International Player Pathway feature reported a 4.50 at the HBCU combine.
- **Role evidence:** fifth-round draft capital, practice catches, blocking work, and an explicitly open path through special teams.
- **Best path:** developmental F, space/vertical receiver, move blocker, and core special teamer.
- **Constraint:** late start in American football, 235-pound frame, developing route detail, and limited projection as an attached NFL blocker.

### Jeremiah Franklin

- **College evidence:** 93 catches for 1,010 yards and six touchdowns at Boston College, including 49 for 506 yards in 2025.
- **Acquisition evidence:** New England signed him July 24, released him August 4, and Miami claimed him August 5.
- **Best path:** developmental H-back/move blocker, short-area depth, and special-teams competition.
- **Constraint:** late entry into Miami's installation, no NFL game evidence, and an NFL.com projection that questions separation and contested-catch success.

## Functional overlap

| Function | Strongest current claims | Roster consequence |
|---|---|---|
| route/target leader | Dulcich | Turner or Traore needs a distinct package or teams role |
| attached Y/run blocker | Kacmarek, Sims | Sims must show value beyond being a less-invested version of Kacmarek |
| movement/F receiver | Dulcich, Traore, Turner | the third receiving-first candidate may be difficult to carry |
| H-back/move blocker | Kacmarek, Franklin | Franklin must catch up operationally and contribute on teams |
| red zone | Dulcich, Sims, Turner, Traore | preseason first-read targets should decide the order |
| special teams | Sims, Traore, Franklin | likely separator for TE3/TE4 roster spots |

## Roster scenarios

### Three tight ends

- Dulcich and Kacmarek are the differentiated pair.
- The final spot goes to the strongest special-teams and emergency-role candidate.
- This outcome would reduce tolerance for a specialist who cannot cover multiple jobs.

### Four tight ends

- Supports sustained 12/13 personnel, injury insulation, and a developmental receiving player.
- Sims can serve as veteran blocking depth while Traore develops.
- Turner and Franklin face the narrowest paths unless their preseason roles separate.

### Five tight ends

- Requires unusually strong special-teams value, an availability concern, or a temporary roster mechanism.
- It should not be projected solely from the current six-man camp count.

## Fantasy implication

Only Dulcich has a current redraft case. Kacmarek is relevant in dynasty and tight-end-premium formats because draft capital and blocking can buy early snaps, but snaps without routes do not produce fantasy value. Every other candidate needs a roster win plus a change in the lead pair's health or role before becoming actionable.
