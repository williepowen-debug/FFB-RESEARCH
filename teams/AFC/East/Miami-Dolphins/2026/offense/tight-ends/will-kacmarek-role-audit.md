# Will Kacmarek role and translation audit

## Finding

Kacmarek is the room's strongest candidate to become the core attached Y immediately. His third-round draft capital, college alignment range, blocking workload, and Miami's public evaluation make that more than a generic rookie projection. The uncertainty is not whether Miami intends to use his blocking; it is whether NFL technique, assignments, and receiving value allow him to stay on the field across play types rather than telegraphing run or rotating out for Dulcich.

Kacmarek can be highly valuable to Miami and De'Von Achane without becoming fantasy relevant. The threshold for fantasy value is **routes and targets**, not pancake blocks, starts, or total snaps.

## Evidence

### Production

- 55 career games, 24 starts, 65 receptions, 761 yards, and four touchdowns across Ohio and Ohio State.
- 23 catches for 253 yards and two touchdowns in two Ohio State seasons.
- Played all 14 games with 10 starts in 2025 and earned honorable-mention All-Big Ten.
- Ohio State used Kacmarek and Max Klare together on 49 percent of plays, according to Miami's draft analysis.

Miami's `Fast Facts` article separately reported 1,742 career snaps, a blocking assignment on 67 percent of them, and 575 routes. Its accompanying 55-catch total conflicts with the 65-catch career total in both Ohio State's biography and Miami's draft release. Use the official 65-catch total for career production and treat the snap/catch-rate calculation as a separately defined or potentially incomplete sample.

### Blocking translation

Miami's evaluation described Kacmarek as an upper-echelon blocker with strong hands, point-of-attack aggression, and the ability to change blocking angles from multiple alignments. NFL.com agreed that size, strength, down-block displacement, and zone-block washing are central traits, while identifying pad level and hand placement as development needs.

Those assessments support a projection, not an NFL result. Preseason must test:

- base and balance against NFL edges;
- hand timing and placement;
- sustain rather than first-contact movement alone;
- reach, sift, insert, and space-block assignments;
- recognition against movement and late defensive rotation;
- pass-protection reliability.

### Receiving translation

Kacmarek says his limited college targets reflected an offense full of weapons rather than an inability to catch. Sullivan identified underneath zone work, seam ability, size, and hands as usable traits. NFL.com's projection was more conservative, expecting most catches from schemed opportunities and short zone-beaters.

The useful middle position is that Kacmarek has enough receiving ability to punish neglect, but no evidence yet that he can consistently separate from NFL man coverage or command volume.

## Miami role paths

| Role path | Description | Fantasy meaning |
|---|---|---|
| core Y / low route | starts, blocks, and leaves for passing packages | major real-football value; little redraft value |
| core Y / functional receiver | stays in across run/pass and releases on play action | dynasty/TE-premium upside; helps disguise |
| 12-personnel co-starter | pairs with Dulcich, with role changing by formation | both can play, but route overlap determines value |
| protection helper | chips or stays in because of tackle issues | high snaps can conceal low opportunity |
| developmental rotation | technique or assignment errors move Sims ahead temporarily | delays early role without disproving long-term investment |

## System effects

### Run game

A credible Y can:

- widen or pin the edge;
- insert or lead through the formation;
- create split-flow and counter action;
- allow similar formations to support zone and gap runs;
- hold backside defenders when paired with Willis's read threat.

### Passing game

The same alignments can create:

- play-action leaks;
- flat and intermediate zone routes;
- seams against linebackers or safeties;
- delayed releases after chips;
- protection indicators that become constraint plays.

The best outcome is not a large target total by default. It is that defenses cannot confidently read run or protection from Kacmarek's presence.

## False fantasy signals

- **Starting:** he may start in base personnel while Dulcich runs more routes.
- **High snaps:** many can be run blocks, pass blocks, chips, or decoy releases.
- **Team rushing success:** raises his job security more directly than his targets.
- **A preseason catch:** does not establish a route tree or first-read role.
- **Multiple-TE usage:** can add blocking snaps without adding eligible routes.
- **Dulcich absence:** can increase Kacmarek's snaps while Turner or Traore absorbs receiving routes.

## Fantasy implication

- **Redraft:** avoid outside very deep and tight-end-premium formats until a meaningful route rate appears.
- **Dynasty:** a reasonable developmental hold because third-round investment and blocking create a path to early NFL playing time.
- **TE premium:** more attractive if preseason shows third-down routes or a real two-TE passing package.
- **Player props:** reception projections should remain low until route participation is measured; anytime-touchdown interest requires red-zone release and first-read evidence.

## Assessment

- Confidence: medium on an immediate blocking-led role; low on receiving volume.
- Reason: investment and college usage strongly support opportunity, but no NFL snaps establish the translation.
- Fact/inference boundary: college production, alignments, and evaluations are evidence. NFL role and fantasy value are projections.
- What would invalidate this: Sims consistently takes the lead Y work, Kacmarek's assignments or technique prevent first-unit use, or Kacmarek unexpectedly leads high-value routes and targets.
- Next review: first two preseason games with run/pass assignment charting.
