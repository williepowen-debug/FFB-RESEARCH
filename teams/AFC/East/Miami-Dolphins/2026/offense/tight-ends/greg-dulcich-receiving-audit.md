# Greg Dulcich receiving-role audit

## Finding

Dulcich is Miami's best current bet to lead the tight-end room in routes and targets, and he has a plausible path to finish among the team's top two target earners. His late-2025 production demonstrated NFL receiving value; it did not establish full-season health, every-down usage, or a repeatable target rate under Malik Willis.

The correct fantasy stance is **cheap upside with explicit participation gates**. Dulcich becomes a viable weekly starter only if he is healthy, clearly leads tight-end routes, stays involved on third down and in two-minute offense, and receives enough red-zone opportunity to overcome an uncertain team passing volume.

## Evidence

### 2025 Miami production

- 10 games, three starts, 26 receptions, 335 yards, and one touchdown.
- 12.9 yards per reception with seven gains of at least 20 yards.
- 16 receptions on 18 targets for 222 yards and one touchdown over the final five games.
- Miami Herald reported that no Dolphin received more targets over the final three games and that Dulcich led NFL tight ends at 7.6 yards after catch per reception for the season.

This is meaningful efficiency and a strong finish. It remains a small, end-of-season sample created after Miami promoted Dulcich from the practice squad and lost other tight ends to injury.

### Receiving profile

- His 2022 Denver rookie season produced 33 catches for 411 yards and two touchdowns in 10 games.
- Across the two seasons in which he appeared in 10 games—2022 and 2025—he combined for 59 catches and 746 yards.
- His UCLA career ended with 77 catches for 1,353 yards, 11 touchdowns, and 17.6 yards per catch.
- Former Miami coaches publicly identified leverage, separation, ball skills, route awareness, downfield speed, and yards after catch as strengths.
- Offseason reporting described early chemistry with Willis, and the two had prior Senior Bowl familiarity.

The evidence supports an NFL receiving role. It does not prove that the late-2025 yards-after-catch rate or explosive frequency will persist with more defensive attention.

## Availability and role limits

### Health

Dulcich played only 12 of a possible 34 regular-season games over his first two Denver seasons and had repeated hamstring trouble. He reached 10 games again in Miami in 2025 but entered the current camp with another day-to-day absence reported on August 4.

Do not label him injury-prone as a deterministic forecast. Do require current participation before projecting a route lead.

### Blocking and deployment

Dulcich has said he is working to become complete in run blocking, pass protection, and route running. Miami Herald cited a below-league-average 2025 PFF assessment for both run and pass blocking. That grade is an evaluation, not an official result, but it identifies the reason Kacmarek can lead attached snaps even if Dulcich leads routes.

The relevant question is not whether Dulcich becomes an elite blocker. It is whether he blocks well enough to:

1. avoid becoming a passing tendency tell;
2. stay on the field when Miami shifts from run to pass;
3. release rather than chip or remain in protection;
4. preserve third-down and two-minute routes.

## Volume gates

| Gate | Useful evidence | Failure signal |
|---|---|---|
| health | full practice and active status without managed limitation | recurring absence or game-day limitation |
| route lead | clear lead in routes per Willis dropback | Kacmarek/Sims rotation keeps Dulcich near a part-time rate |
| high-value situations | third down, two-minute, red-zone, and play-action routes | early-down-only or package-specific work |
| target earning | first-read and progression targets independent of coverage busts | targets arise mainly from broken plays or reserve quarterbacks |
| protection release | low chip/stay-in rate on pass plays | tackle issues turn him into an auxiliary protector |
| team volume | functional passing efficiency at representative attempt volume | low-attempt offense requires touchdown dependence |

## Fantasy implication

### Redraft

- Draft as a TE2/upside bench option, not as a role-secure starter.
- He becomes a strong early-season waiver or late-draft target if preseason confirms the route and high-value-situation lead.
- A nominal Kacmarek starting label is not itself a downgrade. A Dulcich route loss is.

### Tight-end premium

- The price can be more aggressive because a moderate reception floor matters.
- Do not pay for a top-two team target share before route participation is established.

### Best ball

- Explosive history and low acquisition cost are attractive.
- Correlation with Willis is positive, but team passing-volume failure can suppress both receiving ceiling and spike-week frequency.

### Dynasty

- Dulcich is 26 and on a one-year contract, so 2026 opportunity is stronger than long-term role security.
- Kacmarek and Traore give Miami alternatives if Dulcich misses time or fails as a complete player.

## Assessment

- Confidence: medium that Dulcich is the leading receiving tight end; low-to-medium on weekly fantasy value.
- Reason: two separate 10-game samples demonstrate receiving ability, but health and 2026 deployment remain unsettled.
- Fact/inference boundary: production and availability history are facts. Target rank and fantasy finish are projections.
- What would invalidate this: another tight end leads Willis dropback routes or high-value targets, recurring absence, or a protection role that materially reduces release rate.
- Next review: first preseason game in which Dulcich is active.
