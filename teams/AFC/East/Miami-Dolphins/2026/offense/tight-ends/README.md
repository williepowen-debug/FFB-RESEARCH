# Miami Dolphins 2026 tight ends

## Finding

Miami has built a complementary tight-end room rather than a settled one-player hierarchy. Greg Dulcich has the best evidence to lead the position in routes and targets. Third-round pick Will Kacmarek has the clearest path to the core attached-Y and run-blocking role. Ben Sims, Cole Turner, fifth-round pick Seydou Traore, and August 5 waiver claim Jeremiah Franklin compete for receiving packages, blocking depth, special-teams work, and the remaining roster places.

The room can support more 12 and 13 personnel without making one player an every-down fantasy tight end. Miami may call Kacmarek the starter because he opens in an attached formation, then give Dulcich more routes and targets. Conversely, Dulcich can lead the room in snaps while losing valuable passing plays to chips, pass protection, rotation, or injury. **Starter label, raw snap share, route participation, and target share must be tracked separately.**

**Preseason grade: functionally diverse with an unresolved fantasy role.** Dulcich provides the only demonstrated NFL receiving ceiling. Kacmarek supplies the strongest blocking projection. The other four candidates have narrower or less proven paths, and the room became more competitive when Miami claimed Franklin.

## What the construction says

### Facts

- Miami re-signed Dulcich, added two veteran competitors, drafted Kacmarek and Traore, and then claimed Franklin after camp began.
- General Manager Jon-Eric Sullivan described Kacmarek as a difference-making run blocker who could expand the coordinator's options.
- Kacmarek's Ohio State offense used two-tight-end sets on 49 percent of its plays with Kacmarek and Max Klare, according to Miami's draft analysis.
- Ben Sims said Miami was using the room's different skill sets to expand rather than limit the playbook.
- Practice reporting has identified Kacmarek and Traore contributing as blockers and receivers. Sims produced two reported red-zone touchdowns during the offseason/camp, and Dulcich scored in minicamp red-zone work.

### Inferences

- The investment supports a serious multiple-tight-end experiment, but it does not establish a 12-personnel rate.
- Kacmarek can increase Dulcich's receiving value if his blocking allows Dulcich to release, move, or align away from the formation. He can also remove Dulcich from attached snaps and cap raw playing time.
- Miami's roster may be selecting different players for different jobs rather than seeking one complete tight end. That creates football flexibility but can fragment fantasy volume.
- Franklin's claim is more likely a response to room depth, availability, or evaluation opportunity than evidence of an immediate offensive role.

## Fantasy implication

- **Dulcich:** late-round upside bet, not yet a stable TE1. His ceiling requires health, a clear route lead, and enough team pass volume to convert efficiency into weekly targets.
- **Kacmarek:** stronger real-football than redraft profile today. He can earn high snaps without enough routes to matter in standard leagues; he has deeper dynasty and tight-end-premium appeal if his receiving role exceeds the college baseline.
- **Sims, Turner, Traore, and Franklin:** roster and package watches rather than draftable redraft options. Traore has the best developmental receiving traits; Sims has the strongest veteran path to active-day blocking work.
- **Willis and Achane:** credible multiple-TE structure can improve run disguise and play action. It can also reduce pass-catcher routes if tight ends stay in to protect.
- **Wide receivers:** more 12 personnel can remove a third receiver, but two credible tight-end threats can create favorable formations and coverage. Personnel rate alone is not a receiver downgrade.

## Assessment

- Confidence: medium
- Reason: the six candidates and their historical profiles are established; route shares, personnel rates, health, and roster outcomes are not.
- Fact/inference boundary: the room's functional diversity is supported. The deployment hierarchy remains a projection.
- What would invalidate this: one player earns a true every-down role across attached, passing, third-down, and two-minute situations, or roster moves materially change the candidate set.
