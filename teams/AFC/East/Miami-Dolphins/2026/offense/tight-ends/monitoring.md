# Tight-end monitoring framework

## Finding

The room can only be resolved by charting player function on each snap. Depth-chart order, starts, highlights, and total snaps are insufficient because Miami can divide attached blocking, receiving, protection, motion, and special teams among different players.

## Evidence

| Priority | Question | Review trigger |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Who leads routes on Willis dropbacks? | every first-unit pass play |
| 1 | Is Dulcich healthy and active in high-value situations? | every practice status and game |
| 1 | Does Kacmarek stay on the field for both run and pass? | every first-unit series |
| 1 | How often do two or more tight ends release together? | every 12/13-personnel pass |
| 2 | Do tight-end substitutions reveal run or pass? | every change of personnel |
| 2 | Who owns third down, two-minute, and red-zone routes? | every high-value situation |
| 2 | How often are tight ends retained or delayed by protection? | every dropback |
| 2 | Can Kacmarek's blocking translate against starting edges? | every identifiable assignment |
| 3 | Which depth candidate contributes on special teams? | every preseason teams phase |
| 3 | Can Traore or Turner earn a distinct receiving package? | every route and target |
| 3 | Where does Franklin fit after the late claim? | every appearance and special-teams rep |

## Minimum chart fields

### Every offensive snap

- date/game, quarter, down, distance, score, and field position;
- personnel group and each tight end on the field;
- alignment: attached, wing, backfield, slot, wide, or motion;
- pre-snap motion and final location;
- run, pass, RPO, or quarterback design;
- player assignment and result.

### Pass plays

- route, chip-and-release, delayed release, pass block, or decoy;
- route depth and area;
- man/zone indicator when identifiable;
- first-read, progression, outlet, or scramble-drill target;
- target, catch, yards, first down, touchdown, and YAC;
- pressure timing and whether protection changed the route.

### Run plays

- concept, direction, intended gap, and quarterback read;
- block type and target defender;
- point-of-attack, sustain, displacement, loss, or assignment error;
- whether tight-end alignment or motion changed the edge/box;
- play result and situational success.

### Special teams

- unit and role;
- first-wave versus reserve placement;
- coverage/block result;
- penalty or assignment error;
- game-day active status.

## Derived rates

- routes per team dropback;
- routes per player pass snap;
- targets per route;
- first-read targets per route;
- third-down and two-minute route share;
- red-zone and inside-10 route/target share;
- inline, slot, backfield, and wide alignment rate;
- chip, delay, and pass-block rate;
- run-block rate and assignment success by concept;
- 12/13-personnel rate and two-plus-TE release rate;
- personnel tendency by run/pass outcome;
- special-teams snaps by unit.

## Evidence hierarchy

1. Regular-season all-22 or clear broadcast snaps against starters.
2. Preseason first-unit snaps with identifiable assignments.
3. Official participation and gamebook data.
4. Repeated independent practice observations.
5. Coach/player statements about role and technique.
6. Isolated highlights, nominal depth charts, and unsourced fantasy claims.

## Update rules

- Do not move Dulcich into weekly-starter range from targets alone; require a stable route denominator.
- Do not downgrade Dulcich because Kacmarek starts; compare routes and high-value work.
- Do not upgrade Kacmarek from snaps or blocking highlights without routes.
- Do not treat 12 personnel as receiving-friendly unless both tight ends release at a meaningful rate.
- Do not treat one red-zone touchdown as a stable role; chart route and first-read share.
- Separate team offensive success from individual fantasy opportunity.
- Preserve injuries and daily roster state in preseason/weekly records rather than converting them into durable traits.

## Escalation triggers

Update the module immediately when:

- a tight end is signed, claimed, waived, released, or placed on reserve;
- Dulcich's participation changes;
- Miami publishes or materially changes an official depth chart;
- Kacmarek loses or consolidates first-unit attached work;
- a depth player earns first-unit third-down/two-minute routes;
- protection causes repeated chips or stay-ins;
- special-teams placement clearly separates the roster competition;
- personnel strongly predicts run/pass across multiple games.

## Fantasy implication

The first actionable output is not a season-long projection. It is a role classification:

- **route leader:** potential fantasy target;
- **block-first starter:** football value without automatic fantasy value;
- **package receiver:** best-ball/prop relevance only;
- **special-teams active:** roster security without receiving opportunity;
- **inactive/developmental:** dynasty watch, not weekly use.
