# Finding: the projected line raises the run-game ceiling but has a continuity-dependent pass-protection floor

## Scope

- Unit: offensive line and protection structure
- Area: starting five, run-scheme fit, pass protection, depth, and fantasy transmission
- Season: 2026
- Status: active preseason baseline
- Last verified: 2026-08-05

## Finding

Miami's projected line is large, athletic, and capable of supporting outside zone, inside zone, and gap concepts. Its risk is not a lack of physical upside; it is the need to stabilize two young guards, protect Austin Jackson's availability, and develop five-man communication against pressure and line games.

## Evidence

### Facts

- The projected five is LT Patrick Paul, LG Kadyn Proctor, C Aaron Brewer, RG Jonah Savaiinaea, and RT Austin Jackson. Miami lists the group at an average above 321 pounds.
- Brewer earned second-team All-Pro recognition in 2025. His movement ability is central to Miami's capacity to reach and lead blocks in space.
- Paul started 17 games in 2025. Proctor was a college left tackle and has worked at left guard throughout camp. Savaiinaea played left guard as a rookie and opened 2026 camp at right guard.
- Jackson has missed games in each of the past two seasons and was day-to-day after leaving practice on August 3. Slowik said Charlie Heck entered with the first unit on August 4.
- Hafley said the personnel can run outside zone, inside zone, and gap schemes. Slowik said Miami had reached the point in camp where the line had seen its full set of runs, protections, defensive looks, and pressure presentations; the emphasis was shifting toward combinations, technique, and handling games.

### Projected five

| Position | Starter | Primary strength hypothesis | Main risk to test |
|---|---|---|---|
| LT | Patrick Paul | length, power, and 17-start continuity | handling speed and pressure without help |
| LG | Kadyn Proctor | rare size with movement ability; downhill displacement | rookie transition from college tackle to NFL guard |
| C | Aaron Brewer | proven movement and second-level range | offense-wide disruption if unavailable |
| RG | Jonah Savaiinaea | size, athleticism, and second-year confidence | position continuity after playing left guard in 2025 |
| RT | Austin Jackson | established run-game value | availability and replacement quality |

### Inferences

- The line's range of body types and movement skills should allow Slowik to vary run concepts without substituting. That helps motion and personnel disguise.
- Proctor at guard can increase displacement in the run game, but interior pass-protection timing and recognition may be a larger early adjustment than raw one-on-one power.
- Willis's mobility can reduce some negative plays and add rushing value, but it can also make traditional pressure statistics harder to interpret. Chart whether pressure is defeated by design, by the line, or by Willis after structure breaks.
- A right-tackle injury has ecosystem effects: more chips and condensed releases can reduce tight-end/receiver routes, while faster throws can change Achane's target opportunities.
- Slowik's 2024 Houston decline makes protection answers a coaching test as well as a personnel test. Miami must show answers to pressure rather than rely only on quarterback escape ability.

## Fantasy implication

- Achane's rushing efficiency can rise if Miami consistently reaches the edge and creates vertical movement inside.
- Willis's fantasy floor may look healthy through rushing even while pass-catcher opportunity is being damaged by protection. Separate quarterback fantasy output from offensive health.
- Atwell and other deeper-route receivers are particularly exposed to pressure that shortens concepts.
- Dulcich's route ceiling depends partly on whether Miami needs tight ends to chip or remain in protection.

## Assessment

- Confidence: medium
- Reason: the projected five and intended scheme range are well supported; game-level communication and protection quality are not.
- Fact/inference boundary: player positions, honors, availability, and coach statements are facts. Run-game ceiling and fantasy transmission are inferences.
- What would invalidate this: a different opening five, recurring protection failures despite continuity, or a scheme that narrows to a substantially smaller run menu.
- Next review: Jackson's return; first preseason pressure and run-concept chart.
