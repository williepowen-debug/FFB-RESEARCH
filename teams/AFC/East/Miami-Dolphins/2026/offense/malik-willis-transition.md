# Finding: Willis is the clear starter, but his efficiency has not been volume-tested

## Scope

- Player: Malik Willis
- Area: starting role, passing profile, rushing use, pressure response, and target distribution
- Season: 2026
- Status: active preseason baseline
- Last verified: 2026-08-05

## Finding

Willis should be treated as Miami's clear projected starter, not as part of an open competition. The larger uncertainty is whether Green Bay's efficient, tailored usage scales to a full season and a less-established receiving group.

## Evidence

### Facts

- Miami's official camp preview says Willis is in line to start Week 1. The front office described his three-year contract as a commitment to build around him rather than a temporary bridge.
- Across 2024–25 in Green Bay, Willis completed 70 of 89 passes for 972 yards, six touchdowns, and no interceptions. He carried 42 times for 261 yards and three touchdowns.
- Those Green Bay seasons contain only three starts. In Tennessee, Willis completed 35 of 66 attempts for 350 yards, no touchdowns, and three interceptions during 2022–23.
- NFL.com film analysis described the Green Bay version as calmer and more accurate within a catch-and-run menu, with calculated downfield throws, designed runs, and scrambles.
- Slowik said camp is intentionally testing whether Willis will stay in the progression and deliver throws rather than automatically escaping. Designed quarterback runs will vary with the opponent.
- Quinn Ewers is the principal alternative. Miami reports that he started three games as a 2025 rookie, completing 66% of 83 attempts at 7.5 yards per attempt with three touchdowns and three interceptions.

### Inferences

- The 2024–25 efficiency improvement is meaningful evidence of development, but 89 attempts cannot establish a stable interception rate, pressure response, or target distribution over 17 games.
- Willis's legs create real fantasy value and can improve the conventional run game by forcing an unblocked defender to account for him.
- The same mobility can reduce checkdowns and transfer red-zone work away from Achane. Whether that happens is a usage question, not an unavoidable feature of a mobile quarterback.
- Miami is likely to borrow from Green Bay's movement, play-action, and defined-read solutions while asking Willis to handle more full-field and obvious-passing situations than he previously faced.

## Passing-profile tests

| Question | Confirming evidence | Disconfirming evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Does the Green Bay efficiency scale? | stable accuracy and decision-making across full preseason drives and early regular-season volume | efficiency collapses as attempts, third downs, or obvious pass situations rise |
| Can Willis handle pressure without abandoning structure? | completions within progression; throws to outlets; limited avoidable sacks | repeated late scrambles, missed outlets, or sacks after the initial concept is covered |
| Will Miami use his arm beyond horizontal concepts? | intermediate-middle and designed deep targets with acceptable accuracy | passing menu remains almost entirely screen, quick game, or scramble drill |
| Is rushing designed or merely reactive? | option keepers, quarterback power, red-zone designs | most carries arise from broken protection or covered routes |

## Fantasy implication

- Superflex and two-QB value can arrive before Willis proves high conventional passing volume because designed runs and scrambles create a separate scoring channel.
- One-QB value requires evidence that Miami can sustain drives and produce enough passing touchdowns to supplement the rushing floor.
- Willis can support explosive receivers without creating a high-volume receiver. Washington, Tolbert, Atwell, Douglas, and Dulcich must be judged on routes and first-read targets, not only splash plays.
- If Ewers replaces Willis, remove the assumption of designed quarterback rushing and re-evaluate RB targets, protection, and receiver-area preferences from scratch.

## Assessment

- Confidence: medium
- Reason: the starter projection and historical results are clear, but the relevant sample is small and came in a different offense.
- Fact/inference boundary: the statistics, contract framing, and coach statements are facts. Scalable efficiency and 2026 target effects are inferences.
- What would invalidate this: meaningful first-team displacement by Ewers, an injury, or sustained game evidence that the passing profile is materially broader or narrower than projected.
- Next review: first preseason game; then after two regular-season starts.
