# Wide-receiver personnel and roster paths

## Finding

Miami has five relatively strong roster claims—Malik Washington, Jalen Tolbert, Tutu Atwell, Caleb Douglas, and Kevin Coleman Jr.—plus Chris Bell, whose active/NFI status separates long-term investment from immediate availability. The final active places are likely to be decided by special teams, functional differentiation, and whether Bell is ready rather than by receiving highlights alone.

## Evidence

### Leading active group

#### Malik Washington

- Returning receiver with the room's most Miami experience.
- Proven punt and kick returner and a credible rushing/design-touch option.
- Best current path: high route floor, inside/outside movement, third-down work, and selective returns.
- Constraint: 7.5 career receiving yards per catch and no established downfield or boundary-winning season.

#### Jalen Tolbert

- Most productive full NFL receiving season in the room: 49 catches, 610 yards, and seven touchdowns in 2024.
- Current reporting and roster construction support an opening X projection.
- Best current path: every-down boundary routes, back-shoulder/contested opportunities, and red-zone work.
- Constraint: fell to 18-203-1 in 2025 and has not demonstrated alpha-level target earning.

#### Tutu Atwell

- Room's most established vertical résumé: 14.6 career yards per catch and 26 gains of at least 20 yards.
- Miami can use him on motion, overs, clear-outs, shot plays, and occasional manufactured touches.
- Best current path: starting Z whose spacing value earns routes even when targets fluctuate.
- Constraint: 105 catches in five seasons, a 165-pound frame, and a career defined by volatile utilization.

#### Caleb Douglas

- Pick 75 and the active rookie with the clearest outside profile.
- Size, verified speed, vertical targets, and early red-zone practice involvement fill a need no veteran fully owns.
- Best current path: displace a veteran outside, then grow into a full route tree.
- Constraint: independent draft evaluation questioned consistent separation, contested finishing, and focus.

#### Kevin Coleman Jr.

- Fifth-round investment, four years of production, strong hands, and punt-return value.
- Best current path: active-day slot/return role while developing offensive packages.
- Constraint: Washington already occupies the most similar established role; Coleman missed August 4 team work and must show current availability.

### Unavailable investment

#### Chris Bell

- Pick 94 with a physical, productive X/movement profile.
- Miami described the selection as a longer-term investment after his November 2025 ACL injury.
- Best current path: complete rehabilitation, return to full contact, and compete for 2026 boundary/middle-field work without a rushed timetable.
- Constraint: every role conclusion is premature while he remains on active/NFI.

### Final-place competition

#### Theo Wease Jr.

- Six catches for 139 yards and a touchdown after a late 2025 promotion.
- Size and actual Miami regular-season production distinguish him from several competitors.
- Must establish coverage-unit or return-adjacent value if he is not a top-four receiver.

#### Tahj Washington

- Three catches for 37 yards in six 2025 games after missing his rookie season.
- Has continuity and a slot/Z profile but overlaps Washington, Coleman, Atwell, and Henning.
- Ball security and special teams are decisive.

#### Terrace Marshall Jr.

- Former second-round pick with 67 catches for 808 yards; 490 of those yards came in 2022.
- Size and catch radius offer a boundary alternative.
- Minimal recent NFL production and overlap with Tolbert/Douglas/Bell narrow the path.

#### Jalen Reagor

- Former first-round pick with 1,037 receiving yards and NFL return experience.
- Special-teams coordinator Chris Tabor explicitly identified his return skill set as worth evaluating.
- Return security and coverage participation matter more than draft pedigree.

#### AJ Henning

- Spent 2025 on Miami's practice squad and entered the NFL with punt-return experience.
- Needs a core special-teams role because the offensive slot/movement jobs are crowded.

#### Donaven McCulley

- Undrafted 6-4, 203-pound former quarterback with length and catch-point ability.
- Needs to beat press, separate, and contribute on special teams to avoid a developmental-only classification.

## Roster scenarios

### Six active receivers with Bell delayed

- Washington, Tolbert, Atwell, Douglas, and Coleman form the leading five.
- Wease, Tahj Washington, Marshall, Reagor, Henning, and McCulley compete for one place.
- Return and coverage-unit work is the likely separator.

### Six active receivers with Bell ready

- Bell's draft investment creates pressure on both the final depth spot and an outside veteran.
- Miami must decide whether it can carry three rookie receivers plus multiple specialists.
- An active Bell would not automatically enter the top three; practice progression still matters.

### Seven receivers

- Most plausible if Bell's status changes late, special teams requires another receiver, or the staff wants to preserve a developmental outside player.
- More rostered receivers do not imply a larger weekly target pool; game-day actives and personnel rates remain separate.

## Fantasy implication

Roster security is not target security. Douglas and Coleman can be strong roster bets without immediate weekly value, while Atwell can start and still be target-volatile. Bell is a reserve/bench dynasty asset until healthy. The final-place competitors require both a roster win and top-four route work before becoming fantasy relevant.
