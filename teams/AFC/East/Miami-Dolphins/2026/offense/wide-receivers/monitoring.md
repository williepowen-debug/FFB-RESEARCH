# Wide-receiver monitoring framework

## Finding

Miami's receiver room can only be resolved by charting role on every quarterback dropback, not by counting catches or repeating a nominal depth chart. Alignment, personnel, route, target type, blocking, and special teams must be connected so that high snaps are not mistaken for high fantasy opportunity.

## Evidence

| Priority | Question | Review trigger |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Who leads routes on Willis dropbacks? | every first-unit pass play |
| 1 | Who stays on the field in two-receiver personnel? | every 12/21-personnel snap |
| 1 | Who owns third down and two-minute? | every high-leverage pass situation |
| 1 | Are Washington's targets expanding beyond short/design work? | every Washington route and target |
| 1 | Does Tolbert retain X routes and red-zone work? | every first-unit series |
| 2 | Is Atwell a full-route Z or a package player? | every Atwell snap |
| 2 | Does Douglas earn a full outside route tree? | every first-unit rookie rep |
| 2 | Do motion and alignment create targets or decoy value? | every motion snap |
| 2 | Does receiver blocking select the active rotation? | every outside run and play action |
| 2 | When does Bell return, and how is he progressed? | every official participation update |
| 3 | Can Coleman win an independent slot/return role? | every route and return |
| 3 | Which depth player earns core special-teams work? | every preseason teams phase |

## Minimum chart fields

### Every offensive snap

- date/game, quarter, down, distance, score, and field position;
- personnel group and receivers on field;
- alignment: X, Z, slot, wing/stack, backfield, or reduced split;
- motion type, motion timing, and final alignment;
- run, pass, RPO, or quarterback design;
- player assignment and result.

### Pass plays

- route name and depth;
- release result and man/zone indicator when identifiable;
- clear-out/decoy versus viable progression route;
- first-read, progression, outlet, scramble-drill, or throwaway;
- target depth and location;
- target, catch, yards, first down, touchdown, YAC, drop, or contested result;
- pressure timing and whether protection shortened or removed the route.

### Run plays

- block target and technique;
- win, sustain, loss, assignment error, or no meaningful engagement;
- whether receiver motion altered support defenders;
- play direction and situational success;
- whether the same alignment produced pass outcomes.

### Special teams

- returner, backup returner, gunner, jammer, coverage, or blocker;
- first-wave versus reserve placement;
- catch security, decision, return result, penalty, or assignment error;
- game-day active status.

## Derived rates

- routes per team dropback;
- targets and first-read targets per route;
- slot, outside, reduced, and backfield alignment rates;
- short, intermediate, and deep target rates;
- third-down, two-minute, red-zone, and inside-10 route shares;
- two-receiver-personnel route share;
- motion route and target rate;
- designed-touch versus progression-target rate;
- scramble-drill target rate;
- run-block snap and assignment-success rate;
- team 11/12/21-personnel rates;
- punt/kick return and coverage snaps.

## Evidence hierarchy

1. Regular-season all-22 or clear broadcast snaps against starters.
2. Preseason first-unit snaps with identifiable routes and assignments.
3. Official gamebooks, participation, transactions, and injury status.
4. Repeated independent practice observations.
5. Coach/player statements about technique and intended role.
6. Isolated highlights, nominal starts, unofficial depth charts, and fantasy claims.

## Update rules

- Do not upgrade a player from catches without a route denominator.
- Do not call Washington the alpha unless route depth and first-read share expand.
- Do not call Tolbert the target leader solely because he starts at X.
- Do not call Atwell a full-time player from vertical highlights; require core-personnel routes.
- Do not upgrade Douglas from red-zone catches alone; require separation and routes between the 20s.
- Do not assign Bell a role before full participation.
- Do not interpret a return job as offensive opportunity, or offensive investment as return security.
- Do not treat blocking snaps as receiving routes.
- Separate Willis scramble success from receiver concept success.

## Escalation triggers

Update the module immediately when:

- a receiver is signed, traded, waived, released, or placed on reserve;
- Bell's participation designation changes;
- a player consolidates or loses first-unit two-receiver routes;
- the third-down or two-minute trio changes;
- Douglas or Coleman receives sustained first-unit work;
- Washington's return workload changes;
- a depth player moves into the first wave on multiple special-teams units;
- motion or personnel strongly predicts target type across multiple games.

## Fantasy implication

Classify each receiver by demonstrated role:

- **full-route target leader:** redraft candidate;
- **full-route complementary receiver:** matchup/flex candidate;
- **vertical specialist:** best-ball and prop candidate;
- **designed-touch/slot player:** PPR value only with sufficient route share;
- **block-first starter:** football value without automatic targets;
- **return/coverage active:** roster security without receiving value;
- **inactive/developmental:** dynasty watch only.
