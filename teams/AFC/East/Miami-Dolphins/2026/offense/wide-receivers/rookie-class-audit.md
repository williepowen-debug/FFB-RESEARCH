# Rookie wide-receiver class audit

## Finding

Miami drafted complementary archetypes rather than one obvious immediate alpha: Caleb Douglas is the tall vertical flanker, Chris Bell is the physical X/middle-field YAC target, and Kevin Coleman Jr. is the slot/return playmaker. Undrafted Donaven McCulley is a developmental size bet. Douglas has the clearest 2026 opportunity because he is healthy and already involved; Bell has the most consequential long-term ceiling but cannot be assigned a current role while on active/NFI.

## Evidence

### Caleb Douglas — round 3, pick 75

#### Facts

- 135 catches, 2,031 yards, and 16 touchdowns across Florida and Texas Tech.
- Senior season: 54 catches, 846 yards, and seven touchdowns.
- Miami reports a 6-4 frame and 4.39-second 40-yard dash.
- Thirty percent of his final college targets came 20 or more yards downfield; he caught 12 deep passes and four touchdowns.
- Miami has moved him among receiver positions, and early camp included a high-volume red-zone session with two potential touchdowns, other receptions, and a drop.

#### Translation

Douglas gives Miami a rare size/speed profile and can win posts, corners, fades, and back-shoulder opportunities. He must prove that the athletic testing translates against NFL press and tight coverage.

An important evaluation disagreement should be preserved. Miami described a full route tree, dynamic traits, and a large catch radius. NFL.com’s prospect evaluation described uneven tape, focus drops, inconsistent contested success, and separation concerns. Third-round investment creates opportunity; it does not resolve the disagreement.

#### 2026 paths

- **Starter:** earns outside routes in two-receiver personnel and red-zone first reads.
- **Rotational:** replaces Atwell or Tolbert by concept without consolidating a full route share.
- **Developmental:** active roster through investment but remains outside the weekly target tree.

### Chris Bell — round 3, pick 94

#### Facts

- 151 catches, 2,166 yards, and 12 touchdowns at Louisville.
- Produced 917 yards in 2025 despite missing the final two-plus games after an ACL tear.
- Miami highlighted a 6-2, 220-pound build, buildup speed, YAC, and work across the middle.
- His 2025 drop rate fell to four percent, and he generated 372 yards after the catch.
- He remains on active/NFI as of August 6.

#### Translation

Bell is more than a static contested-catch X. His strongest college plays came on crossers and movement targets where size and speed erased pursuit angles. That fits a motion/condensed offense and provides a role distinct from Douglas's vertical emphasis.

The health boundary is absolute: rehabilitation progress is not route readiness, contact readiness, or game availability. Do not convert long-term draft enthusiasm into a 2026 target projection.

### Kevin Coleman Jr. — round 5, pick 177

#### Facts

- 198 catches, 2,501 yards, and 12 touchdowns across four schools.
- Combined 140 catches, 1,664 yards, and seven touchdowns over his final two SEC seasons.
- Two drops on 84 targets in 2025 and nine catches on 11 contested opportunities, per Miami's draft analysis.
- Averaged 12.6 yards per punt return in 2025 with a 67-yard touchdown.
- Minicamp reporting described early uncovering and dependable catches; he later missed August 4 team work.

#### Translation

Coleman's hands, toughness, quickness, slot-fade ability, utility carries, and return résumé create several paths to an active role. His core problem is redundancy: Washington is already the established slot/movement/return player.

The decisive question is not whether Coleman can make plays. It is whether Miami trusts him enough to take independent third-down, two-minute, return, or package work.

### Donaven McCulley — undrafted

#### Facts

- Listed at 6-4, 203 pounds with long arms and large hands.
- Converted from quarterback and played four seasons at Indiana before finishing at Michigan.
- NFL.com credited toughness and catch-point work but questioned press release, separation, and vertical speed.

#### Translation

McCulley's physical profile earns development time, but the room already has Douglas, Bell, Tolbert, and Marshall competing for size-based outside functions. Special teams and rapid route development are necessary for an active-roster path.

## Comparative development map

| Player | Immediate differentiator | Main translation question | Best evidence threshold |
|---|---|---|---|
| Douglas | outside size plus timed speed | separation, focus, and full route detail | first-unit routes against starting corners |
| Bell | physical YAC and middle-field power | health, contact tolerance, and man separation | full practice followed by sustained team reps |
| Coleman | slot quickness, hands, return value | independent role versus Washington | third-down routes and first-team return placement |
| McCulley | length and catch point | release/separation plus special teams | active-game teams work and non-gadget routes |

## Fantasy implication

- **Redraft:** Douglas is the only rookie worth a normal late watch because he can earn immediate outside routes.
- **Dynasty:** Bell has the strongest patient ceiling; Douglas has the clearest near-term opportunity; Coleman has PPR/return-value utility.
- **Best ball:** Douglas can create vertical and touchdown spikes before he earns weekly consistency.
- **Return-yard leagues:** Coleman becomes more interesting if he wins punts or kickoffs.
- **McCulley:** taxi-squad or watch-list only.

Do not draft all three Miami picks as though vacated targets guarantee collective production. One rookie winning routes can reduce the others' opportunities.

## Assessment

- Confidence: medium on player archetypes; low on immediate role order.
- Fact/inference boundary: production, testing, draft capital, health designation, and attributed evaluations are facts. Translation and fantasy paths are projections.
- What would invalidate this: Bell returns ahead of schedule, Coleman takes core offensive work, or Douglas cannot stay in the first-unit rotation.
- Next review: every rookie preseason route, target, return, and blocking assignment.
