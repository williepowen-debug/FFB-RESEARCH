# Depth and contingencies

## Finding

Miami has credible first-wave cover in Jamaree Salyer and Charlie Heck, but the depth model becomes fragile when an absence affects center or forces two changes at once. The most important distinction is between replacing one player and reconfiguring multiple positions to replace one player.

## Evidence

Miami's official roster preview supports Salyer's multi-position value, Heck's veteran tackle experience, and the developmental nature of the remaining depth. The exact reserve order remains unproven.

## Scenario matrix

| Event | Most likely response | Secondary option | System consequence | Severity |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Proctor struggles at LG | Salyer enters at guard | Priebe competes; Proctor continues developmental work | lower physical ceiling but potentially steadier assignments | medium |
| Savaiinaea loses RG job | Salyer starts at RG | Campbell or Priebe rises | more experience; less second-year upside; continuity changes | medium |
| Jackson misses time | Heck enters at RT | Salyer or another developmental tackle | more chips, quicker throws, and fewer free releases are possible | high |
| Paul misses time | Salyer or Heck plays LT | Proctor becomes emergency tackle only if Miami accepts two position changes | blind-side risk and potential disruption at both LT and LG | high |
| Brewer misses time | Meyer leads the center response | Bonifas or cross-training alternative | major communication and movement-blocking downgrade | very high |
| One guard misses time | Salyer enters | Priebe/Campbell follows | manageable if the rest of the five stays fixed | medium |
| Two simultaneous injuries | Salyer plus Heck/young reserve | position shuffle | multiple unproven links; likely contraction of protection and route menu | very high |

## Criticality tiers

### Tier 1: Brewer

Brewer's absence removes both the best established lineman and the organizer between two young guards. This is the hardest single role to replace without changing the unit's communication and movement profile.

### Tier 2: Paul and Jackson

Tackle injuries expose the edge and can require tight ends, backs, and formations to supply help. Paul's absence is especially disruptive if Miami considers moving Proctor, because one injury could change two positions.

### Tier 3: either guard

Salyer gives Miami a credible first response. The cost rises sharply if Salyer is already covering tackle or if both interior spots are affected.

## Decision rules

- Prefer the lineup that changes **one position**, unless the replacement is clearly nonviable.
- Treat Salyer as the primary stabilizer, not automatically the answer at every spot simultaneously.
- Do not describe a reserve as capable at a position based only on a roster label; require camp or preseason snaps.
- When a starter returns, verify whether protection help and run direction normalize before declaring the unit restored.
- Reassess fantasy effects when the lineup changes, even if Miami wins the game or Willis escapes pressure successfully.

## Roster-management watch

- Salyer's actual game-day position coverage.
- Whether Heck clearly wins swing tackle.
- Which player receives reserve-center snaps.
- Whether Campbell earns enough guard work to be active on game day.
- Whether Miami keeps eight, nine, or ten linemen and which reserves contribute on special teams.
- Any veteran addition after league cutdowns.
- Carter Warren's reserve/injured status and any later transaction.

## Fantasy implication

A single guard injury has the most plausible contained response. A tackle injury can reduce eligible-receiver releases, and a Brewer absence warrants an offense-wide downgrade until the replacement proves capable of maintaining communication and run-game timing.
