# Offensive-line monitoring framework

## Finding

Miami's line should be updated through repeatable deployment and play-level evidence, not isolated camp wins or headline sack totals. The monitoring system must distinguish design, blocking, quarterback behavior, and play result.

## Evidence

The current projected five, documented availability concerns, coach-stated concept range, and early camp observations identify the questions below. None yet resolves the competitive outcome.

## Purpose

This file defines what evidence should update the unit assessment. Dated observations remain in [`../../preseason/evidence-log.csv`](../../preseason/evidence-log.csv), and current conclusions remain in [`../../preseason/priority-status.csv`](../../preseason/priority-status.csv). Do not duplicate individual practice reports here.

## Evidence hierarchy

1. Regular-season all-22 or clear broadcast reps against starting defenders.
2. Preseason first-unit reps with identifiable assignments.
3. Repeated practice observations corroborated by multiple independent reporters.
4. Coach/player statements about role, technique, or installation.
5. Team-authored practice highlights and isolated one-on-one results.

Lower-tier evidence can open a question; it should not close one by itself.

## Minimum chart fields

### Pass plays

- date/game, quarter, down, distance, and score state;
- five-man lineup and eligible protectors;
- defensive front and pressure presentation;
- protection type when identifiable;
- chip/stay-in help;
- time to pressure;
- pressure and primary responsibility category;
- Willis response and play result;
- route concept or route-depth effect when visible.

### Run plays

- five-man lineup, personnel, formation, and motion;
- concept family, direction, and intended gap;
- box count and front;
- first contact and yards before contact;
- primary block result and combination-block outcome;
- quarterback-read effect;
- play result and situational value.

## Update states

- **Open:** insufficient competitive evidence.
- **Leaning confirmed:** repeated evidence in one competitive setting.
- **Confirmed:** repeated evidence across at least two meaningful settings or a settled role backed by direct deployment.
- **Leaning rejected:** repeated contrary evidence in one competitive setting.
- **Rejected:** contrary evidence persists across settings or the personnel premise changes.

Sample size and opponent quality must accompany every state change. Do not declare a protection problem solved because a quick-game plan prevented sacks, and do not declare the run game broken because of one short-yardage failure.

## Escalation triggers

Immediately update the module when:

- a projected starter misses practice or changes status;
- Miami changes a first-unit position;
- the same stunt or identification error appears multiple times;
- tight ends or backs are routinely retained in protection;
- a reserve center takes first-unit snaps;
- a veteran lineman is signed, waived, or moved to reserve;
- run concepts or direction narrow materially;
- a lineup change coincides with a clear change in Achane's yards before contact or pass-catcher routes.

## Fantasy implication

The framework converts line changes into player-specific decisions: Achane through contact quality and scoring access, Willis through protection-versus-escape splits, and pass catchers through route releases and target depth.
