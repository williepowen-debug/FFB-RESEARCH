# Pass-protection assessment

## Finding

Pass protection is the unit's principal uncertainty. Paul offers a positive starting point and Brewer supplies communication, but the projected guards are simultaneously changing role or side, Jackson's availability is unresolved, and Miami must integrate those changes with Malik Willis's movement and Bobby Slowik's protection answers.

The key question is not merely how many sacks Miami allows. It is whether five-man protection can preserve intermediate and deep concepts without routine chips, condensed releases, or quarterback rescue.

## Evidence

The projected positions, 2025 starting histories, public performance reporting, and current availability establish the risk map. Competitive Miami pass sets are still needed to measure the outcome.

## Risk map

| Area | Baseline | Risk | Decisive test |
|---|---|---|---|
| Left tackle | Paul started 17 games and received strong team evaluation | repeatability against elite speed and inside counters | true pass sets without chip help |
| Left guard | Proctor brings rare size but is a rookie college-tackle conversion | interior quickness, leverage, twists, and recognition | passing downs against veteran three-techniques and simulated pressure |
| Center | Brewer is the organizer and best established blocker | dependence on one player for both physical and mental stability | front identification and exchanges with both guards |
| Right guard | Savaiinaea has starting experience and right-side familiarity | severe 2025 struggles may persist despite the move | anchor, hand placement, twist exchange, and pressure responsibility |
| Right tackle | Jackson is an experienced starter | recurring availability concern and depth drop-off | health plus isolated protection; Heck's response if needed |
| Quarterback interaction | Willis can escape and punish pressure | holds, scramble tendency, or pocket exits can create or mask pressure | separate time-to-pressure, line responsibility, and quarterback responsibility |

## Protection questions

1. Does Miami identify the pressure correctly before the snap?
2. Do adjacent linemen pass off twists and games without two blockers taking one rusher?
3. Can both tackles survive without routine chip help?
4. Can Proctor anchor with NFL leverage and recover when initially displaced?
5. Does Savaiinaea's right-side return reduce technical and recognition errors?
6. Does Willis climb, escape, or abandon clean pockets?
7. Does the play design create a valid hot answer when the defense brings more rushers than Miami blocks?
8. Are backs and tight ends releasing into routes or being retained to protect?

## Diagnostic taxonomy

Every pressure should be assigned to one primary bucket:

- **individual loss:** blocker identified the correct threat and was beaten;
- **exchange error:** stunt, twist, or game was not passed correctly;
- **identification error:** line or quarterback set the protection away from the threat;
- **numbers loss:** defense brought an unblocked rusher and the throw did not defeat it;
- **design concession:** play intentionally left a rusher unblocked for a screen, option, boot, or immediate throw;
- **quarterback-created:** protection held long enough, but depth, timing, or pocket movement created the pressure;
- **coverage-created:** initial protection held and pressure arrived only after the concept stalled;
- **unclear:** public angle does not permit responsible assignment.

Record time to pressure and help usage where the broadcast angle permits it. A scramble or completion does not erase a protection loss; a sack does not automatically make the line responsible.

## Scheme mitigation versus actual improvement

Miami can reduce negative plays through motion, play action, boots, screens, quick game, max protection, and Willis's legs. Those tools are valuable, but they answer a different question from whether the line can win conventional passing downs.

Track three layers separately:

1. **design:** did the play reduce or redirect the rush?
2. **blocking:** did the assigned protectors win and communicate?
3. **quarterback:** did Willis use the protection and available answer correctly?

If Miami's sack rate improves while route participation and downfield concepts contract, the fantasy benefit will be narrower than the headline suggests.

## Fantasy implication

- Willis can remain fantasy-viable through rushing even if protection lowers his passing volume.
- Downfield receivers are most sensitive to quick interior pressure and max-protection calls.
- Achane may gain checkdowns from pressure, but Willis may also scramble instead of throwing to the outlet.
- Tight-end snap counts can be misleading if protection duties reduce routes.
- Repeated right-side breakdowns would make opposing interior and left-edge matchups a weekly downgrade trigger.
