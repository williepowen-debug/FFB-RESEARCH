# Offensive-line personnel

## Finding

Miami's healthy starting five is substantially more settled than its reserve ordering: Paul, Proctor, Brewer, Savaiinaea, and Jackson lead, with Salyer the first multi-position challenger and Heck the leading veteran tackle response. The starting ceiling is young and physical; the reserve floor is highly position-dependent.

## Evidence

## Starting-unit profiles

### LT Patrick Paul

- **Role:** established starter and blind-side projection.
- **Evidence:** 2024 second-round pick; three starts as a rookie and all 17 starts in 2025. Miami's 2026 preview identifies strength, pass protection, and power off the ball as the traits that emerged during his first full starting season.
- **Strength hypothesis:** length and power can provide a stable edge in both the run game and conventional pass sets.
- **Risk:** one strong season is not yet a durable multi-year baseline. Track speed-to-power, inside counters, penalties, and how often the scheme gives him chip help.
- **2026 test:** whether Miami can leave Paul isolated while sending the back and tight end into routes.

### LG Kadyn Proctor

- **Role:** projected rookie starter after playing left tackle at Alabama.
- **Evidence:** No. 12 overall pick, consensus All-American, listed by Miami at 352 pounds, and used at left guard through the offseason and camp.
- **Strength hypothesis:** displacement power plus enough movement ability to execute Miami's wide and inside run menu.
- **Risks:** NFL guard footwork, reduced reaction space against interior rushers, twist recognition, hand timing, conditioning, and learning the position while learning the offense.
- **2026 test:** whether his physical advantage survives fast interior games and passing downs without repeated help.

### C Aaron Brewer

- **Role:** communication hub, best established lineman, and lead movement blocker.
- **Evidence:** second-team All-Pro in 2025 and extended through 2029. Teammates and coaches have described him as the organizer aligning the five-man unit.
- **Strength hypothesis:** range in space, combination-block timing, and ability to lead or reach linebackers create flexibility across zone and gap concepts.
- **Risk:** the line is unusually dependent on him. Any missed time would remove both its best-known blocker and its primary organizer.
- **2026 test:** clean exchanges with both guards against fronts, stunts, and pressure rotation.

### RG Jonah Savaiinaea

- **Role:** projected second-year starter after moving from left guard to the right side.
- **Evidence:** No. 37 pick in 2025 and starter for all 17 games. Aaron Brewer has said he looks more comfortable on the right, and Zach Sieler cited improved inside-hand placement and unit cohesion in camp.
- **Strength hypothesis:** college right-side familiarity, size, and athleticism support a rebound.
- **Risk:** public PFF figures republished by The Phinsider credit him with 45 pressures and eight sacks allowed plus a 28.4 overall grade in 2025. Those vendor figures are not independently reproduced here, but the scale of the reported struggle makes improvement—not mere position reassignment—the burden of proof.
- **2026 test:** anchor, hand placement, recognition, and whether he can avoid becoming the target of interior pressure packages.

### RT Austin Jackson

- **Role:** incumbent right tackle and experienced run-game contributor.
- **Evidence:** Miami describes him as a difference-maker in the run game. He has also missed games in each of the past two seasons and was day-to-day during the first week of August camp.
- **Strength hypothesis:** a healthy Jackson completes a physically strong starting five and preserves the full run menu.
- **Risk:** availability and the step down to replacement-level tackle play.
- **2026 test:** health, isolated edge protection, and whether his return restores the stable five-man combination.

## Competition hierarchy

1. **Starting five:** Paul–Proctor–Brewer–Savaiinaea–Jackson if healthy.
2. **First challenger:** Salyer, particularly at right guard and as a multi-position sixth lineman.
3. **Veteran tackle response:** Heck.
4. **Interior development:** Priebe, Meyer, Campbell, and Bonifas.
5. **Developmental projects:** Cline, Ayedze, Cox, and defensive-line convert Ester.

The hierarchy is deliberately provisional. Miami's public depth chart was incomplete at verification, and camp access rules limit unit-order reporting. Preseason snaps outrank labels.

## Fantasy implication

Brewer, Paul, and Jackson carry the largest offense-wide replacement costs. A guard change to Salyer is more containable; a tackle injury can reduce route releases, while a Brewer absence can affect both run efficiency and protection communication.
