# Run-blocking assessment

## Finding

Run blocking is the line's clearest path to becoming a strength. Miami has the size to displace defenders, Brewer's movement ability at center, tackles with run-game traits, and a coaching staff explicitly testing outside zone, inside zone, and gap schemes. The claim remains a projection until the unit sustains execution against live fronts.

## Evidence

The personnel traits, coach-stated concept range, and early camp observations support the concept-fit analysis. They do not yet provide a representative efficiency sample.

## Concept-fit matrix

| Concept family | Personnel fit | Potential advantage | Main failure mode | Evidence needed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Outside zone | favorable | Brewer's range; Paul/Proctor combination movement; Achane's speed | backside penetration or guards failing to reach second level | yards before contact, edge capture, cutback lane, backside loss rate |
| Inside zone | favorable but unproven | mass at guard; Brewer's combination timing; decisive backs | interior leakage before the back reaches the read | displacement, double-team exits, run result by interior gap |
| Duo | promising | large guards can generate vertical movement without asking every blocker to reach | linebackers staying clean when doubles release late | yards before contact, linebacker contact depth, short-yardage conversion |
| Power/counter | promising | Proctor/Savaiinaea size; potential pulling options; Willis can hold backside pursuit | slow pull timing or penetration disrupting the path | puller success, kick-out quality, back timing, negative-run rate |
| Pin-pull/perimeter | plausible | Brewer's mobility and athletic tackles can get into space | perimeter congestion and missed blocks on defensive backs | lead-block win rate and explosive-run creation |
| QB-read/option | favorable ecosystem fit | Willis forces an unblocked defender to account for the keeper | mesh/assignment error or excessive quarterback exposure | read outcome, conflict defender behavior, RB efficiency with/without keep |

## Strength hypotheses

- The Paul–Proctor left side becomes Miami's most reliable combination for designed runs.
- Brewer gives Miami more second-level and perimeter range than the rest of the line's size would suggest.
- Proctor's transition is easier to absorb in the run game than in pass protection because his mass and downhill power translate immediately.
- Returning Savaiinaea to the right side reduces assignment and footwork friction.
- Willis's keeper threat delays backside pursuit and protects cutback lanes for Achane.
- A more varied run menu reduces dependence on pure speed-to-the-edge outcomes.

## Weaknesses to test

- **Large does not automatically mean coordinated.** Combination blocks fail if the linemen do not overtake and release on time.
- **Second-level balance and angles.** Size can become a liability when defenders force blockers to redirect in space.
- **Interior penetration.** One guard losing immediately can erase the design before Achane's vision or speed matters.
- **Short-yardage proof.** General rushing efficiency does not establish the ability to create one yard in condensed fronts.
- **Direction predictability.** A successful left-side identity becomes easier to defend if formation or personnel reveals it.
- **Quarterback redistribution.** Better box math can help Achane while Willis simultaneously removes carries or goal-line chances.

## Early evidence

Miami's official camp notebook credited Paul and Proctor with a combination block on a long Ollie Gordon run and noted interior lanes created by Brewer, Proctor, and Savaiinaea. The official minicamp notebook also cited consecutive lanes created by Paul and Proctor. These are positive observations, but team-authored camp reporting is directional evidence rather than an efficiency sample.

## Charting plan

For each competitive first-unit run, record:

- concept family and personnel grouping;
- shotgun or under center;
- run direction and intended gap;
- motion and quarterback-read component;
- box count and defensive front;
- first contact location relative to the line of scrimmage;
- yards before contact and final yards;
- primary block win/loss and whether a combination reached the linebacker;
- negative run, short-yardage conversion, explosive run, or touchdown;
- whether the same look produced a pass elsewhere in the game.

Do not infer line performance from final rushing yards alone. Separate blocked yardage, back-created yardage, quarterback conflict, and defensive error.

## Fantasy implication

- Better yards before contact raise Achane's weekly rushing floor and reduce dependence on explosive plays.
- Successful inside concepts make Achane less formation- and direction-dependent and support sustainable volume.
- Duo and gap success improve the viability of Ollie Gordon or another larger back in short yardage, which could cap Achane's touchdown share.
- Designed quarterback runs can improve the total rushing ecosystem while transferring some high-value carries from the backs to Willis.
