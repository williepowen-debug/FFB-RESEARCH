# Miami Dolphins 2026 offensive line

## Finding

Miami has assembled a large, young line with a credible path to becoming an above-average run-blocking unit. Aaron Brewer is the established centerpiece, Patrick Paul supplied a full season of left-tackle continuity in 2025, and first-round rookie Kadyn Proctor gives the left side unusual mass and movement ability. The ceiling is real.

The floor is less secure. Proctor is learning NFL guard after playing left tackle in college. Jonah Savaiinaea is changing sides after a difficult rookie season. Austin Jackson has missed games in consecutive seasons. The group is also learning the details of Bobby Slowik's protection system while supporting a new starting quarterback whose mobility can both solve and conceal blocking failures.

**Preseason grade: high-variance, with run blocking ahead of pass protection.** The most defensible range is below average if injuries force multiple reserves into the lineup, approximately league average if the five-man combination stabilizes, and potentially above average if the young guards develop quickly.

## Evidence

The projected lineup, player histories, coach statements, current roster, and availability reports establish the factual baseline summarized below. Claims about unit rank and future execution remain projections.

## Projected opening five

| Position | Projected starter | Established evidence | Central question |
|---|---|---|---|
| LT | Patrick Paul | Started all 17 games in 2025; team reporting describes strength, power, and improved pass protection | Can he repeat that performance when isolated against top edge speed? |
| LG | Kadyn Proctor | No. 12 pick; consensus All-American at Alabama; working at guard throughout Miami's offseason | How quickly do his footwork, hand timing, and assignment recognition translate inside? |
| C | Aaron Brewer | Second-team All-Pro in 2025; extended through 2029; line leader and movement blocker | Can he keep both young guards synchronized against fronts and games? |
| RG | Jonah Savaiinaea | Started all 17 games as a rookie; returned to the right side, where he has prior experience | Does the side change produce a real second-year jump after major 2025 struggles? |
| RT | Austin Jackson | Experienced starter and positive run-game contributor when available | Can he stay healthy, and can Miami protect the offense if he cannot? |

This is a projection, not a settled official depth chart. The stable camp combination supports it, but preseason deployment and Jackson's availability remain decisive.

## Strengths

1. **Run-concept flexibility.** Jeff Hafley has said the personnel can execute outside zone, inside zone, and gap schemes. That allows Miami to seek player advantages without changing personnel and telegraphing intent.
2. **Left-side physical ceiling.** Paul and Proctor combine length, mass, and movement skills. Their early camp combination block on a long Ollie Gordon run is useful directional evidence, though not proof of regular-season quality.
3. **Center play and leadership.** Brewer is the unit's best established player and the communication hub between two young guards.
4. **Young-player upside.** Paul, Proctor, and Savaiinaea can improve together rather than merely preserve a veteran baseline.
5. **Quarterback-created numbers.** Malik Willis's designed runs, keepers, boots, and scrambles can hold backside defenders and change run-fit math.
6. **Experienced first-wave depth.** Jamaree Salyer has played across four line positions, while Charlie Heck has 67 career appearances.

## Weaknesses and failure points

1. **Interior pass-protection uncertainty.** Both projected guards face a meaningful transition, and Savaiinaea's 2025 performance cannot be dismissed simply because he is changing sides.
2. **Right-tackle availability.** Jackson has missed time in consecutive seasons. His absence would alter protection calls, route releases, and possibly the usable passing menu.
3. **Center dependency.** Brewer is both the best blocker and the organizer. Depth behind him is much less proven.
4. **Simultaneous change.** New guards, a new starting quarterback, and new coaching terminology create compounding communication risk even if each component is individually viable.
5. **Unproven reserve floor.** Beyond Salyer and Heck, the alternatives are largely developmental or inexperienced.
6. **Mobility can hide the diagnosis.** A Willis escape can produce a positive fantasy play after a protection loss. The result does not mean the line won the rep.

## Three variables that determine the outcome

1. **Young-guard development:** Proctor's transition and Savaiinaea's rebound.
2. **Five-man availability:** especially Jackson and Brewer.
3. **Protection communication:** identification and exchange against interior games, pressure, and simulated pressure.

## Fantasy implication

- **De'Von Achane:** the line can raise rushing sustainability and create more viable inside carries, but goal-line share still depends on backfield and quarterback usage.
- **Malik Willis:** better protection improves passing volume and pass-catcher value; poor protection may leave his own fantasy rushing floor intact while damaging the offense.
- **Wide receivers:** intermediate and deep routes are the first casualties if Miami must shorten concepts or devote tight ends and backs to protection.
- **Tight ends and backs:** chip and stay-in assignments must be measured because nominal snap share can overstate route opportunity.
