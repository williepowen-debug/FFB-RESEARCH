# Finding: Green Bay proved tailored efficiency, not complete starting-quarterback control

## Scope

- Team/player: Miami Dolphins; Malik Willis
- Area: Green Bay passing development, scheme protection, transferable skills, unresolved starter duties, and Miami test plan
- Sample: 2024–25 Green Bay regular seasons
- Status: active
- Last verified: 2026-08-05

## Finding

Green Bay proved that Willis can execute a tailored NFL offense at an exceptionally high level and supplied credible evidence of passing development. It did not prove that he can independently manage every component of a conventional, high-volume passing operation.

Three improvements are credible and potentially transferable:

1. Willis repeatedly converted downfield throws, including difficult outside-the-numbers attempts.
2. He materially improved ball security and situational discipline from his Tennessee baseline.
3. His efficiency survived both prepared starts and unplanned injury-replacement appearances.

The boundary is equally important. Matt LaFleur paired Willis with a strong rushing environment, concentrated throws at the short and deep edges of the field, used option runs heavily in his starts, and reduced normal line-of-scrimmage responsibilities in the 2025 Baltimore start. Green Bay did not establish full-field progression control, intermediate-middle consistency, protection ownership, or efficiency at 30–35 attempts.

## Evidence

### Verified statistical record

Official NFL game logs support the following 2024–25 Green Bay regular-season aggregate:

- 70 completions on 89 attempts for 972 yards, six touchdowns, and zero interceptions.
- 42 rushes for 261 yards and three touchdowns.
- 11 sacks on 100 pass attempts plus sacks, an 11.0% descriptive sack rate.
- Three recorded fumbles, two lost.
- Three starts: Indianapolis and Tennessee in 2024, and Baltimore in 2025.

The 89 attempts include low-volume cameos beyond the six meaningful appearances below. The 11.0% calculation is a transparent small-sample description, not a pressure-adjusted or predictive sack metric.

| Appearance | Passing | Rushing | Verified context and interpretation |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Indianapolis, 2024 | 12/14, 122 yards, 1 TD | 6 carries, 41 yards | Prepared start. Green Bay ran 53 times for 261 yards; Willis was not sacked. This proves clean execution inside an extreme run-first plan, not dropback volume. |
| Tennessee, 2024 | 13/19, 202 yards, 1 TD | 6 carries, 73 yards, 1 TD | Prepared start. He created explosives, converted third downs, protected the ball, and added zone-read value; he also took three sacks. |
| Jacksonville, 2024 | 4/5, 56 yards, 1 TD | 4 carries, 23 yards | Injury replacement. The official recap records a 51-yard play-action completion to Jayden Reed that created the winning field-goal opportunity; he also took one sack. |
| Chicago, 2024 | 10/13, 136 yards | 1 carry, 3 yards | Relief appearance. Passing efficiency held, but he took three sacks and lost a strip-sack fumble. |
| Chicago, 2025 | 9/11, 121 yards, 1 TD | 10 carries, 44 yards | Injury replacement. Strong passing and rushing production survived a high-leverage situation; he took two sacks and accepted responsibility for a cadence change that contributed to the failed overtime exchange. |
| Baltimore, 2025 | 18/21, 288 yards, 1 TD | 9 carries, 60 yards, 2 TDs | Prepared start and the strongest dropback-development evidence. He attacked outside coverage and produced from more pure dropback concepts, but the pre-snap operation and concept menu remained pared back. |

The remaining regular-season work was four brief appearances: one incompletion and one sack against Philadelphia in 2024; 1-of-2 for 34 yards with three rushes for minus-2 yards against New Orleans in 2024; 2-of-2 for six yards with a 16-yard rush against the Giants in 2025; and 1-of-1 for seven yards with two rushes for three yards against Minnesota in 2025. These snaps are included in the aggregate but are too limited to carry independent evaluative weight.

### What demonstrably improved

#### 1. Downfield placement and outside accuracy

The evidence is stronger than a generic arm-talent claim. CBS Sports, using TruMedia charting, reported that Willis completed 13 of 16 throws traveling at least 20 yards in the air for 456 yards and a 158.3 rating across his two Green Bay seasons. The Baltimore film review independently described high-difficulty outside completions when Baltimore crowded the middle.

That charting is secondary rather than an official NFL split, so it should be treated as a sourced analytical result. If correct, those 16 throws produced 46.9% of Willis's 972 Green Bay passing yards. The result supports real downfield accuracy while also exposing concentration risk: full-season efficiency should not assume that an 81.3% deep-completion rate or that share of yardage will persist.

#### 2. Ball security and situational discipline

Willis threw no interceptions in 89 Green Bay attempts after throwing three on 66 attempts during his 2022–23 Tennessee regular-season sample. LaFleur explicitly praised his avoidance of harm after the Tennessee win. This is meaningful directional improvement.

It is not proof of a stable zero-interception baseline. The Green Bay sample is small, the pass menu was selective, and three fumbles with two lost show that ball security while extending or operating late remains an active risk.

#### 3. Prepared-start and relief execution

Willis won both 2024 starts after arriving shortly before the season, then produced efficiently when Jordan Love left the Jacksonville and Chicago games. In 2025 he again entered unexpectedly against Chicago before preparing to start against Baltimore. The repeated ability to enter different game states without a collapse is transferable evidence of poise, preparation, and command of an assigned package.

### What LaFleur simplified or protected

#### Run and option leverage

CBS Sports reported that across Willis's three Green Bay starts the offense used:

- a 63% rush rate, compared with 48% in Jordan Love starts over the same two seasons;
- option runs on 27% of plays, compared with 7% with Love;
- shotgun on 85% of plays; and
- motion on 75% of plays.

These are source-charted tendencies, not official NFL categories. They show that LaFleur changed the offense around Willis. The Indianapolis plan is the clearest official example: 53 rushes against only 14 Willis attempts.

The aggregate motion figure does not conflict with the Baltimore film report. It describes all three starts; the Baltimore-specific report found that Green Bay sharply reduced motion in that game.

#### A selective throwing geography

CBS/TruMedia reported that only 30% of Willis's Green Bay attempts went over the middle, 20% went over the middle from 0–20 air yards, and 4% went to the intermediate middle from 11–20 air yards. His distribution favored flats, screens, outs, and deep sideline throws.

That structure sensibly paired his arm strength with clearer leverage. It also means the results do not establish that Willis can repeatedly throw with anticipation and touch into layered middle-field coverage.

#### Reduced pre-snap and concept burden against Baltimore

The Acme Packing Company film review reported that Green Bay removed nearly all of the protection-setting and checking control normally given to Love. The reviewer observed no Willis checks and one failed `Can` call, while motion at the snap fell to 14.3% and total motion to 42.9%, both well below Green Bay's season rates.

The same review found that 10 Baltimore pass calls, 38.5% of the passing menu, used Stick or Dagger. This does not erase the quality of Willis's throws: he still had to diagnose leverage and deliver accurately. It narrows what the game proved. He excelled from a more static picture and concentrated concept family; he was not shown running Love's full pre-snap operation.

### Transferable skills versus unproven duties

| Area | Green Bay evidence | Miami projection boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Deep outside accuracy | Multiple games and secondary charting support high-end placement | likely transferable, but extreme efficiency must regress over larger volume |
| Designed-run and option value | Productive keepers, scrambles, and red-zone runs | directly transferable if Slowik creates credible run conflict |
| Play-action and movement throws | Efficient explosives off run action and changed launch points | transferable as a core tool; problematic if it becomes the only functional path |
| Ball avoidance | Zero interceptions and repeated game-management discipline | encouraging, not established at starter volume |
| Relief-game operation | Efficient Jacksonville and Chicago entries | credible evidence of preparation and composure |
| Intermediate middle | Very low usage, especially 11–20 yards | unproven |
| Full-field progression | Concentrated concepts and defined leverage reads | unproven after the first concept is removed |
| Protection control | Baltimore checks and adjustments were reportedly stripped down | unproven |
| High-volume dropback offense | No Green Bay game reached 22 pass attempts | unproven at 30–35 attempts |
| Negative-play avoidance | 11 sacks on 100 attempts plus sacks; three fumbles | remains a material concern |
| Defensive counter-adjustment | Small, intermittent sample allowed opponent-specific plans | unproven after defenses remove preferred throws or change pressure |

## Miami test plan

This audit supplies the historical baseline for the companion [`slowik-willis-protection.md`](slowik-willis-protection.md) study. That record defines the Brewer–Willis protection interface and the difference between mitigation and actual protection improvement.

### Training camp

- Identify who declares the front, sets the protection point, and owns late changes: Willis, Aaron Brewer, or the sideline.
- Track Willis's response when the initial outside or deep read is capped: second progression, outlet, reset, scramble, throwaway, or sack.
- Chart competitive attempts by location, especially the 11–20-yard middle.
- Separate designed movement from escapes caused by a failed pocket.
- Test the same concept against changing pressure and coverage presentations rather than scoring only the first successful rep.

### Preseason

- Give Willis representative five-man-protection snaps with backs and tight ends releasing.
- Include obvious-pass downs, simulated pressure, late rotation, and mugged-linebacker looks.
- Preserve identifiable hot routes and checkdowns, then grade whether Willis uses them.
- Record route participation alongside protection result; a completion with two eligible receivers held in is not evidence that the full route menu survived.
- Create at least one extended drive or half in which efficiency cannot depend on a dominant rushing script.

### Regular season

- Review the first two starts for middle-field usage, sack rate, fumbles, and protection ownership.
- Treat the first game with at least 30 pass attempts as a distinct volume test.
- Compare first- and second-half answers after opponents alter pressure or remove deep/outside throws.
- Reassess the offense after 100 Miami attempts; do not combine a tiny early sample with Green Bay as if the environments were identical.
- Measure whether Slowik's run/play-action sequencing creates efficient conventional dropbacks, not merely quarterback rushing production.

## Fantasy implication

Willis's personal scoring and the health of Miami's passing offense must be evaluated separately.

- Designed runs and scrambles can keep Willis fantasy-viable while failed protection reduces pass attempts and scoring opportunities for teammates.
- A scramble can replace an available De'Von Achane outlet rather than create value for him.
- Chips, delayed releases, and max protection can improve a play's survival while reducing routes for backs and tight ends.
- Explosive outside throws create weekly ceiling for Willis and receivers, but dependence on low-frequency plays increases volatility.
- If five-man protection fails, Miami may preserve Willis's floor through movement and rushing while losing the conventional volume needed for stable receiver production.

The correct positive signal is not merely a strong Willis fantasy score. It is a strong score accompanied by preserved eligible routes, used outlets, functional intermediate concepts, manageable negative plays, and answers after the defense changes the picture.

## Registered hypotheses

New audit-specific hypotheses are recorded in [`hypotheses.csv`](hypotheses.csv):

5. His fumble rate does not rise with expanded dropback and scramble volume.

## Sourcing limitations

- NFL game logs establish outcomes but not read structure, protection ownership, air-yard location, or whether a rush was designed.
- The deep-throw, pass-location, start-level tendency, and pressure-to-sack figures originate from CBS Sports/TruMedia charting and could not be independently reconstructed from official NFL tables.
- The Baltimore protection-check, concept, and motion findings are one analyst's film charting. They are reported evidence, not an official team disclosure of Willis's assignments.
- Public broadcast and recap evidence cannot reliably distinguish every quarterback decision from a coached rule. Miami must treat the unresolved duties as tests rather than assumed deficiencies.

## Assessment

- Confidence: medium
- Reason: official results strongly establish exceptional small-sample efficiency and repeated situational execution; the scheme and throw-location boundaries rely partly on reputable but secondary charting.
- Fact/inference boundary: official game statistics and team recaps are verified facts. CBS/TruMedia rates and the Acme film chart are attributed reported evidence. Transfer to Miami, regression risk, and the proposed responsibility split are analytical inferences or open questions.
- What would invalidate this: verified charting materially changes the Green Bay rates, or Miami volume shows that Willis can sustain intermediate-middle, progression, protection, and negative-play performance beyond the identified boundaries.
- Next review: after the first preseason game; after the first two regular-season starts; after the first 30-attempt game; and after 100 Miami pass attempts.
