# Finding: the center-quarterback protection interface is Miami's decisive passing-game variable

## Scope

- Team/player: Miami Dolphins; Bobby Slowik; Malik Willis; Aaron Brewer
- Area: protection design, pressure response, quarterback responsibility, and fantasy transmission
- Season/week: 2026 preseason baseline
- Status: active
- Last verified: 2026-08-05

## Finding

Miami's scheme has credible tools to reduce the offensive line's exposure, but there is not yet evidence that those tools can solve its conventional pass-protection risk. The most likely preseason baseline is that Slowik's run/play-action sequencing and Willis's mobility improve neutral-down efficiency while obvious passing situations remain vulnerable to interior games, simulated pressure, and identification errors.

The decisive variable is the **center-quarterback protection interface**: how Aaron Brewer and Willis divide front identification, protection changes, hot answers, and late defensive movement. Brewer can stabilize a young interior, but Willis must still prove that he can preserve the called progression and outlet rather than replacing a valid answer with an early escape.

This creates an important false signal for fantasy analysis: **Willis can score well while Miami's passing ecosystem remains unhealthy.** Scrambles can convert failed protection into quarterback points while reducing attempts, designed running-back targets, and receiver route value.

## Evidence

### Historical Slowik evidence

- Slowik's 2023 Houston offense established a credible play-action and sequencing ceiling, but the 2024 offense did not protect that success when its line and answers deteriorated.
- Houston allowed 52 regular-season sacks in 2024, second most in the league, and a 38.6% pressure rate, fourth highest. The Houston Chronicle also identified stunts, simulated pressures, and free rushers as recurring failures.
- Houston used 11 offensive-line combinations for at least 10 snaps, so the result cannot be assigned to scheme alone. The relevant lesson is narrower: Slowik's offense did not consistently overcome protection instability once defenses found repeatable pressure answers.

### Historical Willis evidence

- Across 2024-25 in Green Bay, Willis completed 70 of 89 passes for 972 yards, six touchdowns, and no interceptions. That is meaningful development evidence but only three starts and 89 attempts.
- Willis took 11 sacks on those 89 attempts. Mobility improved his playmaking ceiling without eliminating negative passing plays.
- Green Bay's December 2025 Baltimore plan demonstrated that Willis could produce from a pared-back dropback menu, but the film review found that protection checks, motion, and line-of-scrimmage control were substantially reduced relative to Jordan Love's normal operation.
- The same review described a static structure that made the initial picture easier to process. Miami therefore cannot assume that Green Bay's efficiency sample proves full protection-management capacity.

### Current Miami evidence

- Slowik said Miami is intentionally coaching Willis to remain in the progression and deliver the throw when the play is still alive rather than treating every pressure look as permission to scramble.
- Miami has reported encouraging isolated plays, including a downfield completion against an all-out blitz and another explosive after Willis escaped pressure. Those are directional camp observations, not a representative protection sample.
- Brewer is Miami's established line organizer between two young guards. His role makes any absence both a blocking downgrade and a possible protection-communication downgrade.

## Protection operating model to test

| Layer | Primary question | Healthy evidence | Failure evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Identification | Who sets the point and recognizes the pressure presentation? | Brewer and Willis consistently account for the most dangerous threats | free rushers from repeated alignment or rotation patterns |
| Adjustment | Who can change the protection after late movement? | clean response to mugged linebackers, safety rotation, and overloads | correct pre-snap answer becomes wrong after the defense shifts |
| Hot answer | Does the concept give Willis an immediate throw against numbers pressure? | ball replaces blitz; receiver and quarterback read the same leverage | Willis holds, scrambles, or takes a sack with an available answer |
| Five-man exchange | Can the line pass off stunts and games? | adjacent blockers stay square and exchange without two taking one | interior leakage, crossed feet, or an untouched looper |
| Route release | How many eligible receivers enter the pattern? | backs and tight ends release while protection remains functional | routine chips, delayed releases, or max protection shrink the route menu |
| Quarterback behavior | Does Willis use the protection as designed? | climbs, resets, finds outlet, and scrambles only after structure fails | abandons clean pockets or bypasses designed answers |
| Coaching counter | Does Slowik respond after defenses change tactics? | second-half protection and concept adjustments restore answers | the same pressure family creates repeated losses |

## Mitigation is not the same as protection improvement

Miami can create functional offense through play action, boots, screens, quick game, max protection, and designed quarterback movement. Each tool can be valuable. None, by itself, proves that the line can sustain conventional intermediate and deep concepts.

Chart every competitive pass in three layers:

1. **Design:** did the call reduce, redirect, or intentionally leave the rush unblocked?
2. **Protection:** did assigned blockers identify, communicate, and win?
3. **Quarterback:** did Willis use the called progression, hot answer, outlet, and pocket correctly?

A completion or scramble does not erase a protection loss. A sack does not automatically make the line responsible. A lower sack rate accompanied by fewer eligible routes, shorter concepts, or more quarterback runs may improve Willis's fantasy floor while reducing the rest of the offense's ceiling.

## Fantasy implication

- **Malik Willis:** rushing provides a strong independent scoring channel, but sack rate and passing volume can remain volatile. Evaluate his fantasy production separately from passing-game health.
- **De'Von Achane:** pressure does not automatically create receiving value. Willis may scramble instead of throwing the designed outlet, and Achane may also be retained in protection.
- **Wide receivers:** the central test is whether five-man protection preserves intermediate and deep routes. Splash plays from movement or scramble drills do not establish a stable target environment.
- **Tight ends:** route participation matters more than raw snaps. Chips, delayed releases, and stay-in assignments can materially reduce fantasy opportunity.
- **Miami offense:** a successful mitigation package can sustain weekly scoring, but the offense becomes fragile when forced into obvious dropback situations or when opponents change the pressure picture after halftime.

## Registered hypotheses

1. Brewer will remain the primary protection organizer.
2. Miami will give Willis more pre-snap responsibility than Green Bay did in his limited starts.
3. Miami will provide identifiable hot answers against overload and simulated pressure.
4. Movement passes will mitigate pressure without becoming the majority of successful passing plays.
5. Willis will preserve designed outlets rather than replacing them with scrambles.
6. Five-man protection will sustain receiver and tight-end route participation.
7. Slowik will produce effective counters when opponents change pressure tactics after halftime.

## Assessment

- Confidence: medium
- Reason: Houston's protection breakdowns, Willis's Green Bay results, and Miami's stated coaching emphasis establish the risk and intended response; Miami has not yet supplied representative competitive snaps.
- Fact/inference boundary: historical statistics, reported protection failures, Green Bay's reduced operation, and Slowik's statements are evidence. The division of responsibility between Brewer and Willis and the expected 2026 outcome are hypotheses.
- What would invalidate this: Willis demonstrates full protection control with consistently correct hot answers, five-man protection survives varied pressure without route-menu contraction, or Miami assigns protection responsibility in a materially different way.
- Next review: first preseason game with chartable first-unit pass attempts; then each of the first two regular-season games, including second-half counters.
