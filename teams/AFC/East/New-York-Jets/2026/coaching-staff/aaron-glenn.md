# Aaron Glenn — New York Jets

- Role: Head coach; reported defensive play-caller
- Joined team: 2025 (from Detroit's defensive coordinator role)
- Last verified: 2026-08-13

## Responsibilities

Glenn enters his second season and has taken a more hands-on approach after a difficult 2025, overhauling the staff — roughly 12 of the 22 coaches from his initial group are gone. He controls whole-team standards and, per reporting, calls the defense himself, with new defensive coordinator Brian Duker handling the unit's other responsibilities. On offense, he hired veteran coordinator Frank Reich to run a new system. General manager Darren Mougey remains.

This is a mid-tenure retool: same head coach and general manager, but a new offensive coordinator, a new quarterback (Geno Smith by trade), and a roster reshaped by trading away cornerstone cornerback Sauce Gardner.

## Scheme and tendencies

Glenn's defensive background is aggressive, coverage-and-pressure-oriented football rooted in his Detroit tenure. With him calling the defense, the reasonable prior is a physical, multiple defense, but the exact fronts and pressure rates for a reworked personnel group must be charted. On offense, the identity now flows through Reich, not Glenn.

## Personnel usage

The defensive personnel changed materially: Gardner and edge Jermaine Johnson were traded out; safety Minkah Fitzpatrick and lineman T'Vondre Sweat were traded in, and edge David Bailey was drafted second overall. Glenn's deployment of Fitzpatrick, the rebuilt cornerback group, and the rookie edge are the key early indicators. On offense, his main personnel decision was set by acquiring Geno Smith as the starter over the failed 2025 quarterback plan.

## Pace and situational decisions

A difficult 2025 and a staff overhaul make game management a live watchpoint. Track fourth-down and timeout decisions, and how Glenn balances defensive play-calling with head-coaching duties in year two.

## Fantasy implications

- The defense is a retool, not a rebuild-from-nothing: a Fitzpatrick-anchored back end and a high-pick edge rusher can support D/ST value if the corner group holds up after the Gardner trade.
- The offense's fantasy outlook now depends on Reich and Geno Smith, not Glenn.
- Roster transition raises variance across the board; establish roles from preseason evidence.
