# Denver Broncos — 2026 Contender Continuity and Waddle Addition

- Conference/division: AFC West
- Last verified: 2026-08-17
- Scope: changes from the 2025 team to the 2026 preseason roster and staff
- Build tier: Core

Denver is a contender-continuity module with one major offensive addition and one important play-calling change. Sean Payton remains head coach under a new deal through 2030, Davis Webb becomes offensive coordinator and play-caller, and Vance Joseph returns to lead an elite defense. Bo Nix returns from an ankle injury with Jaylen Waddle added to a pass-catching group that already included Courtland Sutton, Marvin Mims Jr., and Evan Engram. The run game retains J.K. Dobbins and RJ Harvey while adding Jonah Coleman.

## Fantasy-relevant snapshot

### Confirmed baseline

- Payton remains head coach and Webb is the new offensive coordinator/play-caller.
- Nix returned to practice after the ankle injury that ended his 2025 postseason.
- Denver traded premium 2026 picks to acquire Waddle and a fourth-rounder from Miami.
- Dobbins re-signed after rushing for 772 yards at 5.0 yards per carry in 10 games.
- Harvey returns after 12 rookie touchdowns and nearly 900 scrimmage yards; Coleman joins as a fourth-round rookie.
- Bolles and Meinerz anchor a line coming off first-team All-Pro seasons.
- Joseph returns after Denver led the NFL in sacks in consecutive seasons.
- Surtain, Hufanga, Jones, Moss, McMillian, and Barron headline a strong secondary.

### Working fantasy hypotheses

- Nix remains a fantasy riser if his mobility is intact and Webb's play-calling preserves Payton's efficiency structure.
- Waddle increases explosive passing and target competition at the same time.
- Dobbins starts as the lead back, but Harvey and Coleman can create weekly touch volatility.
- Denver's offensive line raises the floor for both Nix and the rushing game.
- The defense remains a difficult matchup, with turnover growth determining DST ceiling.

## Offensive identity

### Confirmed changes

The offensive structure remains Payton-designed but Webb-called. Denver added Waddle, retained Dobbins, drafted Coleman and Casey, and kept most of its tight-end depth. The roster supports a balanced offense that can stress defenses horizontally and vertically while leaning on an elite offensive-line core.

### Questions to resolve

- Does Nix's ankle return preserve full mobility and off-platform play?
- How does Webb distribute targets among Waddle, Sutton, Mims, Engram, and the tight ends?
- Does Dobbins control high-value backfield work, or does Harvey/Coleman force a three-way rotation?
- Does Casey become active-day line depth immediately?

The evidence and invalidation tests are maintained in the [offensive research module](offense/README.md).

## Defensive matchup profile

### Confirmed changes

Denver's defense is more continuity than overhaul. The sack engine returns around Bonitto and Joseph, while Strnad is expected to take a larger inside-linebacker role after Greenlaw's release. The secondary remains one of the league's strongest groups, with Surtain and Hufanga as All-Pro anchors and Barron entering Year 2.

### Questions to resolve

- Can Denver lead or threaten the league in sacks for a third straight year?
- Does Strnad hold a full-time nickel role next to Singleton?
- Does Barron earn a larger role without disrupting McMillian/Moss/Hufanga/Jones alignments?
- Can the defense turn pressure into more takeaways?

Denver should begin as a matchup downgrade for most quarterbacks and receivers. The defense's fantasy ceiling depends on takeaways catching up to pressure. See the [defensive research module](defense/README.md).
