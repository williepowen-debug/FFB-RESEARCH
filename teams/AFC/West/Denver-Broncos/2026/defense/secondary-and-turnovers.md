# Finding: Denver's secondary is elite, but turnover growth is the next test

## Scope

- Team/player: Denver Broncos secondary
- Area: coverage matchup quality and takeaway production
- Season/week: 2026 preseason baseline
- Status: active
- Last verified: 2026-08-17

## Finding

Denver's secondary is a confirmed strength. Surtain remains the cornerstone, joined by Moss, McMillian, Hufanga, Jones, and second-year corner Barron. Broncos coverage credited the 2025 group with allowing the seventh-fewest passing yards, the fewest net passing yards per attempt, and a league-high five games without allowing a touchdown.

The improvement point is takeaways. Denver recorded 10 interceptions and 14 total regular-season takeaways, then generated important playoff interceptions. Joseph and Payton have both pointed to turnover creation as a goal. That matters for fantasy because Denver is already suppressing efficiency; more takeaways would raise DST ceiling.

## Fantasy implication

- Perimeter receivers facing Denver should be downgraded unless volume or alignment avoids Surtain.
- Slot and secondary receiver projections require McMillian/Barron alignment charting.
- Denver DST has elite-pressure upside and can rise further if interception production catches up.
- Safety depth behind Hufanga/Jones is a monitoring item after Locke signed with Dallas.

## Evidence

- The Broncos' defensive backfield includes Surtain, Moss, McMillian, Barron, Hufanga, and Jones.
- Denver allowed 187.2 passing yards per game and 4.8 net yards per pass attempt in 2025.
- The defense kept opponents out of the end zone in five games, the league's highest mark.
- Denver had 10 interceptions and 14 regular-season takeaways, with turnover growth named as a 2026 goal.
- Locke departed for Dallas, while Tycen Anderson and Miles Scott were added as depth competitors.
