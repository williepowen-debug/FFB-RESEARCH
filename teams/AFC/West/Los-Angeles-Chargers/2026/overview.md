# Los Angeles Chargers — 2026 Coordinator Reset and Line Rebuild

- Conference/division: AFC West
- Last verified: 2026-08-16
- Scope: changes from the 2025 team to the 2026 preseason roster and staff
- Build tier: Core plus Full-tier source, preseason, and offensive-line modules

Los Angeles retained Head Coach Jim Harbaugh after a second consecutive 11-win playoff season but changed both play-callers. Mike McDaniel replaces Greg Roman with authority over the offense, and Chris O'Leary returns to replace new Baltimore Head Coach Jesse Minter on defense. The roster response was concentrated: rebuild the offensive line and tight-end room around Justin Herbert, retain the defense's central communicators, and add first-round edge rusher Akheem Mesidor.

## Fantasy-relevant snapshot

### Confirmed baseline

- Jim Harbaugh remains head coach, but Mike McDaniel and Chris O'Leary are new coordinators and play-callers.
- Herbert returns after throwing for 3,727 yards and 26 touchdowns in 2025 while facing 54 sacks and a league-high 263 pressures.
- Both starting tackles return from major 2025 injuries. Tyler Biadasz and Cole Strange were added to the interior, and four offensive linemen were drafted.
- Omarion Hampton returns after 737 scrimmage yards and five touchdowns in nine rookie games. Kimani Vidal and Keaton Mitchell provide proven complementary production and speed.
- Ladd McConkey, Quentin Johnston, and Tre' Harris lead a younger receiver room after Keenan Allen's contract expired; fourth-round pick Brenen Thompson adds vertical speed.
- Oronde Gadsden returns after a 664-yard rookie season, joined by David Njoku and Charlie Kolar in a remade tight-end room.
- The defense retained Derwin James Jr., Khalil Mack, Teair Tart, Tuli Tuipulotu, and Daiyan Henley, while Mesidor replaces some of the pass-rush capacity lost with Odafe Oweh.

### Working fantasy hypotheses

- McDaniel can improve Herbert's efficiency by reducing pure dropbacks and marrying outside zone to play action, but passing volume and line cohesion determine the ceiling.
- Hampton leads carries and high-value backfield work while Vidal and Mitchell receive defined complementary packages.
- McConkey leads receiver routes and first-read targets, with Johnston and Harris as the primary perimeter players.
- Gadsden and Njoku split receiving routes while Kolar's blocking enables multiple-tight-end personnel; no single tight end is yet a safe volume projection.
- O'Leary preserves the defense's disguise foundation and keeps James near the ball while using Mesidor as a movable third edge.

## Offensive identity

### Confirmed changes

McDaniel is installing a new offense after Los Angeles parted with Roman. The intended structure uses outside zone, motion, play action, and quicker timing to place defenders in conflict and reduce Herbert's exposure to immediate pass rush. The personnel work supports that structure: a new center and projected right guard, four drafted linemen, fullback Alec Ingold, blocking tight end Kolar, and receiving tight end Njoku.

### Questions to resolve

- Which player wins left guard, and how quickly does the new starting five handle games and pressure?
- Does protection improve while five eligible receivers continue to release into routes?
- How much passing volume remains after the offense becomes more run-connected?
- Does Hampton control routes and goal-line work, or does the backfield rotate by situation?
- How concentrated are targets among McConkey, the perimeter receivers, Gadsden, and Njoku?

The evidence and invalidation tests are maintained in the [offensive research module](offense/README.md).

## Defensive matchup profile

### Confirmed changes

O'Leary replaces Minter after coordinating Western Michigan's defense in 2025 and coaching the Chargers safeties in 2024. The primary corner group and safety core return. The front retains Tuipulotu, Mack, Tart, Caldwell, and Eboigbe, adds Tomlinson and two drafted linemen, and replaces Oweh with first-round pick Mesidor.

### Questions to resolve

- How closely does O'Leary retain the prior disguise and simulated-pressure structure?
- Does Mesidor immediately earn third-edge and interior-rush snaps?
- How are veteran interior snaps divided among Tart, Tomlinson, Caldwell, and Eboigbe?
- Does rookie Genesis Smith earn subpackage work that moves James closer to the line?
- Can the returning corner trio preserve the defense's recent passing-game efficiency?

Los Angeles begins as a potentially difficult matchup rather than an automatic avoid. The coordinator's NFL sequencing and the rookie roles require preseason and early-season confirmation. See the [defensive research module](defense/README.md).

## Coaching and scheme

| Unit | 2025 | 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Head coach | Jim Harbaugh | Jim Harbaugh |
| Offense/play-caller | Greg Roman | Mike McDaniel |
| Defense/play-caller | Jesse Minter | Chris O'Leary |
| Special teams | Ryan Ficken | Ryan Ficken |

McDaniel has broad offensive authority and prior head-coaching experience. His staff includes several assistants from his Miami system plus retained passing-game coordinator/quarterbacks coach Shane Day. O'Leary is a new NFL play-caller but returns to familiar personnel and is supported by promoted pass-game coordinator Steve Clinkscale and run-game coordinator Mike Elston.

The complete staff and authority model are in the [coaching intelligence module](coaching-staff/README.md).

### Analytical projection

The offense should be treated as a true system transition with a credible efficiency ceiling and meaningful implementation risk. The defense should be treated as philosophically continuous but tactically unconfirmed. Both sides require charting rather than direct transfer of prior coordinator results.

## Special teams

Ryan Ficken returns for a fifth season as coordinator with assistant Chris Gould. Kicker Cameron Dicker and punter JK Scott return, while the long-snapper position was retained through Josh Harris. The August 16 unofficial chart lists Derius Davis first at both return positions, with Brenen Thompson second on punts and Keaton Mitchell second on kickoffs. Special teams remains scaffolded as a durable module; dated role changes belong in the [preseason workspace](preseason/README.md) and later in `weekly/2026/`.
