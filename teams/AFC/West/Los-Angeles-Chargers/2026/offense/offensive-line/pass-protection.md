# Pass-protection assessment

## Finding

Pass protection is the line's most important fantasy variable and its least resolved starting-unit outcome. The personnel ceiling is materially higher than the 2025 group that allowed 54 sacks and a league-high 263 pressures against Justin Herbert, but the unit must prove that healthy tackles and a rebuilt interior can protect conventional passing concepts—not only that McDaniel can avoid pressure through quick throws, movement and extra help.

The Houston opener was encouraging for reserve depth. Slaughter moved from center to guard, Branson Taylor played three positions, and Harbaugh praised the overall protection. Herbert and the projected five did not supply a meaningful sample, so no pressure-grade upgrade is warranted yet.

## Evidence

The 2025 pressure baseline, current personnel, health reports, coach statements and Houston reserve deployment establish the risk map. Competitive starting-unit pass sets are still required to measure the outcome.

## Risk map

| Area | Baseline | Risk | Decisive test |
|---|---|---|---|
| Left tackle | Slater has elite healthy-season evidence | workload response after patellar-tendon repair and recent flare-up | isolated first-unit sets over consecutive practices/games |
| Left guard | Awosika is first on the chart; Slaughter is ascending and position-flexible | unresolved winner, lateral range and twist exchanges | starting-defender passing downs beside Slater and Biadasz |
| Center | Biadasz brings experience, athleticism and A-gap anchor | new language and two new adjacent relationships | front identification and clean interior exchanges with Herbert |
| Right guard | Strange started for McDaniel in 2025 | system familiarity may not equal individual or unit performance | anchor, stunt exchange and ability to avoid help |
| Right tackle | Alt owns high-end talent and a healthy camp trend | 2025 ankle reinjury and limited recent game sample | normal full-game workload against starting edge talent |
| Quarterback/design | Herbert has arm talent; McDaniel can change launch point and timing | quick game can hide losses or suppress route depth | separate design, time to pressure and blocker responsibility |

## Protection questions

1. Does Herbert and Biadasz identify pressure consistently before the snap?
2. Can the left guard exchange twists with both Slater and Biadasz?
3. Can Slater and Alt survive without routine chips?
4. Does Strange's system familiarity improve the right-side operation?
5. Are backs and tight ends releasing into routes or being retained?
6. Does Herbert's altered footwork produce on-time throws without rushing clean pockets?
7. Can the group hold up on third-and-long after defenses remove the run threat?
8. Does the same error recur across practice, preseason and regular-season settings?

## Diagnostic taxonomy

Assign each pressure to one primary bucket:

- **individual loss:** correct threat identified; blocker beaten;
- **exchange error:** twist, stunt or game not passed correctly;
- **identification error:** line or quarterback set protection away from the threat;
- **numbers loss:** defense sends one more rusher than the protection can block and the throw does not defeat it;
- **design concession:** screen, boot, option or immediate throw intentionally leaves a defender unblocked;
- **quarterback-created:** protection holds long enough but depth, timing or movement produces pressure;
- **coverage-created:** initial protection holds and pressure arrives only after the concept stalls;
- **unclear:** available angle does not permit responsible assignment.

Record time to pressure and help use. A scramble or completion does not erase a blocking loss, and a sack does not automatically belong to the line.

## Scheme mitigation versus actual improvement

McDaniel can reduce negative plays through motion, play action, screens, boots, quick game, condensed formations and max protection. These are legitimate offensive answers, but the fantasy meaning depends on what the offense sacrifices.

Track three layers separately:

1. **Design:** did the play delay, redirect or outnumber the rush?
2. **Blocking:** did the assigned protectors identify and win?
3. **Quarterback:** did Herbert use the pocket and available answer correctly?

If sacks fall while Kolar, Njoku, Gadsden, Ingold or a running back stays in more often, the protection headline can improve while route opportunity falls. If five-man protection succeeds, McDaniel can preserve all eligible receivers and exploit Herbert's downfield ability.

## Houston evidence boundary

The opening reserve line was Burke–Branson Taylor–Slaughter–Awosika–Logan Taylor. Slaughter moved to left guard after one series and the offense sustained multiple long drives. This raises confidence in cross-training and installation. It does not answer the starting-five risk map because none of the five projected starters played together in that configuration.

## Fantasy implication

- **Herbert:** the largest ceiling gain comes from stable conventional protection plus retained intermediate/deep volume, not merely a lower sack count.
- **McConkey/Johnston/Harris:** quick interior pressure and edge help are direct threats to route depth and eligible releases.
- **Tight ends:** a strong line increases route opportunity; a weak edge response can make blocking-oriented Kolar more valuable to the offense but less valuable in fantasy.
- **Hampton:** pressure can create checkdowns, but a back held in protection receives no target; both outcomes must be charted.
