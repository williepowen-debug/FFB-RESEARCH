# Depth and contingencies

## Finding

The Chargers have more plausible first-wave responses than they did during the 2025 injury cascade, but depth remains role-dependent. Pipkins is an experienced tackle response but also an LG finalist; Burke is another tackle option. Slaughter now starts at center and Branson Taylor is on reserve/injured, so neither should be counted as spare active cover. One absence can be contained; two absences can force the same versatile player to solve competing problems and recreate the multi-position churn that damaged 2025 communication.

## Evidence

The unofficial depth chart, camp cross-training and Houston substitutions support the first-wave responses. The deeper ordering and system consequences remain conditional projections.

## Criticality tiers

### Tier 1: Slater, Alt and center continuity

The tackles determine how freely eligible receivers release. Slaughter must now organize the interior following the Biadasz loss. A further change can alter both execution and play design.

### Tier 2: Strange and the settled left guard

Strange carries unique system familiarity. The eventual left-guard winner becomes more important after the unit builds repetitions around him; a late change can cost more than the raw talent difference implies.

### Tier 3: first-wave reserves

Pipkins and Burke offer tackle flexibility. The remaining interior order needs verification; Slaughter is already committed to center and Taylor is unavailable.

## Decision rules

- Prefer the response that changes one position unless the direct reserve is clearly nonviable.
- Keep Slaughter's established center role separate from hypothetical guard cover.
- Check whether Pipkins' guard use leaves Burke as the first tackle response.
- Do not infer starter-level quality from Houston reserve success; use it to update only depth and assignment confidence.
- When a starter returns, verify that chips, stay-ins, run direction and launch points normalize.
- Assess the existing Biadasz-to-Slaughter transition through protection calls and run timing rather than an automatic numerical downgrade.

## Roster-management watch

- Which linemen dress for Week 1.
- Whether Slaughter's center communication holds across left-guard combinations.
- Whether Pipkins covers both tackle spots on game day.
- Whether Penning's game-day role is guard, tackle or jumbo eligible.
- When Branson Taylor becomes available for a return assessment.
- Which developmental tackle follows Pipkins: Burke, Logan Taylor or another option.
- Which active player is Slaughter's direct center replacement.
- Any later reserve-list activations relevant to line depth.
- Veteran additions after cutdowns.

## Fantasy implication

A single left-guard change is the most containable outcome. A tackle absence can reduce pass-catcher releases and target depth. The Biadasz-to-Slaughter transition can affect both Hampton's blocking geometry and Herbert's protection communication; its fantasy magnitude requires first-unit evidence.
