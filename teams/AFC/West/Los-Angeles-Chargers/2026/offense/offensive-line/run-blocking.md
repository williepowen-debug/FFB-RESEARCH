# Run-blocking assessment

## Finding

McDaniel's run game gives the rebuilt line a plausible route to immediate functional improvement because the system can combine motion, formation variation and similar-looking concepts rather than asking every play to win through static displacement. The personnel also support movement: Biadasz was targeted partly for work in space, Strange knows the system, and healthy Slater and Alt can create perimeter leverage.

Houston supplied positive installation evidence. The reserve offense ran 29 times for 104 yards and two touchdowns, including a six-run scoring sequence, while Harbaugh credited the group with correct assignments and aggressive play. That is not proof of first-unit quality: the projected five did not play together, the opponent used preseason personnel and final rushing totals do not isolate blocking from back or quarterback creation.

## Evidence

Personnel traits, McDaniel's stated preferences, Houston game totals and the documented reserve lineup support the concept-fit analysis. No competitive first-unit sample yet establishes efficiency or preferred direction.

## Concept-fit matrix

| Concept family | Personnel fit | Potential advantage | Main failure mode | Evidence needed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Outside zone | favorable if tackles are healthy | Slater/Alt movement, Biadasz range and McDaniel's established sequencing | LG lateral-range loss or backside penetration | edge capture, cutback lane, first contact and combination timing |
| Inside zone | plausible | Biadasz anchor and veteran interior can keep the read clean | immediate A/B-gap leakage from a new interior | yards before contact by gap and negative-run cause |
| Duo | plausible | Alt, Strange and bigger reserve guards can generate vertical movement | linebackers remain clean when combinations release late | linebacker contact depth and short-yardage conversion |
| Counter/power | situational | personnel versatility and motion can distort fits | slow pull path or penetration crossing the puller | puller success, kick-out result and back timing |
| Pin-pull/perimeter | favorable on paper | athletic center/tackles can lead Hampton or Mitchell into space | congestion and missed second-level angles | lead-block result and explosive-run creation |
| Split-flow/boot companion | strong ecosystem fit | tight ends/fullback can hold backside pursuit and preserve pass disguise | action becomes predictable by personnel or alignment | run/pass tendency from identical looks and backside behavior |

## Strength hypotheses

- Biadasz increases the range and variety of center-led movement concepts.
- Strange's prior McDaniel experience accelerates combination timing on the right side.
- Slater and Alt allow Los Angeles to create perimeter runs without routinely using a tight end solely as edge support.
- Hampton's downhill power gives the line margin when first contact occurs near, rather than behind, the line of scrimmage.
- Mitchell adds a speed constraint that can punish over-pursuit even if his touch volume stays limited.
- Motion and the Alec Ingold/fullback role let McDaniel alter leverage without changing the personnel group.

## Weaknesses to test

- **Left-guard fit is unsettled.** The winner must move laterally and communicate, not just anchor in isolated pass drills.
- **Healthy names do not equal coordinated blocks.** Three new interior relationships can miss overtake and release timing.
- **Tackle return risk.** A physically available Slater or Alt may still need time to restore game-level movement and balance.
- **Scheme-result confusion.** Motion can create a clean edge because a defender misfits; that should not be credited as an individual block win.
- **Back-created production.** Hampton can run through contact and Mitchell can outrun angles, masking first-level losses.
- **Short-yardage is separate.** General efficiency does not establish condensed-box movement or goal-line control.

## Houston evidence boundary

The opener established that reserve linemen could operate varied rushing calls without persistent assignment collapse. It did not establish the preferred first-unit direction, Hampton's fit, the left-guard winner or regular-season efficiency. Amar Johnson's 55-yard reception also inflated the offense's overall success without measuring run blocking.

## Charting plan

For each competitive first-unit run, record:

- five-man lineup, personnel, formation and motion;
- shotgun or under center;
- concept family, direction and intended gap;
- box count and defensive front;
- first contact relative to the line of scrimmage;
- primary block and combination-block outcome;
- whether the read forced an unblocked defender to hesitate;
- negative run, short-yardage conversion, explosive run or touchdown;
- back-created yards after a first-level loss;
- whether the same look produces play action or a screen elsewhere in the game.

Do not infer blocking quality from final rushing yards alone. Separate design leverage, blocker execution, defender error and ball-carrier creation.

## Fantasy implication

- **Omarion Hampton:** cleaner first contact supports sustainable carry volume and touchdown access, but his high-value role still depends on routes and inside-10 deployment.
- **Keaton Mitchell:** outside-zone and perimeter success can create a real changeup package, though explosive ability does not guarantee weekly volume.
- **Justin Herbert:** credible early-down rushing lowers predictable third downs and can create deeper play-action windows.
- **Pass catchers:** run/pass similarity is valuable only if Los Angeles throws often enough from the same looks to preserve target volume.
