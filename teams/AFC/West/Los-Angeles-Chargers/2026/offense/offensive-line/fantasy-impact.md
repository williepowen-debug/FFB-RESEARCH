# Fantasy impact

## Finding

The Chargers line should not be translated into one generic upgrade. Healthy tackles, interior communication, run-blocking movement and McDaniel's mitigation tools affect Herbert, Hampton and the pass catchers differently. The highest-value outcome is not simply fewer sacks; it is five-man protection that preserves five eligible routes and intermediate/deep attempts.

## Evidence

The projected personnel, 2025 pressure problem, McDaniel's public protection description and current availability create the factual inputs. The player effects below are conditional inferences that require first-unit charting.

## Fantasy implication

### Justin Herbert

**Upside path:** Slater and Alt hold without routine help, Biadasz stabilizes the interior and the left-guard winner communicates cleanly. Herbert gains better early-down efficiency without losing access to downfield throws.

**Misleading path:** motion, screens and quick throws reduce sacks while the offense avoids conventional dropbacks. Herbert may become more efficient but fail to gain the volume or target depth implied by a simple line upgrade.

**Track:** pressure and sack rate, time to pressure, play action, five-eligible releases, target depth, third-and-long performance and pressure responsibility.

### Omarion Hampton

**Upside path:** varied zone and gap concepts create cleaner entries, and sustained drives increase carry and scoring opportunity.

**Risk path:** interior leakage creates early contact; Mitchell/Vidal/Johnson take specific packages; or Hampton stays in protection on high-value passing downs.

**Track:** yards before contact by concept, first-unit carry share, inside-10 work, route share, pass-protection assignments and short-yardage personnel.

### Ladd McConkey

McConkey benefits from stable inside protection because timing routes and option work remain on schedule. His volume can stay strong in quick game even if the line struggles, however, so target count alone cannot prove protection quality.

**Track:** first-read share, route depth, pressure splits, third-down routes and whether extra protectors reduce the number of available receivers.

### Quentin Johnston, Tre' Harris and Brenen Thompson

Perimeter and vertical roles have greater sensitivity to protection duration. Tackle injuries, repeated interior pressure or max protection can reduce deep-route volume even when the offense remains functional.

**Track:** routes per dropback, alignment, target depth, play-action explosives, chip-side effects and two-receiver personnel.

### Charlie Kolar, Oronde Gadsden and David Njoku

Snap share can mislead. Kolar may play because the offense needs an attached blocker; Gadsden or Njoku may lose routes to chips or stay-ins even while remaining on the field. Strong tackle play creates the best chance for route concentration to reflect receiving priority.

**Track:** route rate, chip/pass-block rate, alignment, third-down and red-zone releases, and personnel grouping.

### Complementary backs

Mitchell gains from perimeter space, Vidal from stable downhill blocking and Amar Johnson from a role that converts receiving space into explosive yards. None should be upgraded from line quality without independent first-unit deployment.

## Projection adjustment rules

- Do not upgrade the starters because a reserve line succeeded in Houston.
- Upgrade Herbert only when protection improvement coexists with retained route participation and target depth.
- Upgrade Hampton's efficiency after repeatable first-unit yards-before-contact evidence, not one explosive result.
- Downgrade pass catchers when line problems change routes, help assignments or target depth—not from one sack alone.
- Treat Slater or Alt absence as a pass-catcher route-release event.
- Treat Biadasz absence as an offense-wide communication event.
- Separate McDaniel's design success from individual blocking quality.
