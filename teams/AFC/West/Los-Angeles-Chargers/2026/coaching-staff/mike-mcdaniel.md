# Mike McDaniel — Los Angeles Chargers

- Role: Offensive coordinator and offensive play-caller
- Joined team: 2026
- Last verified: 2026-08-16

## Responsibilities

McDaniel is the primary offensive architect and play-caller. Harbaugh described the desired coordinator as a "head coach of the offense," and the team characterized McDaniel as its offensive play-calling hire. He is supported by passing-game coordinator/quarterbacks coach Shane Day, passing-game specialist Adam Gase, offensive-line coach Butch Barry, and multiple assistants with prior McDaniel experience.

The hire followed four seasons as Miami's head coach and offensive play-caller. That background gives McDaniel more practical authority than a coordinator who is merely installing another coach's system.

## Scheme and tendencies

The expected foundation is an outside-zone and motion-heavy offense that marries the run and pass, creates horizontal conflict, and uses play-action and quick timing to limit obvious dropback situations. In Miami, McDaniel's 2023 offense led the NFL in total and passing yards and ranked second in scoring. Those historical results establish the ceiling of the structure, not a guaranteed Los Angeles outcome.

The personnel moves reinforce the intended direction: Tyler Biadasz and Cole Strange were added inside, Butch Barry followed McDaniel from Miami, Alec Ingold restores a true fullback, Charlie Kolar adds blocking flexibility, and David Njoku adds a receiving tight end. Harbaugh said the system should reduce straight dropback protection and make defenders process run before rushing Herbert.

## Personnel usage

Herbert remains the unquestioned quarterback, but the offense is changing his footwork, timing, and progression environment. Ladd McConkey is the leading quick-game and space candidate, with Quentin Johnston and Tre' Harris offering perimeter and intermediate roles. Fourth-round rookie Brenen Thompson supplies field-stretching speed.

Omarion Hampton is the likely backfield foundation after an injury-shortened rookie year. Kimani Vidal returns after 643 rushing yards in 2025, while Keaton Mitchell adds explosive speed. McDaniel has multiple backs and tight ends available, so the formation and route distribution must be charted rather than reduced to a single depth-chart order.

## Pace and situational decisions

Track pre-snap motion, snap timing, early-down pass rate, play-action rate, and how often the offense avoids third-and-long. McDaniel's structure can create explosive plays without extreme raw pace. Red-zone sequencing is especially important because yardage efficiency only becomes fantasy value when drives end in touchdowns.

## Fantasy implications

- Herbert has a credible path to better efficiency and fewer pressure-driven failures, with passing volume as the main ceiling variable.
- McConkey is the cleanest fit for quick-game space targets, but the removal of Allen does not guarantee McConkey inherits all 81 vacated receptions.
- Hampton can benefit from the run-game design and improved line while still ceding some passing-down or explosive-changeup work.
- Njoku, Oronde Gadsden, and Kolar create useful personnel flexibility but also introduce tight-end route and target competition.

## Reliability cautions

Miami's 2023 peak involved different speed, quarterback traits, and line play. McDaniel's later Miami seasons also show that scheme alone does not erase injuries or protection failure. Preseason success against simplified defenses should not be treated as proof that the full offense is ready.
