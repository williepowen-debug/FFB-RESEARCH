# Chris O'Leary — Los Angeles Chargers

- Role: Defensive coordinator and defensive play-caller
- Joined team: 2026 in current role; Chargers safeties coach in 2024
- Last verified: 2026-08-16

## Responsibilities

O'Leary replaces Jesse Minter as coordinator and primary defensive play-caller after Minter became Baltimore's head coach. O'Leary previously coached the Chargers safeties in 2024, spent 2025 as Western Michigan's defensive coordinator and safeties coach, and returned with direct familiarity with Harbaugh, the personnel, and the prior defensive language.

Steve Clinkscale coordinates the pass defense and defensive backs, while Mike Elston coordinates the run defense and line. That promoted support structure suggests collaborative planning under O'Leary rather than a one-person redesign.

## Scheme and tendencies

The working expectation is continuity in core Minter/Macdonald principles: disguise before the snap, simulated and varied pressure, flexible fronts, and safeties who can exchange jobs. O'Leary said the core philosophy and fundamentals came from his prior Los Angeles experience, but Harbaugh has emphasized that O'Leary is not identical to Minter.

At Western Michigan in 2025, O'Leary's defense ranked ninth nationally in scoring defense at 17.4 points allowed per game and 19th in total defense at 305.5 yards per game. The transfer limit is substantial: one college season against MAC competition cannot establish NFL efficiency.

## Personnel usage

Derwin James Jr. remains the movable centerpiece and received a new multi-year extension. Rookie Genesis Smith could earn deep or nickel work that allows James to operate nearer the line. Elijah Molden, Donte Jackson, Tarheeb Still, and Cam Hart supply secondary continuity.

The edge group returns Tuli Tuipulotu and Khalil Mack and adds first-round pick Akheem Mesidor after Odafe Oweh left. Inside, Teair Tart and Jamaree Caldwell are joined by Dalvin Tomlinson and rookie Nick Barrett. Track whether O'Leary uses Mesidor as a true every-down edge or moves him inside in passing packages.

## Pace and situational decisions

Defensive pace is opponent-driven, but O'Leary controls pressure timing, third-down substitution, and red-zone calls. Chart early-down pressure, simulated-pressure frequency, two-high presentation, man/zone splits, and the location of James on money downs. His first NFL play-calling season makes fourth-quarter adjustment quality a material uncertainty.

## Fantasy implications

- James retains elite IDP flexibility if O'Leary continues to deploy him around the box and pressure plan.
- Tuipulotu has the clearest established pass-rush role; Mesidor's snap share determines whether he becomes immediately usable in IDP formats.
- Coverage continuity can suppress opposing perimeter receivers, but coordinator transition makes early-season matchup assumptions provisional.
- A strong pressure plan could raise turnover and sack opportunities without requiring constant blitzing.
