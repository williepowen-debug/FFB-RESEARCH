# Finding: Secondary continuity lets O'Leary preserve disguise while moving James

## Scope

- Team/player: Los Angeles Chargers secondary; Derwin James Jr.; Chris O'Leary
- Area: coverage, pressure disguise, opponent matchups, and defensive-back IDP usage
- Season/week: 2026 preseason baseline
- Status: active
- Last verified: 2026-08-16

## Finding

Los Angeles returns its primary corner trio of Donte Jackson, Tarheeb Still, and Cam Hart plus safeties Derwin James Jr. and Elijah Molden. That continuity gives first-year NFL play-caller Chris O'Leary a plausible path to preserve the disguise and assignment flexibility that helped the Chargers rank sixth in defensive EPA per play and tied for sixth in success rate over 2024-2025.

James remains the movable centerpiece after signing a new multi-year extension. The selection of safety Genesis Smith adds a specific subpackage possibility: Harbaugh said Smith could join Molden in nickel structures that move James closer to the line of scrimmage. That would support James' pressure and tackle ceiling while asking a rookie to handle coverage space. The scheme is continuous in principle, but opponent matchup strength remains provisional until O'Leary reveals his own call sequencing.

## Fantasy implication

- James retains a high-value IDP profile if he continues to align in the box, slot, and pressure plan rather than becoming a conventional deep safety.
- Henley's communication role and James' movement make them the clearest tackle/impact-event bets; the other safeties may rotate by package.
- Returning corners can make the Chargers a difficult perimeter matchup, but coordinator transition and young-player variance argue against automatically downgrading every opposing receiver.
- Smith's emergence could improve structural flexibility while creating a rookie matchup target if offenses can isolate him.

## Evidence

- O'Leary coached the Chargers safeties in 2024 and said he intends to elevate the existing defensive foundation rather than install a radical departure.
- The Chargers ranked sixth in defensive EPA per play and tied for sixth in defensive success rate across 2024 and 2025, allowing 18.9 points per game over that span.
- Jackson, Still, and Hart entered camp as the top three corners; Deane Leonard returned as depth and a special-teams contributor.
- James recorded 94 tackles, two sacks, three interceptions, and eight quarterback hits in 2025 and received a multi-year extension in May 2026.
- Harbaugh identified Smith as a possible nickel participant whose presence could move James nearer the line of scrimmage.
- Smith entered the league with five interceptions, 14 passes defensed, and four forced fumbles across 37 college games.
