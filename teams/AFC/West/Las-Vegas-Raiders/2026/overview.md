# Las Vegas Raiders — 2026 Kubiak Reset and Quarterback Bridge

- Conference/division: AFC West
- Last verified: 2026-08-17
- Scope: changes from the 2025 team to the 2026 preseason roster and staff
- Build tier: Core

Las Vegas rebuilt the team around Head Coach Klint Kubiak, a veteran-rookie quarterback bridge, and a much more aggressive defensive acquisition plan. Kirk Cousins opened camp as the starting quarterback after signing in April, while No. 1 overall pick Fernando Mendoza develops behind him. The offensive core is built around Ashton Jeanty, Brock Bowers, Tyler Linderbaum, and an unsettled receiver room. The defense added Kwity Paye, Quay Walker, Nakobe Dean, Taron Johnson, and multiple rookies around Maxx Crosby.

## Fantasy-relevant snapshot

### Confirmed baseline

- Kubiak is the new head coach; Andrew Janocko is offensive coordinator; Rob Leonard is defensive coordinator.
- Cousins opened camp as QB1, with Mendoza working behind him after being selected No. 1 overall.
- Linderbaum was added at center, and the offensive line has a best-five competition around Kolton Miller, DJ Glaze, Jackson Powers-Johnson, Caleb Rogers, Charles Grant, Spencer Burford, and Trey Zuhn III.
- Jeanty is the clear backfield anchor entering Year 2, with Washington added in Round 4.
- Bowers is the most reliable passing-game projection after leading the 2025 Raiders in receptions, receiving yards per game, and receiving touchdowns.
- Tucker, Nailor, Bech, Thornton, and Benson form a receiver room that needs route-share evidence.
- Crosby returned to practice after knee rehab; Paye, Dean, Walker, Taron Johnson, Stukes, Crawford, McCoy, Dalton Johnson, and Masses reshape the defense.

### Working fantasy hypotheses

- Cousins starts early and stabilizes Bowers/Jeanty while Mendoza remains a live midseason variable.
- Bowers remains the offense's highest-confidence target earner, especially in the red zone.
- Jeanty leads the backfield, but Washington's high-value touch share must be checked.
- Receiver value is more likely to come from best-ball spike weeks until routes consolidate.
- The defense becomes matchup-dependent rather than automatically exploitable if Crosby is healthy and Leonard settles the new coverage pieces.

## Offensive identity

### Confirmed changes

The Raiders replaced the 2025 offensive structure with Kubiak's staff, signed Cousins, drafted Mendoza, and added Linderbaum. That points to a run-game-connected offense with more under-center, play-action, and tight-end answers than a spread-volume approach. The immediate plan is veteran-led, but Mendoza's draft slot makes quarterback timing the season's defining variable.

### Questions to resolve

- Does Cousins keep the job into the regular season, and how quickly does Mendoza close the gap?
- Can Linderbaum stabilize the run game while the guard and right-tackle spots settle?
- Does Jeanty keep passing-down and goal-line work?
- Is Bowers a true offensive centerpiece or part of a broader tight-end/receiver distribution?
- Which receiver earns full-time routes with Cousins?

The evidence and invalidation tests are maintained in the [offensive research module](offense/README.md).

## Defensive matchup profile

### Confirmed changes

Leonard takes over a defense with far more new talent than continuity. Crosby remains the focal point, Paye adds a starting-caliber edge, and Dean/Walker upgrade linebacker athleticism. Johnson and the rookie defensive backs give Joe Woods more coverage options, but camp reports still point to real competition in the secondary.

### Questions to resolve

- Is Crosby full-go by Week 1, and how are edge snaps divided among Paye, Koonce, and Crawford?
- Do Dean and Walker play together in nickel?
- Is Taron Johnson locked into the slot, and who wins outside-corner work?
- Can Leonard generate pressure without creating explosive-pass exposure?

Las Vegas should be treated as an improving but unproven matchup. The front-seven upside is clearer than the coverage floor. See the [defensive research module](defense/README.md).

### Analytical projection

The Raiders are a high-variance fantasy team. Bowers and Jeanty are investable anchors; Cousins is a stabilizing bridge; Mendoza is a dynasty and late-season contingency; the receivers are role bets; and the defense needs early charting before matchup confidence rises.

## Special teams
