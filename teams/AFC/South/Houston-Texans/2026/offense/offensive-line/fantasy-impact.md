# Finding: Offensive-line effects must be routed through role evidence

## Scope

- Team/player: C.J. Stroud, Nico Collins, Houston backs and tight ends
- Area: fantasy translation from offensive-line evidence
- Season/week: 2026 preseason
- Status: active
- Last verified: 2026-08-18

## Finding

Houston's line can improve fantasy outcomes in three different ways: fewer immediate pressures, better early-down rushing efficiency, and fewer protection concessions that pull receivers, tight ends, and backs out of routes. Those channels should not be collapsed into a single "line improved" label.

The best-case fantasy version is Stroud keeping target depth and tempo while Montgomery gets cleaner scoring-area runs and Marks releases as a checkdown option. The lower-ceiling version is fewer sacks but more chips, max protection, and condensed routes.

## Fantasy implication

- **Stroud:** upgrade when protection improves with route volume preserved.
- **Collins:** benefits most from stable edges and enough time for isolation/intermediate routes.
- **Higgins, Dell, Hutchinson, Noel, Bond, and Wayne:** need route participation more than camp catches.
- **Schultz and Klein:** receiving value depends on routes, not snaps.
- **Montgomery:** efficiency and touchdown outlook rise with short-yardage displacement.
- **Marks:** PPR value depends on checkdowns after releases, not protection-only third-down snaps.

## Evidence

- Camp evidence includes both pressure-answer positives and defensive pressure stress.
- Marks has checkdown evidence, while Klein has seam and preseason receiving evidence that should be tested against blocking assignments.
- The preseason priority file now separates OL/protection, Stroud answers, RB roles, WR hierarchy, and TE route roles.

## Assessment

- Confidence: medium.
- Reason: the transmission rules are sound, but usage data has not settled them.
- Fact/inference boundary: reported checkdowns, catches, and role projections are facts; fantasy value changes depend on charted routes and pressures.
- What would invalidate this: measured usage showing route shares unaffected by protection, or the offense leaning into heavy protection enough to cap non-Collins receiving roles.
