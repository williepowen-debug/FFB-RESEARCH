# Houston Texans - 2026 Continuity Staff and Protection Reset

- Conference/division: AFC South
- Last verified: 2026-08-17
- Scope: Core-tier 2026 preseason team module
- Build tier: Core

Houston is a continuity-plus-correction build. DeMeco Ryans, Nick Caley, Matt Burke, and Frank Ross all remain in place, but the offensive roster was rebuilt around C.J. Stroud's protection, David Montgomery's arrival, and a deeper tight-end/line structure. The defense returns the more stable fantasy baseline after a 2025 season with elite official production and multiple All-Pro defenders.

## Fantasy-relevant snapshot

### Confirmed baseline

- Ryans, Caley, Burke, and Ross are retained in the same lead roles.
- Stroud enters Year 2 under Caley, whose official profile credits the 2025 offense with low turnovers, improved sack totals, a career-high Stroud completion percentage, a 1,000-yard Nico Collins season, and a Texans tight-end receptions record for Dalton Schultz.
- Houston released Joe Mixon and traded for David Montgomery, creating a new lead-back baseline.
- The offensive line was reshaped with Wyatt Teller, Braden Smith, Evan Brown, retained Trent Brown and Ed Ingram, first-round guard Keylan Rutledge, and fourth-round guard Febechi Nwaiwu.
- Schultz was extended, Foster Moreau was signed, and Marlin Klein was drafted in Round 2.
- The defense retained Burke and extended Will Anderson Jr. and Danielle Hunter while adding Kayden McDonald, Reed Blankenship, Kamari Ramsey, and Jadeveon Clowney.

### Working fantasy hypotheses

- Stroud's efficiency can rise if protection stabilizes, but camp reports still make offensive-line improvement a prove-it item.
- Montgomery leads carries and scoring-area work, while passing-down snaps remain open to Woody Marks, Jawhar Jordan, and package usage.
- Collins remains the cleanest pass-catching projection; Schultz stays relevant but must hold route share against a deeper tight-end room.
- Texans D/ST begins as a draftable unit because the defense combines continuity, rush talent, and a strong coverage core.

## Offensive identity

Caley's second season is the organizing principle. Houston did not change the coordinator; it changed the tool kit by adding power and flexibility at running back, tight end, and offensive line. The likely intent is better early-down balance, more protection answers for Stroud, and more ability to use tight ends as both blockers and route threats.

The key unknown is whether the investment turns into a coherent starting five. If it does, Stroud can support Collins, Schultz, and at least one secondary receiver with more touchdown efficiency. If it does not, the offense may lean into shorter throws, extra protection, and Montgomery volume.

## Defensive matchup profile

The defense is the stronger confirmed side. Burke's official profile credits Houston's 2025 defense with franchise-best yardage and scoring marks, plus strong ranks in sacks and takeaways. Anderson, Hunter, and Stingley supply premium individual matchups; McDonald and Clowney add front depth; Blankenship and Ramsey add safety flexibility.

Treat Houston as a difficult opponent for quarterbacks and perimeter receivers until injuries or regular-season efficiency prove otherwise. IDP clarity belongs mostly to Anderson and Al-Shaair at this stage; secondary tackle floors require snap and alignment confirmation.

Frank Ross returns as special teams coordinator. Kai Kroeger was acquired by trade and the official transaction ledger later showed Jack Stonehouse waived, making Kroeger the working punter baseline. Ka'imi Fairbairn remains on the active roster at kicker. Special teams stays scaffolded at Core tier unless return usage or kicker/punter changes become weekly-relevant.
