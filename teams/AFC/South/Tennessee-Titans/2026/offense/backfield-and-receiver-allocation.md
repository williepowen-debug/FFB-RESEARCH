# Finding: Pollard and Spears remain the backfield baseline while receiver roles are unsettled

## Scope

- Unit: running backs, wide receivers, tight ends
- Area: touch allocation and target hierarchy
- Season: 2026
- Status: active preseason baseline
- Last verified: 2026-08-17

## Finding

Tennessee's offensive skill group is deeper than it was entering 2025, but it is not yet cleanly tiered. Tony Pollard and Tyjae Spears are the immediate backfield baseline, with official reporting saying Saleh called them the team's bell cows during the offseason. Fifth-round rookie Nick Singleton is the pressure point if he earns preseason work. At receiver, Calvin Ridley remains the veteran name, but Wan'Dale Robinson had the strongest late-offseason momentum, Carnell Tate carries top-five draft capital, and Chimere Dike, Elic Ayomanor, Xavier Restrepo, K.J. Osborn, and others are competing for snaps.

## Fantasy implication

Pollard and Spears should be projected as a split until preseason usage shows a lead back or a clear passing-down specialist. Spears gains value in PPR if Daboll expands his receiving role. Singleton is a bench/late-round watch rather than a projected immediate starter. At receiver, Robinson is the short-area PPR watch, Tate is the high-upside rookie, and Ridley needs camp health and first-team route confirmation before being treated as a stable top target.

## Evidence

### Facts

- The running back camp preview lists Pollard, Spears, Singleton, Michael Carter, Julius Chestnut, and Kalel Mullings.
- Wyatt reported Spears has a chance for a bigger runner/receiver role in Daboll's offense, while Pollard remained steady and reliable in offseason work.
- The same preview reports Singleton was limited early while recovering from a college injury but looked more comfortable later in OTAs.
- The wide receiver preview lists 13 camp receivers and identifies Tate as the player to watch.
- Wyatt reported Robinson ended the offseason with the most receiver momentum and that Ward-Robinson chemistry improved in June.
- Jaren Kanak, the seventh-round tight end, was placed on injured reserve after a preseason injury, reducing 2026 rookie tight end relevance.

### Inferences

- A two-back Pollard/Spears structure is the most defensible preseason baseline.
- Receiver value may be spread too broadly if Tate, Ridley, Robinson, Dike, Ayomanor, and Restrepo all keep roles.
- Daboll could support more receiving-back value than Tennessee has recently produced, but the split needs route evidence.
