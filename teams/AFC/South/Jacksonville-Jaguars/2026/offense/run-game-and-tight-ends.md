# Finding: Jacksonville rebuilt the run game around competition and heavier personnel

## Scope

- Team/player: Jaguars running backs, tight ends, and offensive line
- Area: Etienne replacement, run efficiency, and 12/13 personnel
- Season/week: 2026 preseason baseline
- Status: active
- Last verified: 2026-08-17

## Finding

Jacksonville's run-game question is both personnel and structure. Travis Etienne Jr. left after a 2025 season with 1,107 rushing yards, 36 receptions, and 13 total touchdowns. The Jaguars added Chris Rodriguez Jr., retained DeeJay Dallas, return Bhayshul Tuten and LeQuint Allen Jr., hired Brian Picucci as run game coordinator, extended Brenton Strange, and drafted Nate Boerkircher and Tanner Koziol.

The first unofficial depth chart listed Tuten or Rodriguez as co-starters at running back. That supports a real competition rather than a settled depth chart. Jaguars Media's July offensive analysis repeatedly identified run efficiency, offensive-line health, and heavy personnel as the key offensive storyline.

## Fantasy implication

- Tuten has the most explosive profile and should be monitored for lead-back and return-game overlap.
- Rodriguez has short-yardage and early-down paths, especially if Jacksonville wants more efficient rushing.
- Allen can matter in PPR if he keeps a third-down role, but the first depth chart placed him behind the co-starters.
- Strange gains if 12/13 personnel becomes a weekly base; Boerkircher and Koziol can lower wide-receiver routes if they play early.

## Evidence

- Etienne agreed to terms with New Orleans after leading Jacksonville in rushing in 2025.
- Rodriguez signed after rushing for 500 yards and six touchdowns with Washington in 2025.
- Jacksonville hired Picucci as run game coordinator after Tampa Bay ranked top 10 in several rushing categories during his two seasons with the Buccaneers' line.
- The 2026 draft included two tight ends: Boerkircher at No. 56 and Koziol at No. 164.
- The first unofficial depth chart listed Tuten or Rodriguez as running-back co-starters.
- Jaguars Media analysts identified running back, offensive-line health, run efficiency, and heavier tight-end personnel as central offensive questions.
