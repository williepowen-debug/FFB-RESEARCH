# Finding: Interior pressure is the Jaguars defense's swing factor

## Scope

- Team/player: Jaguars defensive front
- Area: pass rush, run-defense carryover, and DST ceiling
- Season/week: 2026 preseason baseline
- Status: active
- Last verified: 2026-08-17

## Finding

Jacksonville's defensive floor is built on Campanile's run defense and turnover gains, but the 2026 ceiling depends on more reliable pass rush. Jaguars.com credits the 2025 defense with the NFL's best run defense and a franchise-record takeaway profile, yet Jaguars Media's July defensive analysis repeatedly identified interior pressure as the key growth area.

Personnel movement supports that emphasis. Travon Walker signed a long-term extension and may continue moving inside in passing situations. The Jaguars traded for Ruke Orhorhoro, drafted Albert Regis in Round 3, and added edge rookies Wesley Williams and Zach Durfee behind Josh Hines-Allen and Walker.

## Fantasy implication

- Jacksonville DST should carry a useful weekly floor if run defense keeps opponents behind schedule.
- The path to a higher fantasy ceiling is sacks catching up to takeaways.
- Walker has stronger big-play and sack rebound appeal if his late-2025 interior-rush usage expands.
- Rookie defensive-line additions are more important for team DST depth than immediate individual fantasy value.

## Evidence

- The official coaches roster credits Campanile with franchise records in rushing yards allowed per game, takeaways, interceptions, passes defensed, and turnover differential.
- Jaguars Media identified defensive interior pressure as a repeated 2026 concern and route to becoming elite.
- Walker signed a four-year extension in April after playing through wrist and knee injuries in 2025.
- Jacksonville traded Maason Smith for Ruke Orhorhoro and drafted Albert Regis, Wesley Williams, and Zach Durfee.
- The first unofficial depth chart listed Hamilton and Armstead as starters, with Regis, Dickerson, Orhorhoro, and others in the interior rotation.
