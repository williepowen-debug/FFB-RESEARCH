# Jacksonville Jaguars - 2026 Continuity with Usage Volatility

- Conference/division: AFC South
- Last verified: 2026-08-17
- Scope: Core-tier 2026 preseason baseline
- Build tier: Core

Jacksonville is a high-continuity contender module with targeted role churn. Liam Coen, Grant Udinski, Anthony Campanile, and Heath Farwell return from a 13-4 AFC South title season. The offense replaces Travis Etienne Jr. with a Tuten/Rodriguez/Allen backfield question, expands tight-end flexibility with Nate Boerkircher and Tanner Koziol, and must define Travis Hunter's two-way workload. The defense returns Campanile after elite run-defense and turnover results, but needs more pass rush and a Lloyd replacement.

## Fantasy-relevant snapshot

### Confirmed baseline

- Coen, Udinski, Campanile, and Farwell return from the 2025 AFC South Championship staff.
- The 2025 offense set franchise records in points, touchdowns, and first downs.
- Etienne left for New Orleans after leading Jacksonville in rushing and scoring 13 total touchdowns in 2025.
- Rodriguez joined from Washington, and the first unofficial depth chart listed Tuten or Rodriguez as co-starters.
- Hunter is listed as a starting cornerback and second-team wide receiver after missing much of 2025 with a knee injury.
- Strange, Meyers, Walker, Brown, Gardeck, Morris, Matiscik, and others were retained or extended.
- Campanile's 2025 defense set franchise records in takeaways, interceptions, passes defensed, and turnover differential.

### Working fantasy hypotheses

- Lawrence has a strong environmental floor, but pass volume and target concentration depend on run efficiency and tight-end personnel.
- Hunter is a ceiling asset whose weekly offensive route stability remains unproven.
- Tuten/Rodriguez/Allen can become a usable fantasy backfield, but preseason high-value touch allocation is essential.
- Strange is the tight end most likely to matter, with Boerkircher and Koziol influencing personnel usage.
- Jacksonville DST has a good floor; its ceiling requires more sacks from interior pressure and Walker/Hines-Allen packages.

## Offensive identity

### Confirmed changes

Jacksonville retained the main offensive staff and added Picucci as run game coordinator. The roster replaced Etienne's lead-back production with Rodriguez plus returning Tuten, Allen, and Dallas. The draft added Boerkircher and Koziol, and Strange signed an extension that signals tight end as a core part of the offense.

### Questions to resolve

- Does Hunter run enough routes to be a weekly fantasy receiver, or is he a designed-touch complement?
- Does Tuten, Rodriguez, or Allen control goal-line and passing-down work?
- Do 12 and 13 personnel become weekly identity pieces or matchup packages?
- Does the offensive line stabilize with Harrison at left tackle while Van Lanen works back?

The evidence and invalidation tests are maintained in the [offensive research module](offense/README.md).

## Defensive matchup profile

### Confirmed changes

Campanile returns after a major 2025 defensive jump. Araujo joins as defensive pass game coordinator, Walker signed a long-term extension, Orhorhoro arrived by trade, and the draft added Regis, Huskey, Williams, Durfee, and Hughes. Lloyd, Wingard, and Newsome left in free agency.

### Questions to resolve

- Can the defense turn elite run defense and takeaways into a higher sack ceiling?
- Does Walker move inside often enough to improve third-down pressure?
- Who plays full-time next to Oluokun after Lloyd's departure?
- Does Hunter's primary cornerback role materially improve coverage and quarterback hold time?

Jacksonville should begin as a difficult rushing matchup and a viable DST, with weekly spike potential tied to pressure growth. See the [defensive research module](defense/README.md).
