# Finding: Multiple-tight-end usage is a durable 2026 monitoring priority

## Scope

- Team/player: Los Angeles Rams tight ends and receiver depth
- Area: personnel grouping, route distribution, and fantasy role pressure
- Season/week: 2026 preseason
- Status: active
- Last verified: 2026-08-17

## Finding

The Rams have enough evidence to treat heavy tight-end usage as a 2026 identity element, not just a 2025 one-off. Official Rams draft reporting said the 2025 tight-end depth let the offense reach a 30.5% 13 personnel usage rate, and the team then re-signed Tyler Higbee and drafted Max Klare in Round 2 while retaining Colby Parkinson, Davis Allen, and Terrance Ferguson.

## Fantasy implication

The direct fantasy impact is role compression behind Nacua and Adams. If 13 personnel remains a core package, the weekly WR3 role may be unstable even when the offense is strong. The tight-end room is better for best ball, tight-end premium, and dynasty watch lists than standard redraft certainty because five usable tight ends can split routes and touchdowns.

## Evidence

### Facts

- The Rams' Day 2 draft article said 2025 tight-end depth enabled 13 personnel usage of 30.5%, more than double the next-closest team and the highest in the Next Gen Stats era by nearly 13 percentage points.
- The Rams re-signed Tyler Higbee and drafted Max Klare in the second round.
- The official tight-end reset said Higbee, Parkinson, Allen, Ferguson, Redman, Klare, Rohan Jones, and Dan Villari were under contract entering camp.
- The receiver camp preview said the third receiver role is ambiguous because a tight end can function as the third eligible in some concepts and formations.

### Inferences

- Klare's draft capital protects the long-term multiple-tight-end structure.
- Higbee/Parkinson/Allen/Ferguson carryover reduces Klare's immediate redraft floor.
- WR3 candidates need route data, not just depth-chart labels, before fantasy ranking.
