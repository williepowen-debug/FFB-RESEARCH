# Finding: Linebacker and secondary changes drive the defensive ceiling

## Scope

- Team/player: Commanders linebackers and secondary
- Area: coverage, run fits, and IDP/DST relevance
- Season/week: 2026 preseason baseline
- Status: active
- Last verified: 2026-08-17

## Finding

Washington paired Jones' pressure plan with major middle-of-field changes. The Commanders drafted Sonny Styles at No. 7, signed Leo Chenal, added Nick Cross and Amik Robertson in free agency, and later signed Rasul Douglas during camp. The official camp roster placed Styles, Chenal, Frankie Luvu, Jordan Magee, and Kain Medrano in the linebacker room, with Cross, Quan Martin, Percy Butler, Jeremy Reaves, Robertson, Mike Sainristil, Trey Amos, Douglas, and others in the back end.

Chenal's team feature is the strongest scheme clue: it described Jones' system as borrowing from Minnesota pressure concepts, asking players to learn multiple positions, and giving linebackers more chances to make plays. That creates IDP upside but also asks the secondary to survive more stress.

## Fantasy implication

- Styles is the rookie IDP watch if he earns every-down usage.
- Chenal can become a splash-play linebacker if Jones uses him downhill and in pressure looks.
- The Commanders D/ST ceiling depends on whether Cross, Robertson, Douglas, and Sainristil prevent explosive leakage behind pressure.
- Opposing tight ends and slot receivers remain a monitoring point until Washington proves the new middle-field rules.

## Evidence

- Washington drafted Styles at No. 7 and described him as a top defensive piece.
- Washington signed Chenal, Cross, Robertson, and Douglas as part of the defensive reset.
- Chenal said Jones' system is linebacker-friendly and allows movement around the defense.
- The official camp roster showed the rebuilt linebacker, cornerback, and safety groups entering camp.
