# New York Giants - 2026 Harbaugh Reset

- Conference/division: NFC East
- Last verified: 2026-08-17
- Scope: 2026 preseason Core-tier baseline
- Build tier: Core

The Giants are a high-change 2026 module. John Harbaugh replaces the Daboll/Kafka interim structure, Matt Nagy coordinates the offense, Dennard Wilson coordinates the defense, and the roster has major churn on both sides of the ball. The offensive thesis is a Dart Year 2 jump inside a Nagy/Callahan/Roman blend with a stronger run-game spine. The defensive thesis is Wilson fixing the run defense while creating pressure ambiguity with a rebuilt linebacker and edge group.

## Fantasy-relevant snapshot

### Confirmed baseline

- Harbaugh was hired as the Giants' 21st head coach on January 20, 2026.
- Nagy is offensive coordinator with Callahan as passing game coordinator/quarterbacks coach and Roman as senior offensive assistant.
- Dart is the Year 2 quarterback around whom the offense is being rebuilt.
- Nabers is working back from a Week 4 knee injury; Schoen said Week 1 readiness was still wait-and-see at the start of camp.
- Skattebo was cleared for camp team work and is the most important running-back usage watch.
- Mauigoa was drafted No. 10 overall after the Dexter Lawrence trade, and Ricard was added as a run-game identity signal.
- Wilson takes over a defense that Giants.com reported ranked poorly in 2025 yards, scoring, and yards per carry allowed.
- Edmunds, Reese, Reader, Newsome, Washington, Pinnock, and Hood are core additions to the defensive reset.

### Working fantasy hypotheses

- Dart can gain superflex value if the offense keeps designed movement and improves efficiency.
- Nabers remains the clear fantasy anchor when healthy, but his ramp carries real preseason risk.
- Skattebo's upside rises if Mauigoa and Ricard create a more efficient rushing environment.
- Secondary pass-catcher roles are unsettled because the receiver room is crowded.
- The Giants DST has sack-path upside but should not be trusted until the run-defense floor improves.
- Edmunds is the safest IDP profile; Reese has the highest upside if his role is full-time.

## Offensive identity

### Confirmed changes

The offense changes staff, structure, and supporting cast. Nagy brings Chiefs background, Callahan focuses on Dart and the passing game, and Roman adds run-game influence. The Giants drafted Mauigoa in the first round, signed Ricard, added Likely, and added several veteran receivers around Nabers, Slayton, and rookie Fields.

### Questions to resolve

- Does Dart keep rushing and movement value in Nagy's offense?
- Is Nabers ready for Week 1 and full route volume?
- Does Skattebo control early-down and goal-line work, or do Tracy and Singletary force a committee?
- Does Mauigoa start immediately and improve run-game efficiency?
- Which secondary pass catchers actually run first-team routes?

The active findings are in the [offensive research module](offense/README.md).

## Defensive matchup profile

### Confirmed changes

Wilson replaces the 2025 defensive leadership structure and inherits both upside and risk. The Giants moved on from Lawrence and Okereke but added Edmunds, Reese, Reader, Newsome, Washington, Pinnock, Hood, Jamison-Travis, and Kelly. The front-seven vision is multiplicity with Burns, Thibodeaux, Carter, Edmunds, and Reese.

### Questions to resolve

- Can the defense fix the 2025 run leak without Lawrence?
- Does Reese earn full-time work or begin as a package player?
- Does the pressure package produce sacks and takeaways rather than only disruption?
- How do Newsome, Hood, Nubin, Washington, and Pinnock settle into Wilson's coverage structure?

The defense should start as a volatility profile rather than an automatic matchup downgrade. See the [defensive research module](defense/README.md).

Harbaugh is the program authority. Nagy has the coordinator title, but Callahan and Roman materially shape the quarterback and run-game questions. Wilson has the clearest defensive identity statement: repair the run defense and create pressure unpredictability.

## Special teams

Chris Horton is assistant head coach/special teams coordinator. The Giants changed specialists by moving on from Graham Gano and Jamie Gillan, adding Jordan Stout, and setting up a kicker competition after Jason Sanders was also released. Special teams remains scaffolded at Core tier.
