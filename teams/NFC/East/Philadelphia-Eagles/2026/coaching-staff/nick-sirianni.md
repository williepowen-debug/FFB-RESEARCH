# Nick Sirianni - Philadelphia Eagles Head Coach

## Responsibilities

Sirianni remains Philadelphia's head coach and program authority for 2026. The Eagles' official coaches page lists him as head coach in his sixth campaign and credits him with five postseason appearances, three division titles, two conference titles, and the Super Bowl LIX championship through his first five seasons.

Sean Mannion replaces Kevin Patullo as offensive coordinator, while Vic Fangio remains defensive coordinator and Michael Clay remains special teams coordinator. Sirianni is therefore the continuity point over a roster that has a new offensive play-caller, a new offensive line coach, and meaningful receiver and secondary turnover.

## Scheme and tendencies

The 2026 offense is being framed by the team as a blend of Mannion's background and concepts that have worked for the Eagles with Jalen Hurts. Sirianni told team reporters that the coordinator search prioritized detail, conviction, and how candidates would call the offense rather than only prior NFL play-calling experience.

On defense, Sirianni retains Fangio after two high-end defensive seasons. The staff structure lets Fangio continue to own the defensive identity while Sirianni oversees weekly risk tolerance, roster usage, and offensive/defensive balance.

## Personnel usage

The core usage question is how much the new offensive staff changes what already worked around Hurts, Saquon Barkley, DeVonta Smith, Dallas Goedert, and an experienced offensive line. The team also moved away from A.J. Brown and added Makai Lemon, Hollywood Brown, Elijah Moore, Dontayvion Wicks, and Eli Stowers, so Sirianni's staff has to sort target distribution without treating past receiver shares as stable.

## Pace and situational decisions

Core-tier evidence does not support a fixed 2026 pace claim yet. The first preseason and early-season review should chart neutral pass rate, first-down run rate, shotgun/under-center balance, and whether Hurts' designed-run usage changes under Mannion and Kuper.

## Fantasy implications

- Hurts remains the strongest Eagles fantasy anchor, but his coordinator churn and changed pass-catching group should be tracked early.
- Barkley remains the primary back, with role risk more about efficiency and rest-of-room packages than losing the lead job.
- Smith has a clearer path to lead the pass game after A.J. Brown's trade, but Lemon, Wicks, Brown, Moore, Goedert, and Stowers make route concentration a live question.
- The Eagles D/ST remains draftable if Fangio's pass defense and pass rush survive the safety and edge changes.

## Statements and reporting

Official team reporting on February 24 described Mannion as a first-time play-caller and said the offense would keep some successful Eagles concepts while adding Mannion's background. Team reporting on May 21 said Mannion was building a "blend" and explicitly avoided declaring the offense complete in May.

## Reliability cautions

Coordinator intentions from February through June are not the same as September game plans. Treat personnel grouping, motion, pass rate, and high-value touch distribution as preseason charting questions rather than settled facts.
