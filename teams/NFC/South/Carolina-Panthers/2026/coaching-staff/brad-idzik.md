## Responsibilities

Idzik remains offensive coordinator and becomes the regular-season play-caller in 2026. Canales framed the move as a way to broaden his own head-coach scope. Darrell Bevell, Harold Goodwin, Mike Bercovici, Will Harriger, and the position coaches supply experienced support, but no source says they own the call sheet.

## Scheme and tendencies

Idzik inherits a system that improved with Bryce Young in 2025 and now has more receiver and tight-end uncertainty. His prior role with Young and the offense supports continuity, but 2026 is his first regular-season play-calling assignment. That makes sequencing, early-down pass rate, play-action volume, and red-zone calls unproven.

## Personnel usage

The stated offensive baseline entering camp had Young as the clear starter, Hubbard as the backfield leader, McMillan and Coker established as the top two receivers, and Legette in a prove-it role. Chris Brazzell II's injured-reserve placement removes a rookie vertical option, while Waller creates a new tight-end target-share question.

## Pace and situational decisions

Track whether Idzik uses tempo to support Young, whether he protects line injuries with quick game and tight-end help, and whether the offense stays balanced when trailing. Idzik has preseason play-calling exposure, but that is weaker evidence than regular-season fourth-quarter and red-zone sequencing.

## Fantasy implications

Young's superflex stability depends on Idzik turning improved 2025 play into repeatable passing efficiency. Hubbard's value depends on whether he keeps clear lead and goal-line work as Jonathon Brooks returns and AJ Dillon competes. McMillan and Coker are the target-share priorities; Waller's route rate and red-zone usage need immediate monitoring.
