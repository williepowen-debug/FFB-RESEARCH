# Larry Foote - Tampa Bay Buccaneers Defensive Leadership

- Role: Run game coordinator/outside linebackers coach
- Season: 2026
- Joined team: 2019
- Last verified: 2026-08-17

## Responsibilities

Foote is not listed by the team as defensive coordinator; Tampa Bay's public 2026 structure leaves Todd Bowles as the defensive authority. This profile serves as the Core-tier defensive leadership profile because Foote is the senior retained defensive assistant tied to the run front, outside linebackers, and front-seven teaching.

## Scheme and tendencies

Foote's profile aligns with Bowles' run-first, pressure-oriented structure. His official biography credits him with linebacker development and a major role in late-season defensive improvement during prior Tampa seasons. In 2026, his outside-linebacker room adds Rueben Bain Jr. and Al-Quadin Muhammad to Yaya Diaby, David Walker, Anthony Nelson, Chris Braswell, and Mohamed Kamara.

## Personnel usage

The fantasy-relevant question is whether the edge room becomes a wave rotation or produces stable snap leaders. Bain is the highest-upside rookie, Muhammad brings recent sack production, Diaby has pressure-rate upside, and Walker is returning from a rookie-year knee injury. If Foote and Bowles rotate heavily, weekly IDP predictability may lag behind team DST value.

## Pace and situational decisions

Foote's unit affects third downs even if Bowles owns the call sheet. Strong four-man rush would let Bowles avoid some blitz exposure; weak edge conversion would keep Tampa in pressure-heavy game plans that can create sacks and turnovers while exposing the secondary.

## Fantasy implications

Facts: Tampa invested premium draft and free-agent resources in edge rush after a 2025 sack-conversion problem. Inference: the team wants more front-generated pressure and less blitz dependency. Fantasy implication: Bain is the best dynasty/IDP watch, Diaby and Muhammad are sack-spike candidates, and Tampa DST becomes more attractive when opposing tackles are vulnerable.

## Statements and reporting

Buccaneers.com reported that Tampa wanted a bigger, more aggressive defense and hoped additions up front would produce more pressure from four-man rushes. Foote's group is central to that proof point.

## Reliability cautions

This is an influence profile, not a claim that Foote is a titled defensive coordinator. Update the authority model immediately if Tampa Bay announces a DC or changes Foote's title.
