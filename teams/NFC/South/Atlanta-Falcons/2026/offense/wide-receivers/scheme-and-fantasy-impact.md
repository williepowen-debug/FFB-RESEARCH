# Finding: A Stefanski/Rees run-and-play-action scheme compresses WR volume and concentrates it on London

## Scope

- Unit: wide receivers, offensive scheme
- Area: how the new system distributes WR targets in fantasy terms
- Season: 2026
- Status: active preseason baseline
- Last verified: 2026-08-17

## Finding

The move to a Kevin Stefanski system with Tommy Rees calling plays points toward an under-center, wide-zone, play-action offense with multi-tight-end sets and a max-contract bell-cow back. That profile tends to *compress* total pass volume while *concentrating* the targets that remain. The practical effect for fantasy is a clear split: it suppresses aggregate and depth-WR production but protects a defined alpha's target share — and that alpha is Drake London.

## Fantasy implication

- London is a back-end WR1 whose floor is his volume and red-zone role; the run-leaning scheme is a modest cap on his ceiling, not a threat to his role.
- The projected target hierarchy is London, then TE Kyle Pitts, then Bijan Robinson out of the backfield, then Dotson, Zaccheaus, and Branch — meaning no second WR profiles as a weekly starter.
- Dotson and Zaccheaus are best-ball/bye-week darts; Branch is a dynasty/best-ball speculation. QB uncertainty caps everyone but London.

## Evidence

### Facts

- Rees came up under Stefanski in Cleveland (pass-game specialist/TE coach 2024, OC 2025) and is expected to call plays in Atlanta. (NFL.com; Heavy.com, 2026)
- ESPN camp intel notes the system "emphasizes play-action and situational drills," with London used as a safety net for the quarterbacks adjusting to the offense. (ESPN, 2026-08-13)
- Stefanski's Cleveland passing offenses concentrated targets and ran lower volume — in 2022 only three pass catchers topped 50 targets. (Dawgs By Nature, 2025-05-20)
- Bijan Robinson is a max-contract, high-volume back, and Kyle Pitts (TE) projects second on the team in targets. (ESPN; FantasyLife, 2026)

### Inferences

- A run-heavy, play-action offense with a bell-cow back mathematically limits the pass attempts available to spread across the receiver room.
- Concentration favors the alpha: with a thin WR room and a max-extended London, the funnel points his way even as total WR points fall.
- Format read: London is a PPR and standard WR1; Dotson/Zaccheaus are deep-league/best-ball; Branch is dynasty/best-ball speculative.

## Assessment

- Confidence: medium
- Reason: the scheme direction is well supported by Stefanski's track record, but the 2026 pass-volume figure is a projection until games are played.

- What would invalidate this: a pass-heavier lean than Stefanski's Cleveland norm, a London injury, or a secondary receiver seizing clear target volume.
