# Finding: Bijan is a QB-agnostic bell-cow; Brian Robinson threatens his goal-line TDs, not his volume

## Scope

- Unit: running backs
- Area: the Bijan Robinson / Brian Robinson Jr. workload split in the new offense
- Season: 2026
- Player: Bijan Robinson (see [canonical profile](../../../../../../players/bijan-robinson/profile.md))
- Last verified: 2026-08-17

## Finding

The Falcons plan a complementary backfield, not a committee that dilutes Bijan Robinson. Bijan remains the bell-cow and the clear passing-down back; Brian Robinson Jr. is the designated short-yardage and goal-line complement, inheriting the role that produced Tyler Allgeier's vulture touchdowns. The practical fantasy consequence is narrow but real: Brian Robinson threatens Bijan's rushing-touchdown equity specifically, while leaving his early-down volume and receiving role intact.

## Fantasy implication

- Bijan is a top-2 overall pick and one of the safest RB1s in PPR — his roughly 79-catch, 820-yard receiving floor is largely Brian-Robinson-proof.
- The goal-line cession is the one dent: Bijan's rushing TDs already fell from 14 (2024) to 7 (2025), and Brian Robinson now owns that role.
- Brian Robinson is a low-end standalone but a must-roster handcuff with independent goal-line TD equity; any Bijan absence elevates him to RB2/flex.

## Evidence

### Facts

- The AJC reported a complementary plan with Brian Robinson handling short-yardage and goal-line work previously held by Allgeier; no fixed touch percentages were set. (AJC, 2026-07-01)
- Brian Robinson Jr. joined from San Francisco (2025: 100 touches, 425 scrimmage yards); he is a former Washington third-round pick (2022), listed 6-1, 225. (Atlanta Falcons, 2026-07-13)
- Bijan led the backfield in receiving in 2025 (79 receptions, 820 yards) and has posted 365-plus touches in each of the last two seasons; the offense plans to use both backs as extensions of the passing game. (AJC, 2026-07-01; Atlanta Falcons, 2026-07-13)
- Depth behind them: Nathan Carter, Tyler Goodson (special-teams value), and UDFA Cash Jones; Allgeier was traded to Arizona. (Atlanta Falcons, 2026-07-13)

### Inferences

- Brian Robinson is a genuine threat to Bijan's rushing-TD ceiling — the goal-line role is the exact source of vulture scores — but not to his early-down or passing-down usage.
- A Stefanski wide-zone run game with a bell-cow plus a physical closer (the Nick Chubb template) maps onto Bijan (lead/receiving) and Brian Robinson (power/goal line).
- Pass protection on obvious passing downs is likely Bijan's; a dedicated RB pass-protection breakdown was not sourced, so treat that allocation as inference.
