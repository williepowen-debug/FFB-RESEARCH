# Finding: Pitts is a paid core piece in a system expected to emphasize under-center timing and play action

## Scope

- Unit: tight ends, quarterbacks, and play-action pass game
- Area: Kyle Pitts Sr. role projection in the Stefanski/Rees offense
- Season: 2026
- Status: active preseason baseline
- Last verified: 2026-08-17

## Finding

Atlanta's new staff doubled down on Kyle Pitts Sr. after a strong 2025 finish, replacing the franchise tag with a three-year contract through 2028. The staff's quarterback teaching emphasis points toward under-center run game, timing, and play action, all of which can support tight-end targets if Pitts' route participation is high. The key uncertainty is not whether the Falcons value Pitts; it is whether the offense creates consistent routes and high-leverage targets for him alongside Drake London and Bijan Robinson.

## Evidence

### Facts

- Pitts signed a new three-year contract on 2026-06-29, replacing the franchise tag and keeping him with Atlanta through 2028.
- Atlanta's own analysis of the deal said new leadership used the tag window to evaluate Pitts and concluded he belonged beyond 2026.
- The team reported that Pitts caught 88 passes for 928 yards in 2025, was a second-team All-Pro, and led tight ends in receiving yards over the final six weeks.
- Alex Van Pelt's quarterback-room teaching emphasized footwork, rhythm, under-center work, and opening play action from the run game.
- Austin Hooper returned as a veteran tight end familiar with Stefanski from Cleveland.

### Inferences

- Pitts' contract and 2025 finish reduce the risk that he is marginalized, but they do not guarantee elite route rate.
- Hooper can help the offense live in heavier personnel, but he can also cap Pitts if two-tight-end packages become block-heavy.
- Tua Tagovailoa's rhythm passing may help Pitts quickly; Penix's under-center development and health are the swing variables if Penix wins the job later.

## Fantasy implication

- Pitts belongs on the preseason watchlist as a tight-end premium and PPR upside bet, not merely a name-value hold.
- His fantasy ceiling depends on routes per dropback, slot/move usage, and red-zone work more than on the contract headline.
- London remains the clearer target-share leader; Pitts is the leverage piece if the offense uses play action and middle-field concepts heavily.
