# Finding: Atlanta kept the premium skill core but reset the quarterback competition and complementary roles

## Scope

- Unit: quarterback, running back, wide receiver, and tight end
- Area: 2026 offensive role projection under Kevin Stefanski and Tommy Rees
- Season: 2026
- Status: active preseason baseline
- Last verified: 2026-08-17

## Finding

Atlanta's offense is not a talent rebuild. The Falcons retained Bijan Robinson through 2027, extended Drake London through 2030, extended Kyle Pitts Sr. through 2028, and extended left guard Matthew Bergeron through 2030. The reset is at quarterback and complementary roles: Tua Tagovailoa was signed, Michael Penix Jr. is still working back from a 2025 ACL injury, Kirk Cousins was released, and the team added Brian Robinson Jr., Jahan Dotson, Olamide Zaccheaus, Austin Hooper, Jawaan Taylor, and rookie Zachariah Branch.

## Evidence

### Facts

- The Falcons officially signed Tagovailoa on 2026-03-13 and framed the room as a competition, with Penix rehabbing a season-ending knee injury from 2025.
- Stefanski said on 2026-03-30 that the quarterback position would be a competition, but he could not define the exact shape until Penix was healthy.
- Penix remained short of full 11-on-11 clearance as training camp opened on 2026-07-29.
- As of the 2026-08-12 camp report, Tua took the first-team 11-on-11 reps and started the preseason opener while Penix stayed limited to individual and 7-on-7 work; Stefanski declined to name a Week 1 starter.
- London signed a four-year extension through 2030; Pitts signed a three-year contract through 2028; Bergeron signed a four-year extension through 2030. Bijan Robinson's fifth-year option had been exercised through 2027, and in August 2026 he signed a new three-year extension (reported at up to about $75M) as the highest-paid running back in the NFL.
- Atlanta added Dotson, Zaccheaus, Brian Robinson Jr., Hooper, Taylor, and Branch while Tyler Allgeier, Darnell Mooney, KhaDarel Hodge, and Cousins left the 2026 plan.

### Inferences

- Tua has the cleaner short-term projection if Penix remains limited, but the organization has not closed the competition.
- Bijan Robinson remains the highest-confidence fantasy asset because his elite 2025 role and team-control status survived the regime change.
- London is the target-share anchor. Pitts is the tight-end swing piece. Branch is a package/YAC and return-role hypothesis until he earns full-time routes.
- Brian Robinson Jr. is more likely a power complement than a true committee threat, but goal-line allocation must be charted.

## Fantasy implication

- Treat Bijan and London as stable core bets; price quarterback and secondary pass catchers with more uncertainty.
- Tua/Penix status affects passing efficiency, under-center/play-action timing, and red-zone distribution.
- Pitts has upside if route rate and play-action/middle-field usage rise, but he remains usage-sensitive.
- Branch is more appealing in dynasty and best ball than managed redraft until snap share is proven.
