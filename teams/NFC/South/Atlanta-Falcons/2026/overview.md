# Atlanta Falcons - 2026 Stefanski Reset With Retained Skill Core

- Conference/division: NFC South
- Last verified: 2026-08-17
- Scope: changes from the 2025 team to the 2026 preseason roster and staff
- Build tier: Core

Atlanta made a leadership reset without tearing down the fantasy-relevant offensive core. Kevin Stefanski is the new head coach, Ian Cunningham is the new general manager, Matt Ryan is president of football, Tommy Rees is offensive coordinator, and Jeff Ulbrich was retained after a franchise-record defensive sack season. The central 2026 question is whether the new staff can stabilize quarterback play and role distribution around Bijan Robinson, Drake London, and Kyle Pitts Sr.

## Fantasy-relevant snapshot

### Confirmed baseline

- Kevin Stefanski replaced Raheem Morris as head coach, Tommy Rees replaced Zac Robinson as offensive coordinator, and Jeff Ulbrich was retained as defensive coordinator.
- Atlanta officially signed Tua Tagovailoa and released Kirk Cousins. Michael Penix Jr. is returning from a 2025 ACL injury and was still short of full 11-on-11 clearance when training camp opened.
- Bijan Robinson, after his fifth-year option was exercised through 2027, signed a new three-year extension in August 2026 (reported at up to about $75M) as the highest-paid running back in the NFL. Drake London signed an extension through 2030, Kyle Pitts Sr. signed a new three-year deal through 2028, and Matthew Bergeron signed an extension through 2030.
- Major offensive additions: Brian Robinson Jr., Jahan Dotson, Olamide Zaccheaus, Austin Hooper, Jawaan Taylor, and rookie Zachariah Branch.
- Major defensive additions: Azeez Ojulari, Samson Ebukam, Cameron Thomas, Christian Harris, Channing Tindall, Darnay Holmes, Avieon Terrell, Kendal Daniels, Harold Perkins Jr., and Anterio Thompson.
- Major exits: Tyler Allgeier, Kaden Elliss, Darnell Mooney, KhaDarel Hodge, Kirk Cousins, Zane Gonzalez, and Jovaughn Gwyn.

### Working fantasy hypotheses

These are projections to test, not established 2026 outcomes:

- Bijan Robinson remains the highest-confidence fantasy asset; Brian Robinson Jr. is a power complement unless goal-line work shifts more heavily than expected.
- London is the primary target-share anchor. Pitts is a high-leverage tight-end swing if route rate and play-action/middle-field usage rise.
- Tua has the cleaner short-term quarterback path while Penix completes the ACL and under-center transition, but the staff has kept the competition frame open.
- Branch is a package slot/YAC and return-role candidate before he is a bankable weekly fantasy receiver.
- Atlanta D/ST has full-season pressure upside, but early-season edge availability is the risk point with James Pearce Jr. suspended and Jalon Walker's status to monitor.

## Offensive identity

### Confirmed changes

The offense moves from the 2025 Zac Robinson/Raheem Morris structure to a Stefanski/Rees system. Atlanta hired Bill Callahan as offensive line coach and Alex Van Pelt as quarterbacks coach, which reinforces the public offseason emphasis on quarterback footwork, under-center work, run game, and play action.

The roster is built around a retained core rather than a wholesale rebuild. Robinson, London, Pitts, and Bergeron all received team-control or extension signals. The new additions fill specific complementary jobs: Tua for quarterback competition and experience, Brian Robinson Jr. for physical backfield work, Dotson/Zaccheaus/Branch for speed and slot/YAC options, Hooper for tight-end depth, and Taylor for right-tackle help.

### Questions to resolve

- Who wins the quarterback job, and when is Penix fully cleared for 11-on-11 work?
- Does Brian Robinson Jr. take meaningful goal-line work from Bijan?
- Does Pitts earn high route participation in heavier/personnel-diverse packages?
- Does Branch earn enough first-team slot work for managed fantasy relevance, or is he mostly a return/package player?
- Is right tackle stable by Week 1?

The durable offensive baseline, role hypotheses, and fantasy translation are maintained in the [offensive ecosystem module](offense/README.md).

## Defensive matchup profile

### Confirmed changes

The defense has coordinator continuity, not full personnel continuity. Ulbrich was retained after Atlanta set a franchise record with 57 sacks in 2025. The linebacker room changed after Elliss left, with Deablo stepping into the green-dot frame and Harris, Daniels, Perkins, Andersen, and Tindall shaping the competition around him. The front added Ojulari, Ebukam, Thomas, Hand, and Williams while Pearce is unavailable for the first eight games.

### Questions to resolve

- Can Atlanta sustain pressure without Pearce in the first half of the season?
- How quickly does Walker return to a full pass-rush role?
- Does Deablo become an every-down IDP anchor with the green dot?
- Who wins the second linebacker job and the nickel/secondary competition?
- Does Avieon Terrell force immediate coverage-package snaps?

These items affect opponent quarterback pressure, turnover expectation, IDP value, and early-season matchup volatility. The durable defensive baseline is maintained in the [defensive ecosystem module](defense/README.md).

## Coaching and scheme

Atlanta's 2026 coordinator structure is:

| Unit | 2025 | 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Head coach | Raheem Morris | Kevin Stefanski |
| Offense | Zac Robinson | Tommy Rees |
| Defense | Jeff Ulbrich | Jeff Ulbrich |
| Special teams | Marquice Williams | Craig Aukerman |

The analytical frame is offensive reset plus defensive continuity. The offense requires charting because the new system must reconcile a quarterback competition with an elite skill core. The defense has a clearer 2025 identity, but early edge availability and linebacker communication are material uncertainties.

### Analytical projection

The reasonable preseason prior is a Robinson/London-centered offense with Pitts upside and quarterback uncertainty, paired with a defense that is more trustworthy after Pearce returns than during the opening suspension window. Verify through first-team preseason drives, route participation, red-zone work, pressure rates, and linebacker snap shares rather than relying on contract or staff headlines alone.

## Special teams

Atlanta changed the special-teams coordinator to Craig Aukerman and added Nick Folk and Jake Bailey. Branch, Zaccheaus, Goodson, Cash Jones, and other depth skill players should be monitored for return duties. At Core tier, no separate special-teams record is maintained.
