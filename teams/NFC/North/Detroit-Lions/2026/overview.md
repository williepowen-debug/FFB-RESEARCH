# Detroit Lions - 2026 Petzing Transition and Sheppard Year 2

- Conference/division: NFC North
- Last verified: 2026-08-17
- Scope: changes from the 2025 team to the 2026 preseason roster and staff
- Build tier: Core

Detroit remains a premium fantasy ecosystem, but the 2026 file is a transition module. Dan Campbell remains head coach, Drew Petzing replaces John Morton as offensive coordinator, Kelvin Sheppard returns as defensive coordinator, David Montgomery was traded, Jahmyr Gibbs was extended through 2030, and the offensive line is being rebuilt after Graham Glasgow and Taylor Decker were released. The defensive side has elite anchors in Aidan Hutchinson and Jack Campbell, with safety health and complementary pass rush as the main volatility points.

## Fantasy-relevant snapshot

### Confirmed baseline

- Campbell remains head coach, Petzing is the new offensive coordinator, Sheppard remains defensive coordinator, and Dave Fipp remains special teams coordinator.
- Gibbs is signed through 2030 and has a clearer workload path after Montgomery was traded to Houston.
- Goff still has St. Brown, Williams, LaPorta, Gibbs, and Sewell as the offensive core.
- Detroit added Pacheco, Tyler Conklin, Greg Dortch, Cade Mays, Larry Borom, Ben Bartch, Juice Scruggs, and first-round tackle Blake Miller.
- Hutchinson and Jack Campbell are signed through 2030; Kerby Joseph is signed through 2029.
- The defense added Wonnum, Turner, Roger McCreary, Chuck Clark, Christian Izien, and second-round edge Derrick Moore while releasing Josh Paschal and later Terrion Arnold.

### Working fantasy hypotheses

- Gibbs should be projected as the offensive centerpiece, but Pacheco's goal-line and physical-run role must be charted.
- LaPorta has tight-end-friendly coordinator upside from Petzing's background, but Conklin and actual route shares matter.
- St. Brown remains the passing-game floor; Williams remains the vertical ceiling; WR3 and return jobs are open.
- The offensive line is the largest offense-wide risk because Detroit is changing center, left tackle/right tackle alignment, and left-guard competition.
- Detroit D/ST has rebound upside if Hutchinson gets complementary rush and Joseph/Branch are healthy enough to stabilize the back end.

## Offensive identity

### Confirmed changes

Petzing replaces Morton after coordinating Arizona from 2023-25. The official hire announcement emphasized Arizona's rushing efficiency under Petzing and Trey McBride's high-end tight end production. Detroit then traded Montgomery, signed Pacheco, extended Gibbs, added Conklin, and invested first-round capital in Miller.

### Questions to resolve

- Does Gibbs become a true bell-cow or retain a managed explosive-back role?
- Does Pacheco take enough short-yardage work to cap Gibbs' touchdown ceiling?
- Does LaPorta gain target quality in Petzing's tight-end structure, or does Conklin create more rotation?
- Does the offensive line settle quickly enough to keep Goff efficient and Williams viable downfield?
- Who wins WR3 and punt-return roles among Dortch, TeSlaa, Wilson, Law, Kennedy, and others?

The evidence and invalidation tests are maintained in the [offensive research module](offense/README.md).

## Defensive matchup profile

### Confirmed changes

Sheppard remains defensive coordinator and is entering Year 2 with more reported comfort and a more adaptable personnel plan. Hutchinson, Jack Campbell, and Joseph are long-term core pieces. Detroit added veterans and draft picks to the edge, linebacker, corner, and safety rooms, but Joseph and Branch health were still central camp questions.

### Questions to resolve

- Does Derrick Moore earn immediate nickel-rush work next to Hutchinson?
- Do Wonnum or Turner become reliable complementary rushers?
- Are Joseph and Branch ready for full regular-season roles?
- How much nickel/hybrid personnel does Sheppard actually use after a base-heavy 2025 defense?
- Which corners settle around D.J. Reed after Arnold's release and McCreary's addition?

Detroit should be graded as a high-variance early matchup defense: strong front-seven anchors, but weekly pass-defense confidence depends on safety health and corner/nickel stability. See the [defensive research module](defense/README.md).

### Analytical projection

Detroit should remain fantasy-friendly if the offensive line reaches competence quickly. Gibbs, St. Brown, Williams, LaPorta, and Goff are all draftable/useful, but their exact tiers should be reviewed after first-team preseason and Week 1 usage. Defensively, Hutchinson and Campbell are the strongest individual bets; D/ST confidence requires healthy safeties and a verified second rush lane.

## Special teams

Fipp remains special teams coordinator. The main Core-tier special teams note is punt return: Day 1 camp reporting said Detroit was seeking a replacement after Raymond left for Chicago, with St. Brown, Dortch, Dominic Lovett, Kennedy, Lawrence Keys, and Pacheco among players getting looks. This matters only if it determines active status or changes low-volume offensive roles.
