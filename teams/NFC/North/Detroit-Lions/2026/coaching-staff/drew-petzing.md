# Drew Petzing - Detroit Lions

- Role: Offensive coordinator
- Season: 2026
- Joined team: 2026
- Last verified: 2026-08-17

## Responsibilities

Petzing was hired as Detroit's offensive coordinator on 2026-01-26 after three seasons as Arizona's offensive coordinator. He replaces John Morton as the primary offensive transition point for Jared Goff, Jahmyr Gibbs, Amon-Ra St. Brown, Jameson Williams, Sam LaPorta, and a rebuilt offensive line.

## Scheme and tendencies

The official hire announcement says Arizona ranked second in the NFL in rushing average and ninth in rushing yards during Petzing's three years coordinating the Cardinals. It also highlights Trey McBride's 2024-25 Pro Bowl run and 2025 All-Pro season. Detroit's July camp preview described Petzing's concepts as smoothly installed through the offseason program and framed the offense as matchup-based, with tight ends used to create run/pass stress.

## Personnel usage

The immediate usage questions are Gibbs' post-Montgomery workload, Sam LaPorta's target ceiling with Tyler Conklin added and injured to start camp, and whether Petzing keeps St. Brown/Williams as the first two receiving pillars while Greg Dortch, Isaac TeSlaa, Cedrick Wilson, Kendrick Law, and others compete for WR3 and return roles.

## Pace and situational decisions

No Detroit regular-season sample exists for Petzing. Because his Arizona background and Detroit's roster both point toward a strong run game, early charting should split "run-heavy by design" from "efficient balanced offense with condensed/personnel multiplicity." Red-zone grouping with Gibbs, LaPorta, Conklin, and Pacheco is the highest-leverage fantasy watch.

## Fantasy implications

Gibbs is the main winner if Petzing keeps a high rushing-efficiency environment while adding pass-game flexibility. LaPorta has a plausible structural boost because Petzing's official history includes high-end tight end production, but that is an inference until routes and targets confirm it. Goff, St. Brown, and Williams retain strong baselines if line play stabilizes.

## Statements and reporting

Goff told the team site in May that Petzing had his own ideas but was listening to player input during installation. Petzing's OTA media session was reported as mixing Arizona concepts with what Detroit's veterans already do well. Those are intent and practice-installation signals, not regular-season evidence.

## Reliability cautions

Do not overfit Petzing's Arizona tight end output to LaPorta without Detroit route/target evidence. The offensive line has multiple new starters, and Petzing's Lions play-calling sample is still zero regular-season games.
