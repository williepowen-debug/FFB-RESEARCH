# Chicago Bears - 2026 Johnson Continuity and Roster Rebalance

- Conference/division: NFC North
- Last verified: 2026-08-17
- Scope: Core-tier 2026 preseason team module
- Build tier: Core

Chicago enters 2026 as a high-upside fantasy offense with fewer excuses and a more concentrated young skill core. Ben Johnson remains head coach, Press Taylor was promoted from pass game coordinator to offensive coordinator, and Caleb Williams enters another year in the same system after setting a Bears single-season passing record in 2025. DJ Moore's trade is the central target-tree event: Rome Odunze, Luther Burden III, Colston Loveland, Cole Kmet, Kalif Raymond, and rookie Zavion Thomas now determine how concentrated the passing game becomes.

The defense has a different shape. Dennis Allen remains coordinator, but Chicago rebuilt the safety room with Coby Bryant, first-round pick Dillon Thieneman, Cam Lewis, and later Xavier Woods while replacing Tremaine Edmunds with Devin Bush and other linebacker depth. The front has a rebound thesis built on health and development rather than a single new star edge.

## Fantasy-relevant snapshot

### Confirmed baseline

- Johnson remains head coach and offensive authority.
- Taylor replaced Declan Doyle as offensive coordinator after working as 2025 pass game coordinator.
- Williams set a franchise single-season passing record with 3,942 yards in 2025, and Chicago scored 441 points.
- Moore was traded to Buffalo, opening target opportunity for Odunze, Burden, Loveland, Kmet, and depth receivers.
- Swift and Monangai return after each rushed for 750-plus yards in 2025.
- Bradbury replaced retired center Drew Dalman, while Darnell Wright signed an extension through 2031.
- Allen remains defensive coordinator, but safety and linebacker personnel changed materially.

### Working fantasy hypotheses

- Williams can remain a high-ceiling quarterback if the center transition and left-tackle competition stabilize.
- Odunze, Burden, and Loveland are the most important route/target winners to verify after Moore's departure.
- Swift remains the lead back, but Monangai has standalone value if Johnson keeps two-back rushing volume.
- Bears D/ST is a rebound candidate only if the front generates pressure and Bryant/Thieneman settle coverage communication quickly.

## Offensive identity

### Confirmed changes

The Bears kept the offensive system but changed the staff and distribution. Taylor's promotion keeps the passing-game voice in-house. Moore's trade removes a veteran target-earner, and Kalif Raymond/Zavion Thomas add speed and return utility rather than an obvious one-for-one replacement. Loveland's rookie receiving production and the Odunze/Burden development path make the target tree more youth-driven.

The offensive line remains the floor variable. Bradbury is the center replacement, Wright is locked in long-term at right tackle, and left tackle must sort through Braxton Jones, Jedrick Wills Jr., and Ozzy Trapilo's health.

### Questions to resolve

- Do Odunze and Burden both run full starter routes?
- Does Loveland stay a primary pass-game piece with Kmet and rookie Sam Roush also active?
- Does Swift keep receiving and red-zone work, or does Monangai split the valuable touches?
- Can Bradbury and the left-tackle winner keep Williams in structure?

The evidence and invalidation tests are maintained in the [offensive research module](offense/README.md).

## Defensive matchup profile

### Confirmed changes

The back seven was reset. Edmunds, Brisker, Byard, Gardner-Johnson, Owens, and other depth pieces departed, while Bryant, Bush, Lewis, Thieneman, Muhammad, Elliott, and Woods joined or entered the 2026 competition. The defensive line returns Sweat, Booker, Dexter, Jarrett, Odeyingbo, and Turner, with Gallimore, Street, and van den Berg added to the rotation.

### Questions to resolve

- Does Booker become a stable rush partner for Sweat?
- Does Thieneman play deep safety only, or does he get box/nickel snaps that raise IDP value?
- Do Bryant, Jaylon Johnson, and Kyler Gordon stay healthy enough for the new secondary to communicate cleanly?
- Can Allen create pressure without heavy blitz dependence?

Chicago should be treated as a defensive rebound candidate, not an already-proven avoid for opposing fantasy starters. See the [defensive research module](defense/README.md).

Johnson's continuity raises the offensive floor. Taylor's promotion lowers installation risk, but the new target hierarchy still has to be proven. Allen's defense has enough new pieces to improve, yet its early-season matchup profile should be graded by pressure and communication rather than names alone.

### Analytical projection

Chicago starts as a fantasy-positive offense with a crowded but exciting group of young pass catchers and a two-back run game. The draft-market edge is likely in correctly pricing which pass catcher gets Moore's vacated work and whether the defense is improved enough to create more positive scripts without suppressing shootout volume.
