# Finding: Safety reset gives Allen more range but creates cohesion risk

## Scope

- Team/player: Chicago Bears secondary
- Area: safety turnover, coverage roles, matchup profile
- Season/week: 2026 preseason baseline
- Status: active
- Last verified: 2026-08-17

## Finding

Chicago replaced a large share of its 2025 safety room. NFL's departure tracker lists Jaquan Brisker, Kevin Byard, Chauncey Gardner-Johnson, and Jonathan Owens among Bears departures, while the Bears signed Coby Bryant and Cam Lewis, later added Xavier Woods, and drafted Dillon Thieneman at No. 25 overall. Official Bears reporting says Thieneman can play strong safety, free safety, and nickel, and that Bryant is viewed by Allen/Johnson as a tone-setting safety.

The upside is more range and physicality. The risk is that a new safety mix must communicate quickly around Jaylon Johnson and Kyler Gordon returning from injury-affected 2025 seasons.

## Fantasy implication

- Bears D/ST can be drafted as a rebound candidate, not a locked-in weekly starter.
- Opposing passing games remain usable until Chicago proves the new safety communication works at game speed.
- IDP interest centers on Thieneman's alignment; box/nickel snaps would matter more than deep-only usage.
- Bryant's health and role are important for early-season matchup grading.

## Evidence

- The Bears signed Bryant to a three-year deal and Cam Lewis to a two-year deal.
- The Bears selected Thieneman 25th overall and official draft/training-camp coverage emphasized his speed and safety/nickel versatility.
- Official defensive-back preview reporting says Jaylon Johnson and Kyler Gordon are looking to rebound after injury-limited 2025 seasons.
- NFL's departure tracker lists multiple veteran Bears safeties leaving in the 2026 offseason.
