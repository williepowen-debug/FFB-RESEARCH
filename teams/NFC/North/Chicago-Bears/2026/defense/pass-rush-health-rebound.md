# Finding: Pass-rush rebound depends more on health than a new star

## Scope

- Team/player: Chicago Bears defensive front
- Area: pass rush, defensive-line health, matchup pressure
- Season/week: 2026 preseason baseline
- Status: active
- Last verified: 2026-08-17

## Finding

Chicago did not add a headline veteran edge, so the pass-rush thesis rests on internal health and development. Official Bears reporting says the top three 2025 sack leaders return: Montez Sweat, Austin Booker, and Gervon Dexter Sr. Sweat had 10 sacks, 18 quarterback hits, and 13 tackles for loss in 2025; Booker closed 2025 with 3.5 sacks and six quarterback hits in Weeks 15-17 after missing the first seven games; Dexter had six sacks.

The interior rotation adds Neville Gallimore and rookie Jordan van den Berg while expecting healthier seasons from Grady Jarrett, Dayo Odeyingbo, and Shemar Turner. That gives Allen credible depth, but fantasy matchup upgrades require proof that the front can pressure without blitz dependency.

## Fantasy implication

- Bears D/ST has ceiling if Sweat, Booker, Dexter, Jarrett, and Odeyingbo are healthy together.
- Opposing quarterbacks and wide receivers should be downgraded only after pressure rate improves in live games.
- Booker is the key IDP/pressure-sleeper watch because his late-2025 production suggests a larger role.
- If the pass rush stalls, the rebuilt secondary becomes more vulnerable to explosive plays.

## Evidence

- Official defensive-line preview reporting identified Sweat, Booker, and Dexter as the top returning 2025 sack leaders.
- The same preview noted 2025 injuries to Booker, Jarrett, Odeyingbo, and Turner.
- The Bears signed Gallimore and Street in March and drafted van den Berg in Round 6.
- Official camp reporting said Allen liked van den Berg's movement, power, athleticism, effort, toughness, and football character, while cautioning he still has to improve.
