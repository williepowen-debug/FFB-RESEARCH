# Finding: Run-game ceiling depends on center transition and left tackle

## Scope

- Team/player: Chicago Bears run game and offensive line
- Area: Swift/Monangai usage, center replacement, tackle competition
- Season/week: 2026 preseason baseline
- Status: active
- Last verified: 2026-08-17

## Finding

Chicago returns D'Andre Swift and Kyle Monangai after official team reporting said they were the only NFL running back duo in 2025 to each rush for 750-plus yards, helping the Bears finish third in the league with 2,456 rushing yards. Swift set career highs with 1,087 rushing yards and nine rushing touchdowns, while Monangai produced 783 rushing yards and five rushing touchdowns.

The line context changed. Drew Dalman retired, the Bears traded for Garrett Bradbury, Darnell Wright signed an extension through 2031 after a 2025 second-team All-Pro season, and left tackle remains a competition around Braxton Jones, Jedrick Wills Jr., and Ozzy Trapilo's health.

## Fantasy implication

- Swift remains the lead fantasy option because he owns receiving utility and the larger 2025 rushing profile.
- Monangai is more than a handcuff if Johnson keeps a top rushing environment and uses both backs weekly.
- Williams' passing efficiency depends on Bradbury's center transition and left-tackle competence.
- If the line stabilizes quickly, Chicago can support multiple startable fantasy pieces without needing extreme pass volume.

## Evidence

- Official running back preview reporting credited Swift and Monangai with a rare dual-750-yard rushing season.
- Official offensive line preview reporting described Wright as an emerging right-tackle star and highlighted left tackle as an intriguing training-camp battle.
- The Bears officially acquired Bradbury from New England after Dalman's retirement and re-signed Braxton Jones.
- The Bears officially signed Jedrick Wills Jr. and extended Wright.
