# Finding: Moore trade concentrates upside in young pass catchers

## Scope

- Team/player: Chicago Bears passing offense
- Area: target distribution after DJ Moore trade
- Season/week: 2026 preseason baseline
- Status: active
- Last verified: 2026-08-17

## Finding

Chicago traded DJ Moore and a 2026 fifth-round pick to Buffalo for a 2026 second-round pick, removing a veteran receiver who still finished among the Bears' top 2025 receiving-yardage options. Official team reporting says Rome Odunze and Luther Burden III enter camp as key Williams targets, while Colston Loveland led the 2025 Bears with 58 catches, 713 yards, and six touchdowns as a rookie.

The working football inference is that Chicago wants the passing game to tilt toward Odunze, Burden, Loveland, and Cole Kmet rather than carry a veteran-heavy receiver room. Kalif Raymond adds speed and return value, but his profile reads more like field-stretching and best-ball volatility than a replacement for Moore's full role.

## Fantasy implication

- Williams gains a clearer young core but loses Moore's veteran floor.
- Odunze and Burden are the biggest ceiling beneficiaries if their routes become full-time and Johnson keeps explosive-pass volume high.
- Loveland has a strong tight end breakout profile because he already led the team in 2025 receiving yards and is trusted across alignments.
- Kmet's weekly fantasy viability depends on whether 12 personnel creates enough routes for two tight ends rather than turning him into a blocking-heavy complement.

## Evidence

- The Bears officially announced the Moore trade in the same transaction cycle that brought Garrett Bradbury to Chicago.
- Official receiver preview reporting identified Odunze and Burden as key playmakers and trusted Williams targets.
- Official tight end preview reporting said Loveland led the 2025 Bears with 58 receptions, 713 receiving yards, and six receiving touchdowns as a rookie.
- The official staff page and 2026 staff announcement show Johnson/Press Taylor passing-game continuity.
