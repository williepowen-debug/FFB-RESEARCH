# Minnesota Vikings - 2026 Quarterback Reset With Flores Continuity

- Conference/division: NFC North
- Last verified: 2026-08-17
- Scope: 2026 Core-tier roster, staff, offensive, and defensive baseline
- Build tier: Core

Minnesota enters 2026 as a high-variance fantasy environment: Kevin O'Connell and Brian Flores remain in place, the receiving core is still led by Justin Jefferson, Jordan Addison, and T.J. Hockenson, and the defense retains its aggressive coordinator identity. The change is the spine. Kyler Murray was signed into a quarterback competition with J.J. McCarthy, Blake Brandel is the center transition after Ryan Kelly's retirement, Jonathan Greenard was traded, Harrison Smith was released, and the draft leaned heavily into the defensive front and secondary.

## Fantasy-relevant snapshot

### Confirmed baseline

- Kevin O'Connell, Wes Phillips, Brian Flores, and Matt Daniels are retained in the key head coach/coordinator roles.
- Kyler Murray signed with Minnesota on 2026-03-12; J.J. McCarthy, Carson Wentz, and Max Brosmer remain on the official roster.
- Justin Jefferson, Jordan Addison, Jauan Jennings, T.J. Hockenson, Aaron Jones Sr., and Jordan Mason are on the active roster as the primary fantasy skill group.
- Ryan Kelly retired, moving Blake Brandel's center transition into a larger offensive variable.
- Jonathan Greenard was traded to Philadelphia; Harrison Smith, Jonathan Allen, and Javon Hargrave were released.
- Minnesota drafted nine players, including defensive linemen Caleb Banks and Domonique Orange, linebacker Jake Golday, safety Jakobe Thomas, tackle Caleb Tiernan, fullback Max Bredeson, corner Charles Demmings, running back Demond Claiborne, and center Gavin Gerhardt.

### Working fantasy hypotheses

These are projections to test, not established 2026 outcomes:

- Murray is the working QB1 and has superflex upside if he masters O'Connell's system and stays healthy.
- Jefferson remains the target anchor; Addison, Jennings, and Hockenson decide the secondary target and touchdown distribution.
- Jones and Mason begin as a split backfield until one controls passing downs or goal-line work.
- Dallas Turner is the main pass-rush breakout variable after the Greenard trade.
- The defense remains scheme-dangerous under Flores, but the front and safety turnover create a lower floor than the coordinator continuity alone implies.

## Offensive identity

### Confirmed changes

The offense is continuity in coaching but transition at quarterback and center. Murray arrives from Arizona, McCarthy moves from 2025 starter to competitor/contingency, Wentz returns as veteran depth, and Brandel takes first-team center work after Kelly's retirement. Jennings adds a bigger receiver profile behind Jefferson and Addison, while rookie Max Bredeson gives the staff another heavy-personnel option.

### Questions to resolve

- Whether Murray's reported QB1 status is confirmed by the official Week 1 depth chart.
- How quickly Murray handles O'Connell's timing, protection, and progression demands.
- Whether Jennings materially changes Addison's or Hockenson's route and red-zone shares.
- How Jones and Mason divide passing-down, short-yardage, and two-minute usage.
- Whether Brandel's center transition is clean enough to avoid exchange and protection issues.

## Defensive matchup profile

### Confirmed changes

Flores remains defensive coordinator, but the personnel base changed. Greenard's trade removes the 2025 pressure leader; Allen and Hargrave's releases cleared veteran interior snaps; Smith's release ends a long-term safety era. Minnesota responded by drafting Banks, Golday, Orange, Jakobe Thomas, and Demmings in the first five defensive selections and by adding veteran corner James Pierre.

### Questions to resolve

- Whether Turner replaces enough of Greenard's pressure to keep the D/ST ceiling intact.
- How quickly Banks and Orange enter the defensive-line rotation.
- Whether the safety group can preserve Flores' disguise package without Smith.
- How the corner depth holds up behind Byron Murphy Jr., Isaiah Rodgers, and Pierre.

These items affect opponent passing-game and pressure matchups. The durable defensive baseline is maintained in the [defensive ecosystem module](defense/README.md).

## Coaching and scheme

The Vikings return the top coordinator structure:

| Unit | 2025 | 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Head coach | Kevin O'Connell | Kevin O'Connell |
| Offense | Wes Phillips | Wes Phillips |
| Defense | Brian Flores | Brian Flores |
| Special teams | Matt Daniels | Matt Daniels |

Frank Smith is listed as assistant head coach and Ryan Nielsen as defensive running game coordinator. Football operations changed after Kwesi Adofo-Mensah was relieved of GM duties, Rob Brzezinski guided the staff and personnel department through parts of the offseason, and Nolan Teasley was hired by June 2026.

### Analytical projection

The reasonable prior is offense-friendly volatility and defense-friendly volatility. O'Connell gives Murray a premium environment, but there is no regular-season Minnesota sample yet. Flores gives the defense pressure and disguise upside, but the proven front and safety losses need to be replaced on the field.

## Special teams

Matt Daniels remains special-teams coordinator. Andrew DePaola was retained, Johnny Hekker was signed as punter and holder, and Will Reichard is listed on the active roster. Return roles remain open enough to monitor Myles Price, Demond Claiborne, and rookie/UDFA candidates.
