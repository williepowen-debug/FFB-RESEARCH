# Finding: Jefferson anchors the offense while the secondary volume depends on Murray fit and personnel grouping

## Scope

- Team/player: Vikings pass catchers and running backs
- Area: target hierarchy, backfield split, and offensive-line context
- Season/week: 2026 preseason
- Status: active
- Last verified: 2026-08-17

## Finding

The Vikings' skill core is stronger than the quarterback uncertainty suggests. Justin Jefferson and Jordan Addison remain the top receiver pair, T.J. Hockenson is the featured tight end, Jauan Jennings adds a bigger veteran receiver profile, and Aaron Jones Sr. plus Jordan Mason lead the backfield. The main 2026 question is not whether Minnesota has weapons; it is how Murray's timing, Brandel's center transition, and O'Connell's grouping choices distribute the non-Jefferson volume.

## Fantasy implication

Jefferson remains the safest Vikings fantasy asset. Addison profiles as a starting fantasy receiver if Murray supports two wideouts. Hockenson's tight-end value depends on whether Jennings absorbs red-zone and intermediate targets or whether the offense uses more heavy personnel with Josh Oliver and Max Bredeson. Jones and Mason should be treated as a workload split until one back controls passing downs or goal-line snaps.

## Evidence

### Facts

- The official roster lists Jefferson, Addison, Jennings, Hockenson, Jones, Mason, Oliver, and rookie Demond Claiborne as active offensive skill players.
- Vikings.com announced veteran newcomer jersey numbers including Murray, Jennings, and Hekker on 2026-05-06.
- Vikings.com reported that Jefferson worked with both Murray and McCarthy during the spring and observed growth in both quarterbacks.
- The official offensive-line preview said Brandel received first-team center reps throughout OTAs and minicamp and that his chemistry with Murray and McCarthy was a camp watchpoint.
- Daily Norseman preseason analysis noted Jones and Mason were efficient in the first preseason game, while Claiborne carried momentum from camp.

### Inferences

- Jefferson's target command should survive the quarterback transition, but touchdown and explosive-play rates are tied to Murray comfort.
- Jennings is more likely to affect Addison and Hockenson's touchdown/route mix than Jefferson's role.
- The backfield should be charted by high-value touches rather than early-down carries alone.
