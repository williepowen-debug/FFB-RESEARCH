## Durable player profile

### Traits and role fit

Stevenson is a 6-0, 227-pound early-down and short-yardage back with unusual receiving
skill for his size: he led the 2022 Patriots in receptions (69) and averaged 10.8 yards
per catch in 2025. He is a patient, contact-balance runner rather than a breakaway
threat — his career long is 64 yards and his career average is 4.4 per carry. He has
been New England's most-trusted back in pass protection, a role that keeps him on the
field in two-minute and obvious passing situations independent of the carry split.

His weakness is ball security: 17 fumbles (9 lost) in 70 games, including a 2024 stretch
that cost him the starting job.

Three patterns matter.

**Receiving usage peaked in 2022 and has been cut roughly in half since.** Targets by
year: 88, 51, 41, then 37 assigned targets in 2025 (definition note below). The 2025 line was efficient (10.8 yards per catch, 345 yards on 32
grabs) but the volume is a third-down and two-minute role, not a hub.

**Carry volume fell sharply in 2025 as Henderson arrived.** 130 attempts in 14 games
(9.3 per game) against 207 in 2024 (13.8). The offense was better and he was more
efficient — 4.6 per carry, and team-cited 5.7 per carry over the final eight games
including playoffs — but on fewer touches.

**Touchdowns held up on low volume.** Seven rushing touchdowns in each of 2024 and 2025
despite the carry drop, plus two receiving in 2025, which points to retained goal-line
and short-yardage priority.

### Strengths, limitations, and relevant context

Strengths: pass protection, short-yardage and goal-line trust, receiving ability, and a
late-2025 efficiency run that reporting credits for his lead role in the playoff run.

Limitations: fumbles (a 2024 benching), a career 4.4 yards per carry with no season above
5.0, a 2025 toe injury that cost three games, and a per-game workload that has fallen
two straight years.

Context: he signed a four-year, $36 million extension ($17 million guaranteed) on
2024-06-20 running through 2028, so he is the contractual incumbent. On 2024-10-04 head
coach Jerod Mayo announced his demotion for fumbling; he regained the lead role under
Vrabel's staff in 2025.

## Current situation

### Team and depth-chart role

Lead back **by availability** for Week 1. TreVeyon Henderson (ankle) was officially ruled
OUT on 2026-09-08 after DNP on Sunday, Monday, and Tuesday, having not practiced since
2026-08-24 per Mike Reiss (2026-09-06). Behind Stevenson are Corey Kiner (acquired from
Arizona for a 2028 seventh-round pick on cutdown weekend, about five practices with the
team) and Lan Larison, elevated from the practice squad on 2026-09-09 over Hassan
Haskins. Team-site analysis and Doug Kyed independently describe the same depth order.

### Coaching/scheme fit

McDaniels's offense returns intact with a new interior line (Alijah Vera-Tucker at guard,
Jared Wilson moving to center after the Garrett Bradbury trade) and first-round tackle
Caleb Lomu as depth. Andrew Callahan's 2026-09-07 column assesses Stevenson as the only
back the staff should trust in pass protection against Seattle's pressure packages — a
columnist judgment, not measured usage, but consistent with his 2025 role. Fullback
Reggie Gilliam's arrival adds two-back and short-yardage packages that suit Stevenson's
style.

### Health and availability

Available. He carries no Week 1 designation. History: a 2021 concussion, a 2023 ankle
injury that ended his season after 12 games, and a 2025 toe injury (Week 8, three games
missed). He has played 70 of a possible 84 regular-season games (83 percent).

## Fantasy assessment

- Applicable format: all; his receiving and goal-line roles make him more valuable in
  PPR and touchdown-heavy formats than his carry count alone suggests.
- Range of outcomes: while Henderson is out, a volume-secure RB2 with RB1 weeks
  possible in a top offense — the only credible alternatives are a five-practice
  acquisition and a practice-squad elevation. Once Henderson returns, the durable
  expectation is a 1A/1B split in which Stevenson holds short-yardage, protection, and
  two-minute work: roughly 150-190 carries and 35-45 targets over a full season.
- Key upside condition: Henderson's absence extending past Week 1, or Stevenson retaining
  goal-line and two-minute work when both are healthy — the pattern 2025 touchdowns
  suggest.
- Key downside condition: a fumble recurrence (the documented cause of his only career
  demotion), or Henderson returning to a three-down role that pushes Stevenson to a
  pure short-yardage specialist.
- Confidence: medium — role is secure for Week 1 but the healthy-roster split remains
  unresolved, and this assessment uses no 2026 game-play evidence.

## What would change this assessment

- Henderson's Week 2 practice participation — the single most important near-term
  trigger for Stevenson's volume.
- Postgame Week 1 charting of Stevenson versus Kiner: routes run, two-minute snaps, and
  inside-the-five carries. High Stevenson shares are expected and confirm little; any
  meaningful Kiner passing-down or goal-line usage would be a genuine negative signal.
- With both backs healthy, first-unit goal-line and third-down assignments over the
  first four games — the battleground named in the timeshare finding.
- Two or more fumbles in the first month, given the 2024 precedent.
- Any transaction adding a veteran back, which would signal staff concern about depth
  beyond Henderson's return.
