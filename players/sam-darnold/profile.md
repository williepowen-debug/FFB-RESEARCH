# Sam Darnold

## Durable player profile

### Traits and role fit

Darnold is a pocket-first, rhythm-and-timing passer whose production has tracked his
environment more closely than almost any starter of his era. Three organizations (Jets,
Panthers, a 49ers backup year) produced sub-60-percent completion rates and negative
touchdown-to-interception balance; two seasons in Shanahan-tree offenses with a stable
line and an elite primary receiver (Minnesota 2024, Seattle 2025) produced 4,000-plus
yards, 25-plus touchdowns, and a passer rating near 100 in each. He is not a designed
runner: his career rushing high is 222 yards (2021) and he had zero rushing touchdowns
in 2025, so his fantasy value is purely passing yards and passing touchdowns.

He is a two-time Pro Bowl selection (2024, 2025) and the starting quarterback of the
Super Bowl LX champion, which made him the first quarterback from the 2018 draft class
to win a Super Bowl as a starter.

Three patterns matter for projection.

**Volume fell, efficiency held, touchdowns fell.** From Minnesota 2024 to Seattle 2025
his attempts dropped from 545 to 477 (12.5 percent) while completion rate rose to a
career-high 67.7 percent. Passing touchdowns fell from 35 to 25 on the lower volume;
the 2025 rate (5.2 percent of attempts) is close to his career mean, so 2024's 6.4
percent looks like the outlier, not 2025.

**The interception rate never fully normalized.** Fourteen interceptions in 2025 (2.9
percent) after 12 in 2024 (2.2 percent). He has thrown at least 11 in every full season.
This matters in formats that penalize interceptions.

**No rushing floor.** Thirty-five carries for 95 yards and zero touchdowns in 2025. In the
weeks the passing offense stalls there is no secondary scoring channel.

Postseason 2025: three wins including a 19-of-38, 202-yard, one-touchdown line in the
Super Bowl LX win over New England — a game Seattle won on defense and the run game.

### Strengths, limitations, and relevant context

Strengths: two consecutive full 17-game seasons; back-to-back 4,000-yard years; a
proven fit in the Shanahan-tree play-action and timing structure; the league's leading
receiver as his primary target; a returning five-man offensive line.

Limitations: no rushing production; an interception rate that has stayed near 3
percent; a pass volume that Seattle's run-and-defense identity holds down; a 2025
Super Bowl line that shows the ceiling in this offense is not guaranteed even in the
biggest game.

Context: Darnold signed a three-year, $100.5 million contract with Seattle on
2025-03-13 (Over the Cap: $37.5 million fully guaranteed at signing; 2026 cap charge
$37.9 million; 2027 cap charge $44.9 million with a $10 million roster bonus due on the
fifth day of the 2027 league year). The structure makes 2026 a secure starting season
and 2027 a decision point for the team.

## Current situation

### Team and depth-chart role

Unquestioned QB1 entering his second Seattle season. He is listed first on the
official depth chart with no competition, and he carried no injury designation on the
Week 1 report. The offensive line returns all five 2025 starters (Cross, Zabel,
Sundell, Bradford, Lucas), with Bradford upgraded to full practice before Week 1.

See the [Seahawks 2026 overview](../../teams/NFC/West/Seattle-Seahawks/2026/overview.md).

### Coaching/scheme fit

Brian Fleury replaced Klint Kubiak as offensive coordinator on 2026-02-19 after Kubiak
left to become the Raiders' head coach. Seahawks.com (John Boyle) reported the hire was
made with continuity in mind after Seattle finished third in scoring in 2025; Fleury
came from San Francisco, where he coached tight ends and was run-game coordinator, and
Mike Macdonald described the offense during minicamp as a partial evolution of last
year's. Darnold therefore keeps the same structural family that produced his two best
seasons, but Fleury has no regular-season play-calling sample in Seattle. See
[Fleury continuity finding](../../teams/NFC/West/Seattle-Seahawks/2026/offense/fleury-continuity-offense.md).

The pass-catching room is Jaxon Smith-Njigba (extended in March 2026), Cooper Kupp,
Rashid Shaheed (first full Seattle offseason), Tory Horton (see the weekly record for eligibility), and tight end AJ Barner. The backfield lost Kenneth Walker III to
Kansas City; see
[post-Walker backfield](../../teams/NFC/West/Seattle-Seahawks/2026/offense/post-walker-backfield.md).

### Health and availability

Durable across the last two seasons: 34 of 34 regular-season starts in 2024-2025 plus
three 2025 playoff games. Earlier-career absences (2020, 2021, 2022) were injury and
benching related. No 2026 injury or designation reported as of 2026-09-09.

## Fantasy assessment

- Applicable format: all; most valuable in superflex and two-QB formats where a stable
  4,000-yard passer with 25-plus touchdowns is a weekly starter. In one-QB formats he
  is a high-floor QB2 or streaming QB1 because he has no rushing channel.
- Range of outcomes: central expectation of roughly 3,900-4,200 passing yards and
  23-28 touchdowns if he stays healthy, with 12-15 interceptions. Ceiling requires the
  2024-style touchdown rate, which the 2025 offense did not reproduce.
- Key upside condition: Fleury's offense raising pass attempts back toward 520-plus
  while Smith-Njigba and Shaheed both stay healthy and Barner's route rate grows, which
  would add both volume and red-zone targets.
- Key downside condition: Seattle leaning even harder on defense and the run with a
  rookie-led backfield, holding attempts near 460-480, plus an interception rate near 3
  percent; or any offensive-line injury after two seasons of unusual continuity.
- Confidence: high on role and availability; medium on volume under Fleury; low on the
  touchdown rate reproducing 2024.

## Team history

- 2018-2020: New York Jets. Selected third overall in the 2018 NFL Draft out of USC;
  signed a four-year rookie contract on 2018-07-30.
- 2021-2022: Carolina Panthers. Acquired by trade on 2021-04-05 for a 2021 sixth-round
  pick and 2022 second- and fourth-round picks.
- 2023: San Francisco 49ers. Signed a one-year contract on 2023-03-16 as a backup.
- 2024: Minnesota Vikings. Signed a one-year, $10 million contract on 2024-03-13;
  started all 17 games and made his first Pro Bowl.
- 2025-present: Seattle Seahawks. Signed a three-year, $100.5 million contract on
  2025-03-13; won Super Bowl LX and made his second Pro Bowl.

## What would change this assessment

- Team pass attempts per game over the first four weeks: above roughly 32 raises his
  ceiling toward the 2024 line; below 27 pushes him to a volume-limited QB2.
- Interceptions at or above one per game through Week 4, which would signal the 2025
  rate was not the floor.
- Any multi-week absence for Smith-Njigba, which removes the one receiver who has
  driven both of his 4,000-yard seasons' target concentration.
- An offensive-line injury among the five returning starters; his 2018-2021 record is
  the template for what happens behind a poor line.
- Any 2027 contract restructure or roster-bonus decision would be a durable-signal
  change for dynasty formats but does not affect 2026.
