## Durable player profile

### Traits and role fit

Charbonnet is a between-the-tackles, contact-balance runner and a capable receiver
whose NFL value has been as the second back and goal-line finisher behind Kenneth
Walker III. His college production was elite on volume (1,137 and 1,359 rushing yards
in his two UCLA seasons at 5.6 and 7.0 yards per carry, with 37 receptions in 2022),
but his NFL efficiency has been ordinary: 4.3, 4.2, and 4.0 yards per carry across
three seasons. What he has done is score: 12 rushing touchdowns on 184 carries in 2025
with zero starts, the clearest goal-line-back profile on the roster.

Three patterns matter.

**Carries rose every year, efficiency fell every year.** 108, 135, 184 attempts at 4.3,
4.2, 4.0 yards per carry. He was never the explosive back; Walker was.

**Touchdowns are the asset.** 8 and then 12 rushing touchdowns in the last two seasons
on a backup's carry count. Twelve scores on 184 carries (6.5 percent) is a goal-line
role, and it was earned with zero starts in 2025.

**Receiving usage collapsed in 2025.** From 42 receptions in 2024 to 20 in 2025 as
Seattle's offense funneled targets to Smith-Njigba and Barner. His PPR case weakened
even as his touchdown case strengthened.

Durability before the injury: 49 of 51 possible regular-season games, no fumbles in
three seasons.

### Strengths, limitations, and relevant context

Strengths: proven goal-line finisher; zero career fumbles; three-down capability shown
in 2024 (42 catches); familiarity with the Shanahan-tree structure the new
coordinator keeps.

Limitations: a torn ACL suffered 2026-01-19 in the divisional-round win over San
Francisco, with surgery in February 2026; sub-4.3 yards per carry every NFL season; a
first-round rookie drafted into his absence; a contract year (2023 second-round rookie
deal runs through 2026).

Context: Seattle placed him on the active/PUP list 2026-07-22 and moved him to
reserve/PUP at the 2026-08-30 cutdown, which requires him to miss at least the first
four games. A practice or activation date has not been announced. Macdonald said in July that
he was doing a great job in recovery, with no timetable set.

### Coaching/scheme fit

Fleury's offense is a continuity build from the Kubiak system in which Charbonnet
scored 12 touchdowns, so scheme fit is not the question; timing and the rookie's
performance are. If Price establishes the lead role in Weeks 1-4, Charbonnet's most
likely re-entry point is the goal-line and short-yardage role he already held. See
[Fleury continuity finding](../../teams/NFC/West/Seattle-Seahawks/2026/offense/fleury-continuity-offense.md).

### Health and availability

Out through at least Week 4 on reserve/PUP (torn ACL 2026-01-19, surgery February
2026). The team has published no return timetable.
An ACL return roughly eight to nine months after surgery is on the aggressive side for
a running back, so the first eligible week should not be assumed to be the actual
return week.

## Fantasy assessment

- Applicable format: dynasty and deep redraft benches now; a touchdown-dependent
  flex in standard scoring once active. Best value in leagues with injured-reserve
  slots.
- Range of outcomes: from a Week 5-8 return into a goal-line committee role (6-10
  carries, high touchdown rate, few catches) to a lead role by November if Price
  struggles or is hurt; to a season-long depth role if Price seizes the job and the
  knee delays him.
- Key upside condition: Price failing to secure goal-line or passing-down work in
  Weeks 1-4, leaving both roles open at Charbonnet's return; or a Price injury.
- Key downside condition: Price producing as a three-down back before Week 5 and
  Charbonnet returning slowly, making him the third option in a run-heavy but
  concentrated backfield.
- Confidence: high that he misses Weeks 1-4; low on the return date; low on the
  post-return share.
