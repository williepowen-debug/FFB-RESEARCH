## Durable player profile

### Traits and role fit

Brown is a 6-1, 226-pound outside receiver whose game is built on contested catches,
yards after the catch, and intermediate-to-deep route winning rather than pure speed. He
has produced 1,000 receiving yards in six of seven NFL seasons, and his 34 career
100-yard games since 2019 trail only Justin Jefferson (37) and Davante Adams (35) over
that span. He is a three-time Pro Bowler and three-time second-team All-Pro, holds the
NFL record for consecutive games with 125-plus receiving yards (six, in 2023), and set
the Eagles' single-season receiving-yards record (1,496 in 2022).

He is a true X receiver who earns targets by design: 121 to 158 targets in each of his
four Philadelphia seasons despite sharing the field with DeVonta Smith and a run-heavy
offense.

Rushing is negligible (5 career carries, 70 yards, 1 TD, all in 2019-21). Postseason:
13 games, 44 receptions, 623 yards, 5 TD, including a Super Bowl LIX touchdown in the
Eagles' 40-22 win over Kansas City.

Three patterns matter.

**Volume has been elite and stable when healthy.** Per-game targets: 8.5 (2022), 9.3
(2023), 7.5 (2024), 8.1 (2025). Even his 2025 "down" year — publicly frustrating enough
that he aired it on a livestream after Week 10 — produced 1,003 yards and 8.1 targets per
game in a run-first offense.

**Yards per reception has declined four straight years** (17.0, 13.7, 16.1, 12.9) as
Philadelphia's passing game grew more conservative. This is at least partly scheme; the
2026 offense is the test of whether it is also aging.

**Availability has slipped.** He has missed 3, 0, 0, 4, and 2 games in his last five
seasons — a 2021 chest injury (IR), a 2024 hamstring (three games), and a 2025 hamstring
aggravation (two games plus the bye). He also missed the 2023 playoffs with a sprained
knee. He turned 29 on 2026-06-30.

### Strengths, limitations, and relevant context

Strengths: consistent alpha-level target share, contested-catch dominance, six 1,000-yard
seasons in seven, and a red-zone body type; 56 career touchdowns.

Limitations: soft-tissue history in 2024 and 2025, a declining yards-per-catch trend,
and an age-29 season in a new system with a new quarterback.

Context: the Eagles traded Brown in a cap-motivated deal executed after 4 p.m. on
2026-06-01 so the dead-money hit would split across 2026 and 2027. The trade reunites him
with Mike Vrabel, his Titans head coach from 2019-21, who publicly opposed Tennessee's
2022 trade of Brown. He remains on the three-year, $96 million extension he signed with
Philadelphia on 2024-04-25, which runs through 2029.

## Current situation

### Team and depth-chart role

Projected WR1 and clear first read. The 53-man receiver room (2026-08-30) is Brown,
Romeo Doubs, DeMario Douglas (slot), Mack Hollins, Kyle Williams, and Efton Chism III.
Stefon Diggs was released in March and Kayshon Boutte was traded to Houston on
2026-08-25, removing the two largest 2025 veteran target shares. Brown carries no
designation on the Week 1 injury report (2026-09-08). The Week 1 FanDuel receiving-yards
line was 62.5 — a market reference, not evidence.

### Coaching/scheme fit

Josh McDaniels's system has run both high-concentration and distributed passing games
depending on personnel; the Patriots have not had a receiver of Brown's profile in this
era. The team site reports Brown and Maye's lockers are adjacent and describes Brown
speaking to the team in a spring meeting — culture reporting, not usage evidence. The
scheme-fit question is whether McDaniels's red-zone and third-down menus route through
Brown, which is what would lift both his touchdown total and Maye's. See the
[McDaniels coaching profile](../../teams/AFC/East/New-England-Patriots/2026/coaching-staff/josh-mcdaniels.md).

### Health and availability

Available for Week 1 with no designation. The relevant durable risk is the hamstring
history from 2024 and 2025; there is no reported 2026 recurrence as of 2026-09-09.

## Week 1 2026 — measured usage

*New England at Seattle, 2026-09-09. Seattle won 13-10.*

**INJURED — left the game and did not return. 31 of 71 offensive snaps (44%), 3 receptions on 4 targets for 26 yards.**

Brown was injured on a third-and-4 incompletion at 13:30 of the third quarter — that incompletion
is his fourth target, not a drop or a coverage failure. The gamebook lists him Questionable at 12:41
and **Out to start the fourth quarter**.

**No prognosis exists.** A high ankle sprain has been widely reported, but that diagnosis originates
with NFL Network — not a registered source — and head coach Vrabel **explicitly declined to confirm
it**, said further testing was needed, promised an update the following week, and said he did not
know whether Brown would be available for Week 2. Circulating "3-to-6 week" and "2-to-6 week"
figures are general expectations for the injury class, **not reported timelines for this player**.

**His full-game line understates him.** Restricted to the snaps he actually played, Brown led the
team with 4 targets to DeMario Douglas's 3 — roughly a 31% share of the 13 targets thrown in that
window — and led in receptions and yards when he exited. His fourth-place finish in team targets is
an artefact of missing the final 24 minutes and is not evidence against his alpha-WR1 role.

## Fantasy assessment

- Applicable format: all; the target-volume case is strongest in full PPR, and the
  contested-catch and red-zone profile keeps him relevant in standard.
- Range of outcomes: a high floor built on the most secure target share on the roster
  and a top-five real-world quarterback. Central expectation is roughly 120-140 targets,
  80-90 receptions, 1,100-1,300 yards, and 7-10 touchdowns if he plays 16-17 games —
  a WR1 in every format. The ceiling is a top-three receiver season if the offense
  concentrates around him as the hierarchy finding hypothesizes.
- Key upside condition: measured target share above 25 percent with defined red-zone
  priority in the first four games, confirming `ne-2026-off-q01` and `q02`.
- Key downside condition: a distributed McDaniels passing game that treats Brown, Doubs,
  Douglas, and Henry as near-equals, or a hamstring recurrence that costs games in a
  17-game format.
- Confidence: high on role and target quality; medium on the size of the share until
  charted.

## What would change this assessment

- Target share over the first four 2026 games: 25 percent or more confirms the alpha
  hypothesis; below 20 percent means the offense is distributing and his projection
  drops toward a high-end WR2.
- Red-zone target count relative to Henry and Doubs — the specific mechanism for the
  touchdown ceiling.
- Any hamstring-related practice limitation, given two consecutive seasons with missed
  games from the same injury.
- Yards per reception continuing to fall below 13 in a new system, which would indicate
  the decline is player rather than scheme.
- A quarterback injury; his projection is more quarterback-dependent than his Eagles
  seasons were because Maye is the offense's engine.
