# James Cook III

- Position: RB
- Current team: Buffalo Bills
- Season: durable profile (verified 2026-08-20)
- Last verified: 2026-08-20
- Status: active

## Durable player profile

### Traits and role fit

Cook is an explosive, one-cut zone runner whose value comes from efficiency and
big-play rate rather than short-yardage mass. He has averaged at least 4.7 yards per
carry in every NFL season and 5.1 for his career across 842 attempts — a rare
combination of volume and efficiency. He is a competent receiver with soft hands, but
he has never been deployed as a true passing-down hub, and his profile is explosive
runner first, pass-catcher second. Buffalo drafted him in the second round in 2022 out
of Georgia.

Honors: three consecutive Pro Bowls (2023, 2024, 2025); Second-Team All-Pro (2025);
NFL rushing yards leader in 2025 (1,621) and NFL leader in rushing yards per game
(95.4); tied for the NFL rushing-touchdown lead in 2024 (16). No. 20 on the NFL Top
100 Players of 2026.

Three patterns matter more than the totals.

**Touchdown output is volatile and not workload-driven.** Cook scored 2 rushing
touchdowns on 237 carries in 2023, then 16 on *fewer* carries (207) in 2024, then 12
on a career-high 309 in 2025. Touchdown totals in this offense are a function of
where Josh Allen is used near the goal line, not of Cook's carry count. Any
projection that scales Cook's touchdowns linearly with volume is mis-specified.

**Receiving usage peaked in 2023 and has been flat since.** Receptions by year:
21, 44, 32, 33. His best receiving season was his second, under a different
offensive arrangement. Two consecutive years at 32-33 receptions establish a role,
not a fluke, which is the relevant base rate against any stated plan to expand it.

**2025 was a genuine volume leap with an efficiency gain, not just more carries.**
309 attempts (third in the NFL) at 5.2 yards per carry, 962 yards after contact
(fourth), 342 total touches, 1,912 scrimmage yards, and nine 100-yard rushing games
including a 216-yard, two-touchdown game against Carolina in Week 8. He also recorded
24 goal-line carries (eighth in the NFL) and a reported red-zone touch share near
40 percent — both materially higher than his earlier seasons.

### Strengths, limitations, and relevant context

Strengths: elite per-carry efficiency sustained across a 300-plus carry season,
genuine breakaway speed, contact balance beyond his listed size, and four seasons of
availability (66 of a possible 67 regular-season games).

Limitations: a receiving role that has not grown in two years, touchdown output
hostage to his quarterback's goal-line usage, and a ball-security regression in 2025
(6 fumbles, up from 1 in 2024 — note this is total fumbles, not fumbles lost).
The 2025 workload itself (342 touches) is the standard post-heavy-usage attrition
concern.

Context: Cook signed an extension ahead of the 2025 season, before his rushing title.
The 2025 training-camp hold-in is resolved and he is now widely described as one of
the league's underpaid backs — a contract posture that reduces holdout risk and
raises the team's incentive to use him heavily.

## Current situation

### Team and depth-chart role

Unquestioned lead back with no committee threat. In 2025 Cook took 309 of the
backfield's carries against 58 for Ray Davis and 50 for Ty Johnson; 151 of Davis's
275 rushing yards came in a Week 18 game in which Cook sat after the second offensive
snap. Davis's standing value to the team is as the kickoff returner, where he led the
NFL in return average (32.4). Frank Gore Jr. rounds out the room. Buffalo added no
running back by trade, draft, or free agency in the 2026 offseason.

See [Bills 2026 backfield record](../../teams/AFC/East/Buffalo-Bills/2026/offense/backfield-and-cook-role-002.md).

### Coaching/scheme fit

Joe Brady — the coordinator under whom Cook's production developed — was promoted to
head coach for 2026 and is confirmed as the play-caller, preserving the exact scheme
relationship. New offensive coordinator Pete Carmichael has stated an intent to use
Cook more extensively as a receiver, with angle routes and motion-adjusted deeper
concepts described. That is stated intent, not charted usage, and it runs against a
two-year 32-33 reception base rate.

The blocking environment is the live variable: center Connor McGovern has been out
since 2026-08-08 with a lower-body injury and left guard is unresolved between Alec
Anderson and Austin Corbett. See
[interior offensive line](../../teams/AFC/East/Buffalo-Bills/2026/offense/interior-offensive-line.md).

### Health and availability

Durable. Cook has played 66 of 67 possible regular-season games across four seasons
and started all 17 in 2025. He participated fully in 2026 training camp and played
both first-team possessions in the 2026-08-15 preseason opener (five carries, 21
yards) without incident. No injury designation is reported as of 2026-08-20.

## Fantasy assessment

- Applicable format: all; the assessment below is most sensitive in full PPR.
- Range of outcomes: a high floor built on a secure, league-leading rushing workload
  in a top-five scoring offense; a ceiling constrained by receiving volume and by
  Josh Allen's goal-line rushing. Reasonable central expectation is regression from
  1,621 rushing yards toward roughly 1,300-1,450 with double-digit touchdowns, which
  remains top-tier production.
- Key upside condition: Carmichael's stated receiving expansion actually lands. Moving
  from 33 receptions to the mid-50s is worth more than 20 points in full PPR and is
  the only realistic path to a positional overall-RB1 finish. The secondary upside is
  the continued rise in goal-line share (24 goal-line carries, ~40 percent red-zone
  touch share in 2025) at Allen's expense.
- Key downside condition: touchdown regression from 12 toward his 2023 baseline while
  receiving volume stays flat, compounded by early-season efficiency loss behind a
  reshuffled interior line against the league's hardest opening RB schedule.
- Confidence: high on role and workload; medium on efficiency retention; low on the
  magnitude of any receiving increase.

In a league with a per-game 100-yard rushing bonus, Cook is systematically underpriced
relative to generic PPR ranking: he produced nine such games in 2025. In leagues whose
yardage bonuses are receiving-only, the reverse holds — his career single-game
receiving high is 83 yards, so he collects a 100-yard receiving bonus at a rate
indistinguishable from zero. See

## What would change this assessment

- A route participation or target rate in the first four 2026 games that clearly
  exceeds or clearly matches the 2025 baseline — this resolves the central open
  question in either direction.
- Buffalo shifting goal-line carries toward or away from Allen.
- A McGovern setback or an unresolved left guard extending into the regular season.
- Any contraction of Cook's early-down share, which no current evidence supports.
- A recurrence of the 2025 fumble rate, which would be the most plausible cause of a
  genuine role reduction.
