# Drake Maye

- Position: QB
- Current team: New England Patriots

- Last verified: 2026-09-09

## Durable player profile

### Traits and role fit

Maye is a big-framed (6-4, 225) quarterback whose two defining traits are accuracy and
off-script mobility. He led the NFL in completion percentage (72.0) and passer rating
(113.5) in 2025, his first full season as a starter, and he is a genuine rushing threat —
103 carries for 450 yards in 2025, plus 66 and 65 rushing yards in the Wild Card and AFC
Championship games. His rushing is scramble- and situation-driven rather than a designed
goal-line package: four rushing touchdowns in 2025 against 31 passing.

He is the third overall pick of the 2024 draft out of North Carolina and was the 2025 NFL
MVP runner-up (361 points to Matthew Stafford's 366), a second-team All-Pro, and a two-time
Pro Bowler (2024 as a replacement, 2025 on the initial ballot). Players ranked him 11th on
the NFL Top 100 of 2026.

2025 postseason (4 games, 3-1): 70 of 120 for 828 yards, 6 TD, 4 INT, 21 sacks; 29 carries
for 178 yards and 1 TD. He completed 27 of 43 for 295 yards, 2 TD, and 2 INT with six sacks
and a lost fumble in the 29-13 Super Bowl LX loss to Seattle.

Three patterns matter.

**The 2024-to-2025 jump was efficiency, not just volume.** Attempts per game rose from
26.0 to 28.9, but yards per attempt went from 6.7 to 8.9 and touchdown rate from 4.4 to
6.3 percent. The Week 17 line against the Jets (19 of 21, 256 yards, 5 TD) produced the
highest single-game QBR in that metric's history.

**Sacks and fumbles are the durable weakness.** Maye was sacked 47 times in 2025 (an 8.0
percent sack rate, seventh-highest of 33 qualifiers per the team site) and 21 more times in
four playoff games; he fumbled 15 times including playoffs. The Super Bowl exposed
cover-zero pressure specifically — his EPA per dropback against cover zero ranked 20th of
33.

**Rushing is real but not a touchdown engine.** 871 career rushing yards on 157 carries
(5.5 per carry) but only 6 rushing touchdowns in 30 games. He is not a Josh Allen-style
goal-line runner; his rushing floor is yards, not scores.

### Strengths, limitations, and relevant context

Strengths: league-best accuracy in 2025, arm talent to all levels, scramble yardage,
a low interception rate (1.6 percent in 2025), and a first-time repeat coordinator.

Limitations: sack and fumble volume tied to his play-extension style, a two-season sample
with one elite year, and a Super Bowl performance that showed pressure-package
vulnerability.

Context: 2026 is his first season since high school with the same offensive coordinator
two years running (Longo and Lindsey at UNC; Van Pelt in 2024; McDaniels in 2025 and
2026). He signed a four-year, fully guaranteed $36.64 million rookie contract on
2024-05-28, so he is under contract through 2027 with a fifth-year option.

## Current situation

### Team and depth-chart role

Unquestioned franchise quarterback. The 53-man roster carries Tommy DeVito as the top
backup and seventh-round rookie Behren Morton as the emergency third quarterback. Maye
carries no injury designation on the Week 1 report (2026-09-08). The Patriots open
2026-09-09 at Seattle in a Super Bowl rematch.

### Coaching/scheme fit

Year two under Josh McDaniels (2025 AP Assistant Coach of the Year) and quarterbacks coach
Ashton Grant. Team-site reporting (2026-09-08) describes the staff pushing Maye toward
full line-of-scrimmage control of the offense — protection adjustments and getting out of
bad plays — with the Super Bowl cover-zero problem as the named case study. His
supporting cast changed materially: A.J. Brown arrived by trade on 2026-06-01 and Romeo
Doubs by free agency on 2026-03-12, while Stefon Diggs was released and Kayshon Boutte
traded (2026-08-25). Hunter Henry returns at tight end. See the
[2026 team overview](../../teams/AFC/East/New-England-Patriots/2026/overview.md), the
[receiving hierarchy finding](../../teams/AFC/East/New-England-Patriots/2026/offense/receiving-hierarchy.md),
and the [McDaniels coaching profile](../../teams/AFC/East/New-England-Patriots/2026/coaching-staff/josh-mcdaniels.md).
The open hypothesis is `ne-2026-off-q08` in the
[offense hypotheses file](../../teams/AFC/East/New-England-Patriots/2026/offense/hypotheses.csv):
Maye takes another step with the upgraded cast.

### Health and availability

Durable to date. One missed stretch: a Week 8 2024 concussion. He started all 17 games and
four playoff games in 2025. No 2026 injury or designation as of 2026-09-09. The structural
availability risk is exposure to hits from his sack rate and scrambling, not a documented
injury history.

## Fantasy assessment

- Applicable format: all; most valuable in superflex and two-QB formats and in leagues
  paying per completion or passing yard, where his accuracy-driven profile compounds.
- Range of outcomes: 2025 was a top-three real-world season. A realistic central
  expectation is modest regression from a 72.0 completion rate and 6.3 percent touchdown
  rate toward roughly 68 percent and 5.5 percent, offset by more attempts if the offense
  leans on Brown. That still lands as a QB1 in one-quarterback formats; the ceiling is
  overall QB1 if the touchdown rate holds with a true alpha receiver.
- Key upside condition: Brown consolidating targets and red-zone looks, raising the
  touchdown ceiling without requiring more attempts — the mechanism described in the
  receiving hierarchy finding.
- Key downside condition: sack and fumble volume persisting against pressure packages,
  which caps drives and produces negative plays; or regression in touchdown rate without
  a compensating rise in volume.
- Confidence: high on role and availability; medium on repeating 2025 efficiency.

## Team history

## What would change this assessment

- Sack rate through the first four 2026 games: below roughly 6 percent confirms the
  year-two protection-control improvement the staff described; above 8 percent again says
  the pressure problem is structural.
- A fumble total on pace to exceed 2025's 15 (including playoffs), the most likely cause
  of a coaching-driven change in how much he extends plays.
- A.J. Brown's target share: if Brown draws 25 percent or more of targets over the first
  four games, the touchdown-ceiling case strengthens; a fully distributed profile weakens
  it.
- Any expansion of designed quarterback runs near the goal line, which would raise his
  floor materially — no current evidence supports this.
- Pass attempts per game: 2025 averaged 28.9; a sustained move above 32 would mark a
  shift toward a pass-first identity around Brown.
