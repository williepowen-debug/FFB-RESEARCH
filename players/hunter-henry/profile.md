# Hunter Henry

## Durable player profile

### Traits and role fit

Henry is a 6-5, 249-pound traditional in-line tight end whose fantasy value has always
come from red-zone and chain-moving targets rather than yardage or explosiveness. His
career average is 11.6 yards per reception with a long of 59 (as a rookie); he has 47
touchdowns in 136 games. He was the 2015 John Mackey Award winner at Arkansas and the
first tight end selected in the 2016 draft (35th overall).

In New England he has been the quarterback's safety valve on third down and in the red
zone — nine touchdowns from rookie Mac Jones in 2021, and career highs in receptions
(66, 2024) and yards (768, 2025) once Drake Maye arrived.

Three patterns matter.

**Target volume rose with Maye.** Targets by New England season: 75, 59, 61, 97, 87. The
two Maye seasons are his two highest-target seasons as a Patriot and two of his three
highest ever.

**Touchdowns are volatile and not volume-driven.** 9, 2, 6, 2, 7 across his five New
England seasons. His 97-target 2024 produced two scores; his 75-target 2021 produced nine.
Touchdown projections for Henry should be anchored on red-zone target share, not total
targets.

**Availability has been excellent since 2021** after a fragile Chargers tenure. He has
played 81 of 85 possible regular-season games in New England, including all 17 in 2021,
2022, and 2025.

### Strengths, limitations, and relevant context

Strengths: red-zone and third-down trust from a top quarterback, reliable hands (three
career fumbles), in-line blocking that keeps him on the field, and durability since 2021.

Limitations: no yards-after-catch or seam-stretching element, an age-31 season (born
1994-12-07), and a receiver room that has been upgraded around him in a way that
historically pulls tight-end targets down.

Context: the Patriots extended him on 2026-08-10 for two years (base $16 million, up to
$20 million, $14.5 million guaranteed) through 2028, the third contract he has signed
with the team. That is an organizational statement that he remains the TE1.

## Current situation

### Team and depth-chart role

TE1. The 53-man tight end and fullback group (2026-08-30) is Henry, third-round rookie
Eli Raridon (Notre Dame), undrafted rookie Tanner Arkin, and fullback Reggie Gilliam.
Austin Hooper left for Atlanta in free agency; blocking tight end Julian Hill, signed
2026-03-12, was placed on injured reserve on 2026-06-01. Henry carries no designation on
the Week 1 injury report (2026-09-08).

The receiving environment changed materially: A.J. Brown (trade, 2026-06-01) and Romeo
Doubs (free agency, 2026-03-12) arrived; Stefon Diggs was released and Kayshon Boutte
traded (2026-08-25). The governing hypothesis is `ne-2026-off-q05` (Henry retains a
steady middle-field and red-zone role) in the
[offense hypotheses file](../../teams/AFC/East/New-England-Patriots/2026/offense/hypotheses.csv);
the [receiving hierarchy finding](../../teams/AFC/East/New-England-Patriots/2026/offense/receiving-hierarchy.md)
places him as the steady secondary option whose share depends on how much Brown and the
slot absorb.

### Coaching/scheme fit

McDaniels's system has historically featured the tight end heavily when the personnel
supported it. Henry's 2025 role — a career-high 768 yards on 87 targets — was earned in
year one of that system with a thinner receiver room. Whether Brown's arrival
concentrates targets away from him or simply displaces Diggs's and Boutte's share is the
open question; no 2026 charting exists.

### Health and availability

Available with no designation. Injury history is front-loaded: a lacerated kidney (IR,
2017-12-19), a torn ACL in 2018 OTAs (missed the season), and a tibial plateau fracture
in 2019 (missed four games). Since 2021 he has missed four games total.

## Fantasy assessment

- Applicable format: all; most valuable in tight-end-premium and PPR formats where his
  reception floor is rewarded; touchdown-dependent in standard.
- Range of outcomes: a floor of roughly 55-65 targets and 40-45 receptions if the
  offense concentrates around Brown, a ceiling near his 2024-25 level (85-95 targets,
  60-plus receptions, 700-plus yards) if Brown mostly displaces the departed veterans.
  Touchdowns between 3 and 8 are all plausible at either volume level.
- Key upside condition: retained red-zone priority. Seven touchdowns in 2025 came with
  Maye as the passer and no alpha receiver; if Henry still draws the second red-zone
  look behind Brown, he is a top-eight tight end.
- Key downside condition: Brown and Doubs together absorbing the intermediate middle and
  red-zone targets, dropping Henry to a 2022-23 volume level (around 60 targets).
- Confidence: medium — role is secure, but target share in the reshaped room is
  unmeasured.

## Team history

- 2016-2020: San Diego / Los Angeles Chargers. Selected in the second round (35th
  overall) of the 2016 NFL Draft out of Arkansas; four-year, $6.38 million rookie
  contract signed 2016-06-02. Franchise-tagged on 2020-03-13 and played 2020 on the tag.
- 2021-present: New England Patriots. Signed a three-year, $37.5 million free-agent
  contract on 2021-03-19; a three-year extension on 2024-03-08; and a two-year extension
  (up to $20 million, $14.5 million guaranteed, through 2028) on 2026-08-10.

## What would change this assessment

- Route participation over the first two 2026 games: routes on a clear majority of
  dropbacks confirms `q05`; a drop toward half of dropbacks means Raridon or 12-personnel
  usage is eroding his role.
- Red-zone target count relative to Brown and Doubs over the first four games — the
  touchdown mechanism.
- Target share: above 15 percent tracks his 2024-25 level; below 11 percent is the
  2022-23 level.
- Any increase in Raridon's snap share, which is the named disconfirming evidence in the
  hypotheses file.
- A Maye injury; Henry's production has been quarterback-sensitive across his career.
