### Why this record exists

### Traits and role fit

A strong-armed pocket passer drafted in the second round in 2019, Lock has had repeated opportunities
as a starter without holding a job. The recurring pattern across his career is volatile
decision-making: his most extensive season produced 16 touchdowns against 15 interceptions. His
value to a team has consistently been as a capable veteran backup who can run an offense
competently on short notice rather than as a long-term starter.

**Durable questions this profile does not yet answer:** whether he sustains efficiency across
multiple starts, and how a Fleury offense changes its distribution with him under center rather
than Darnold. One relief appearance cannot resolve either.

## Current situation

Seattle brought Lock back in April 2025 on a two-year, $5 million contract to back up Darnold. He
entered the 2026 opener at 6:22 of the first quarter, after Darnold was injured on the sack ending
Seattle's first drive, having taken almost no practice work with the starting skill players that
week.

**No Week 2 starter has been named.** Darnold has a hip injury with a fracture ruled out and the
problem described as muscular, but **no prognosis, timetable, designation or roster move exists**.
Asked whether Darnold could play in Week 2 at Arizona, head coach Mike Macdonald said he did not
know and that his own thinking was that Lock would play — explicitly noting this was not a decision
he had been given. That is a coach's provisional lean, **not a named starter**, and this record does
not treat it as one.

**45 of 50 offensive snaps (90%). 16 of 22 for 187 yards, 1 touchdown, 0 interceptions, 113.3 passer

Lock led Seattle back from 10-0 down, throwing a 45-yard touchdown to Jaxon Smith-Njigba that tied
the game in the fourth quarter. He turned the ball over zero times while New England turned it over
three times, which is the whole margin of the game.

**He accounted for 22 of Seattle's 24 team targets**, including 10 of Smith-Njigba's 11. Every
Seattle receiving rate from this game is effectively a Lock-unit rate; Darnold threw two passes.

A third-quarter touchdown pass to Cooper Kupp was erased by an illegal-shift penalty, so his line
understates the offense's red-zone execution by one score. Seattle nonetheless finished 0-for-2 in
the red zone and won on three field goals and the one touchdown.
