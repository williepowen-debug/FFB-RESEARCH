## Durable player profile

### Traits and role fit

Price is an explosive, efficiency-first runner drafted to replace the big-play element
Seattle lost when Kenneth Walker III left. His college profile is high yards per carry
on modest volume (6.0 per attempt across 280 career carries, never more than 120 in a
season) with limited receiving usage (15 career catches) and elite kick-return
production (three return touchdowns on 22 returns; first-team All-American as a
returner in 2025). He measured 5-foot-10 5/8 and 203 pounds at the combine (listed
5-foot-11, 209).

The durable questions are the ones his college usage never answered: whether he can
carry 15-plus times a game, whether he can pass-protect, and whether he can be a
receiver. Draft capital (32nd overall) says Seattle expects yes on the first.

Two patterns matter.

**He was never the volume back in college.** 120 carries was his single-season high in
a shared Notre Dame backfield. Projecting 200-plus NFL carries is a projection of role,
not of demonstrated workload.

**Touchdown rate spiked in 2025.** Eleven rushing touchdowns on 113 carries, up from 7
on 120. Whether that reflects goal-line trust or offensive context is unknown; it is
the best evidence that he was used near the goal line at all.

### Strengths, limitations, and relevant context

Strengths: first-round draft capital; explosive per-touch efficiency at every level;
return ability that gives him a roster role independent of the backfield; a Seattle
line that returns all five starters.

Limitations: a ruptured Achilles cost him his entire 2022 freshman season; a
lower-body issue cost him about a week of 2026 camp and the team held him out of all
three preseason games; no NFL evidence in pass protection or as a receiver; a
career-high of 120 carries.

Context: signed a four-year, $16.7 million rookie contract on 2026-05-29. Seattle's
roster construction around him — George Holani retained, Emanuel Wilson signed, Zach
Charbonnet on reserve/PUP for at least four games — is consistent with a plan to
feature him early while protecting him from every role.

## Current situation

### Team and depth-chart role

Expected to make his NFL debut in the Week 1 opener against New England on 2026-09-09
after not playing in any preseason game (he did take joint-practice reps against
Tennessee). He carries no injury designation. The official team framing (John Boyle,
2026-09-07) is that Price will help fill Walker's void but will not carry the load
alone, with Holani and Wilson behind a line returning all five starters. Brady
Henderson's ESPN feature (2026-08-31) framed him as the lead back, with Holani heavily
involved on passing downs and Wilson as a power complement, and noted the role will
shift when Charbonnet returns. Both framings are compatible: lead, but not alone. No
split has been measured or announced. Wilson was a full participant with no
designation, so the full healthy backfield is available for Week 1.

### Coaching/scheme fit

Brian Fleury's Shanahan-tree background (49ers run-game coordinator and tight ends
coach) is a wide-zone, outside-zone family that rewards one-cut explosiveness — the
same structure that made Walker productive in 2025. Macdonald said in camp that Price
has handled all three downs in practice but acknowledged the team lacked the bodies to
give him normal rookie preseason snaps. Darnold described him as able to catch, run
screens, and execute multiple run concepts. Those are practice observations and
quotes, not game usage. See
[Fleury continuity finding](../../teams/NFC/West/Seattle-Seahawks/2026/offense/fleury-continuity-offense.md).

### Health and availability

Available for Week 1 with no designation. History: ruptured Achilles (2022, full season
missed); minor lower-body soreness in August 2026 that cost about a week of camp and
contributed to the decision to hold him out of preseason games. Nothing reported as
lingering as of 2026-09-09.

## Fantasy assessment

- Applicable format: all; the widest variance in redraft and DFS, the highest
  long-run value in dynasty because of draft capital and age (22).
- Range of outcomes: from a 12-16 touch complementary back in a three-way committee
  to a 200-plus-carry lead back before Charbonnet's return. The Week 1 anytime-TD
  market priced him second on the field behind Rhamondre Stevenson, which is market
  context rather than evidence. The team's own framing is the committee side of that
  range; ESPN's is the lead side.
- Key upside condition: first-team early-down work plus goal-line carries in Weeks
  1-4 while Charbonnet is out, establishing the job before Charbonnet is eligible in
  Week 5.
- Key downside condition: Holani owning passing downs and Wilson taking short-yardage
  work, leaving Price as an early-down-only runner in a defense-first offense; or a
  quick Charbonnet return that reduces him to a rotational role by midseason.
- Confidence: medium on being the leading carry-getter in Weeks 1-4; low on
  passing-down and goal-line share; low on the post-Charbonnet split.

## What would change this assessment

- Week 1 and Week 2 snap, route, and carry shares among Price, Holani, and Wilson:
  a share above roughly 55 percent of carries with any passing-down work confirms the
  lead-back reading; below 40 percent confirms the committee reading.
- Who takes the first inside-the-five carry and the two-minute-drill snaps.
- Charbonnet's return-to-practice date (eligible as early as Week 3) and any coach
  statement about the post-return hierarchy.
- Any pass-protection failure that leads to visible substitution on third downs.
- A recurrence of the lower-body issue that held him out of preseason.
