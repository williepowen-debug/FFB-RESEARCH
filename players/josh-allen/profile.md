# Josh Allen

## Durable player profile

### Traits and role fit

Allen is the archetype of the dual-threat quarterback whose fantasy value is
structurally insulated: he is simultaneously the offense's primary passer and its
primary goal-line runner. That second role is what separates him from every other
quarterback in the tier — it converts red-zone possessions into his own touchdowns
rather than his running back's, and it makes him far less dependent on game script,
receiver quality, or pass volume than a pocket passer of similar ability.

He holds the NFL record for career rushing touchdowns by a quarterback (79) and shares
the single-season record (15, in 2023). He is the 2024 NFL MVP and a four-time Pro
Bowler (2020, 2022, 2024, 2025), with second-team All-Pro selections in 2020 and 2024.

The defining pattern is a **four-year, monotonic decline in passing volume** — 646
attempts down to 460, a 28.8 percent reduction — with passing touchdowns falling in
lockstep (36, 35, 29, 28, 25) and rushing touchdowns rising to fill the gap (6, 7, 15,
12, 14). His 2025 completion rate of 69.3 percent was a career high at his lowest
attempt volume: this is an offense taking fewer and better passing shots, not a
quarterback in decline.

Over the last five seasons he has averaged roughly 640 rushing yards and 10.8 rushing
touchdowns per year. He has played all 17 games in four consecutive seasons.

Despite setting six-year lows in passing yards per game (215.8), passing touchdowns
(25), and conventional fantasy points per game (22.0) in 2025, he still finished as the
overall QB1 — the fourth time in six seasons.

### Strengths, limitations, and relevant context

Strengths: elite rushing-touchdown floor that no other quarterback matches, durability
(four straight 17-game seasons), improving accuracy, and a scoring role that survives
poor receiver play.

Limitations: passing volume that has shrunk every year for four years, a receiver room
that has not produced a 900-yard target since Stefon Diggs left after 2023, and an
interception rate that has swung widely (6 in 2024, 18 in 2023).

Context: Buffalo led the NFL in rushing yards, rushing yards per game, and rushing
touchdowns in 2025 while ranking 25th in pass attempts. The offense's designer, Joe
Brady, was promoted to head coach for 2026 and retains play-calling, so the run-first
identity has the same author.

## Current situation

### Team and depth-chart role

Unquestioned franchise quarterback with no competition; Kyle Allen signed as the
veteran backup. Played two series in the 2026-08-15 preseason opener, completing 6 of 8
for 111 yards and a touchdown.

### Coaching/scheme fit

Brady remains the play-caller as head coach — maximum continuity. New offensive
coordinator Pete Carmichael arrives from a passing tradition and is expected to
influence situational and passing-game detail without changing the base identity. The
Bills traded for DJ Moore in March 2026 specifically to address the receiver deficit.
Whether those two additions actually raise pass volume against a four-year downtrend is
the central open question — see
[Buffalo's passing-volume decline](../../teams/AFC/East/Buffalo-Bills/2026/offense/passing-volume-decline.md).

### Health and availability

Durable. Four consecutive 17-game seasons and no reported 2026 injury or limitation as
of 2026-08-20.

## Fantasy assessment

- Applicable format: all; most valuable in formats paying per completion, per passing
  yard, and 6 points per passing touchdown.
- Range of outcomes: the consensus 2026 QB1, chosen first by seven of eight ESPN
  panelists. His floor is unusually high because rushing touchdowns are the least
  volatile part of his profile; his ceiling is capped by team pass volume rather than
  by his own ability.
- Key upside condition: pass attempts recovering toward 500-plus (public projections
  have 509) *while* he retains double-digit rushing touchdowns. Both must happen —
  historically they have moved in opposite directions.
- Key downside condition: pass volume falling further as Buffalo leans harder on the
  run, or the goal-line rushing work shifting to the backfield. Either alone is
  survivable; together they would move him out of the tier.
- Confidence: high on role, durability, and floor; medium on the direction of team
  pass volume.

### Format-specific note

Scored in the Gridiron Guru's Only 2026 format, Allen's totals have declined every year
for four years: 515.5 (2021), 507.4 (2022), 491.1 (2023), 461.0 (2024), 450.5 (2025) —
26.5 points per game in 2025, before big-play and yardage-game bonuses. Applying the
public 2026 projection yields roughly 457 points, or 26.9 per game: **essentially flat**,
because passing-volume recovery is worth about what rushing-touchdown regression costs.
A realistic band is 26-28 points per game.

Two consequences for that league specifically. First, 2025 was Allen's *worst* passing
season in six years and it still cleared 26 points per game there, so the downside is
well protected. Second, his rushing touchdowns are the same carries that cap James
Cook's scoring, so a roster cannot hold bullish projections for both — see the
[red-zone record](../../teams/AFC/East/Buffalo-Bills/2026/offense/red-zone-and-touchdown-share.md).

## Team history

- 2018-present: Buffalo Bills. Selected seventh overall in the 2018 NFL Draft out of
  Wyoming.

## What would change this assessment

- Team pass attempts per game across the first four weeks: above roughly 33 breaks the
  four-year downtrend and raises his ceiling materially; below 25 pushes his value
  almost entirely onto rushing.
- Any reported plan to reduce his designed goal-line runs, which would lower his floor
  and raise Cook's ceiling in the same stroke.
- DJ Moore reaching a target share that produces a 900-plus-yard pace, ending Buffalo's
  two-year receiver drought.
- An interception rate returning toward the 2023 level (18), which would cost real
  points in formats penalizing them.
