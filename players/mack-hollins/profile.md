## Durable player profile

### Why this record exists

Created on 2026-09-10 because Hollins led New England in receiving yards in Week 1 and played more
offensive snaps than either Romeo Doubs or A.J. Brown. **He was already recorded in the repository
as a roster fact** — the six-man receiver room in the

### Traits and role fit

A 6-foot-4, 221-pound veteran receiver drafted in the fourth round in 2017, Hollins has built a
long career as a complementary and special-teams contributor across six franchises rather than as a
volume target. His size profile fits a blocking and contested-catch role on the perimeter, and his
snap counts have historically outrun his target share — a pattern that repeated exactly in Week 1.

**The durable question is whether snap volume ever converts to target volume.** Across his career it
mostly has not; his first Patriots season produced 46 receptions in 15 games.

## Current situation

Hollins signed a two-year, $8.4 million contract with New England in March 2025 and enters 2026 in a
six-man receiver room alongside A.J. Brown, Romeo Doubs, DeMario Douglas, Kyle Williams and Efton
Chism III, following the August 2025 trade of Kayshon Boutte.

**A.J. Brown's Week 1 ankle injury is the live variable.** Brown was ruled out during the third
quarter with no prognosis and unknown Week 2 availability. Lazar's film review projects a large role
for Hollins if Brown misses time, and Callahan observed that Maye came off most of his receivers
except Hollins once Brown left. Both are interpretation, not commitment.

## Week 1 2026 — measured usage

*New England at Seattle, 2026-09-09. Seattle won 13-10.*

**50 of 71 offensive snaps (70%), 4 receptions on 5 targets for 51 yards.**

Hollins led New England in receiving yards and played the second-most offensive snaps of any pass
catcher, behind only Hunter Henry (54) and ahead of Douglas (44), Doubs (40) and Brown (31).

Two readings of the snap count are on record and both should be kept. Kyed reports Hollins was used
heavily on **run plays** in the first half — which suppressed Doubs's and Douglas's snaps for a
reason unrelated to receiving standing — while Callahan observed that Maye targeted him
preferentially after Brown's exit. A 70% snap share producing five targets is consistent with the
first reading; the 51 yards are consistent with the second.

He was also the intended receiver on the game-ending interception: Maye's deep throw for Hollins at
the Seattle 16 with 26 seconds left was picked by Josh Jobe.
