# Rashid Shaheed

## Durable player profile

### Traits and role fit

Shaheed is a vertical field-stretcher and one of the league's best return men. His
receiving profile is low volume and high yield: a career 14.7 yards per reception,
season averages of 17.4 and 17.5 in his two healthiest New Orleans years, and career
long receptions of 68, 58, 70, and 87 yards. He has never had more than 46 receptions
in a season. His return value is durable and honored: first-team All-Pro (2023) and two
Pro Bowls (2023, 2025) as a returner, with three career punt-return touchdowns and one
kickoff-return touchdown in the regular season plus a divisional-round kickoff-return
touchdown in the 2025 playoffs.

The durable fantasy tension is that his return role is the reason he is on the field
every week and also a cap on his offensive snaps and an injury-exposure risk.

Three patterns matter.

**2025 was two different players.** Nine games in New Orleans produced 44 catches for
499 yards (a career-high pace on volume, career-low yards per catch); nine games in
Seattle after the 2025-11-04 trade produced 15 catches for 188 yards and no receiving
touchdowns, but 1 kickoff-return touchdown, 1 punt-return touchdown, and a 2025 Pro Bowl
nod as a returner. Seattle got the returner immediately and the receiver barely at all.

**He has never sustained volume.** 46 receptions is his best season. Every efficient
year came on 20-46 catches. Any projection above roughly 50 receptions is a projection
of a role he has not held.

**Availability is the recurring problem.** He has never played 17 games: 12, 15, 6
(meniscus surgery and injured reserve from 2024-10-17), and 18 across two teams in 2025.

### Strengths, limitations, and relevant context

Strengths: genuine deep speed and long-touchdown ability; an All-Pro return profile
that guarantees game-day activity; a full 2026 offseason and camp with Darnold after
joining mid-season; a contract that signals a planned offensive role.

Limitations: no season above 46 receptions; a 2025 Seattle sample of 15 catches in nine
games; a meniscus surgery in 2024; the return job competes with offensive snaps;
Smith-Njigba's target dominance and Cooper Kupp's presence cap the available share.

Context: Seattle re-signed him to a three-year, $51 million deal (agreed around
2026-03-09, signed 2026-03-11; Over the Cap lists $22.235 million fully guaranteed
including a $20 million signing bonus, per-game bonuses of $765,000 per year, and a
2027 salary guarantee that vests shortly after the 2026 season). The guarantee structure
makes 2026 and 2027 planned seasons.

## Current situation

### Team and depth-chart role

Listed as a starting receiver alongside Smith-Njigba and Kupp, and listed at both punt
and kick returner. John Boyle's 2026-07-28 feature reported that Shaheed expects a
significantly expanded passing-game role after a full offseason, that Macdonald cited
improved timing with Darnold and said the staff would keep exploring ways to get him
the ball, and that Shaheed wants to keep returning. Ari Horton's 2026-07-06 camp
storyline framed the question directly: if his offensive load grows, does he keep both
return jobs, with Tory Horton (punts) and Holani, Price, and Emmanuel Henderson Jr.
(kickoffs) as alternatives. Horton is questionable for Week 1 with a hamstring, which
if he is inactive leaves Shaheed as the default returner and makes the Week 1 route
share a thinner-room reading. He carries no injury designation.

### Coaching/scheme fit

Fleury's Shanahan-tree offense uses play-action shot plays that suit a deep-speed
receiver, and the continuity framing means the 2025 Seattle alignment is the starting
point. The 2025 problem was integration time, not scheme: he arrived in November into
a running offense. A public route-share figure of about 62 percent after the trade
circulated in fantasy coverage (unverified against charting data) and is the baseline
any expansion must beat. See
[Fleury continuity finding](../../teams/NFC/West/Seattle-Seahawks/2026/offense/fleury-continuity-offense.md).

### Health and availability

Available for Week 1 with no designation. History: 2024 meniscus surgery and injured
reserve (2024-10-17); has never played a full 17-game season.

## Fantasy assessment

- Applicable format: best ball and DFS first, where his big-play variance is an
  asset; formats that award return yards or return touchdowns second; weekly
  redraft last, where his floor is the problem.
- Range of outcomes: a 35-55 catch, 550-850 yard boom-bust WR3/WR4 with 4-7
  touchdowns and meaningful return scoring in formats that count it. A true breakout
  (60-plus catches) requires a role he has never held and a share that Smith-Njigba
  and Kupp currently occupy.
- Key upside condition: a route share above roughly 75 percent in Weeks 1-4 with
  designed touches (his 7 Seattle carries in 2025 hint at this) while keeping return
  duties.
- Key downside condition: Kupp and Smith-Njigba holding three-receiver snaps, leaving
  him a rotational deep threat at 3-4 targets a game; or an injury in the return game.
- Confidence: medium on being a weekly starter on offense; low on volume growth; high
  on retaining return duties for Week 1.

## Team history

  to the active roster 2022-10-15. Signed a one-year, $5.2 million extension on
  2024-07-20.
- 2025-present: Seattle Seahawks. Acquired by trade on 2025-11-04 for 2026 fourth- and
  fifth-round picks. Re-signed to a three-year, $51 million deal on 2026-03-11. Super
  Bowl LX champion.

## What would change this assessment

- Route participation in Weeks 1-4: above 75 percent confirms the expanded role;
  below 60 percent confirms the 2025 rotational reading.
- Targets per game above 5 sustained over a month, which he has never done outside the
  2025 New Orleans stretch.
- Seattle removing him from one or both return jobs (Horton healthy on punts, Price on
  kickoffs), which would raise his offensive snap ceiling and lower his return-format
  value.
- Any Kupp absence, which is the most direct route to a larger share.
- Any soft-tissue or knee issue, given the 2024 meniscus history and return-game
  exposure.
