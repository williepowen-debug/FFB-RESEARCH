# Jaxon Smith-Njigba

## Durable player profile

### Traits and role fit

Smith-Njigba is a target-dominant, route-and-separation receiver who has grown from a
slot-only rookie into an every-alignment No. 1. His defining fantasy trait is target
concentration: 93, 137, and 163 targets across three seasons, the last of them the
largest share of any Seattle pass-catcher by a wide margin. He does not win primarily
on size or contested catches; he wins on release, route timing, and yards after the
catch, which is why his yards per reception rose from 10.0 to 15.1 as the offense moved
him outside and downfield under Klint Kubiak in 2025.

Honors: 2025 AP Offensive Player of the Year, unanimous first-team All-Pro (2025),
Pro Bowl (2025), NFL receiving-yards leader (1,793 in 2025), and the second Seahawk to
win Offensive Player of the Year after Shaun Alexander in 2005. Super Bowl LX champion.

Three patterns matter.

**Monotonic growth in every volume column.** Targets, receptions, yards, and touchdowns
all rose each season. 2025 was not a fluke on top of a plateau; it was the third step
of a straight line.

**Target share is the durable asset.** The reproducible 2025 nflverse count is
163 of 455 assigned Seattle targets (35.8%). Kupp is next at 70, a gap of 93;
the earlier claim that nobody was within 100 was incorrect. The September 9 audit corrected the filter to exclude two-point tries. Assigned targets exclude
unassigned throws and are not the same denominator as Darnold's 477 pass attempts.
See the [calculation and definitions](../../weekly/2026/week-01/research/ne-sea/README.md).
This establishes concentration; it does not establish a league-wide floor ranking.

**Touchdowns lag yardage.** Ten touchdowns on 1,793 yards is a modest rate for a
receiver of this volume; his scoring is yardage-driven, not red-zone-driven, so
formats with yardage bonuses value him more than touchdown-heavy scoring does.

Durability: 51 of 51 possible regular-season games in three seasons.

### Strengths, limitations, and relevant context

Strengths: unmatched target concentration in his offense; three straight full seasons;
production that grew when he moved outside; a quarterback who has thrown for 4,000-plus
yards two years running; a contract that makes him the offensive centerpiece through
2031.

Limitations: touchdown rate below his yardage rate; ball security regressed in 2025
(3 fumbles, 1 lost); the team's overall pass volume is capped by a run-and-defense
identity, so his ceiling is a function of share, not of a high-volume passing game.

Context: Seattle signed him to a four-year, $168.6 million extension (agreed
2026-03-23, signed at team headquarters 2026-03-25; Over the Cap lists $42.15 million
per year, the highest receiver average at signing, with about $120 million in
practical guarantees and control through 2031).

## Current situation

### Team and depth-chart role

WR1 with no competition for the primary role. The 2026 receiver room is Smith-Njigba,
Cooper Kupp, Rashid Shaheed (extended in March; first full Seattle offseason), Tory

### Coaching/scheme fit

Brian Fleury replaced Klint Kubiak as offensive coordinator; Seahawks.com framed the
hire as continuity within the Shanahan tree, and the offense was described in
minicamp as a partial evolution of 2025. The 2025 usage that moved Smith-Njigba
outside and downfield is therefore the base case, but Fleury has no regular-season
sample. The one structural change is the backfield: with Kenneth Walker III gone and a
rookie lead back, the passing game may be asked to carry more, which helps his target
count. See
[Fleury continuity finding](../../teams/NFC/West/Seattle-Seahawks/2026/offense/fleury-continuity-offense.md)
and [post-Walker backfield](../../teams/NFC/West/Seattle-Seahawks/2026/offense/post-walker-backfield.md).

## Week 1 2026 — measured usage

## Fantasy assessment

- Applicable format: all; highest relative value in full PPR and in formats with
  per-game receiving-yardage bonuses (he cleared 100 yards frequently in 2025 on
  15.1 yards per catch).
- Range of outcomes: a top-five receiver in every format on target share alone.
  Central expectation is 140-165 targets, 100-120 receptions, 1,400-1,700 yards and
  8-12 touchdowns. A repeat of 1,793 requires both the 2025 share and the 2025 yards
  per catch; either alone still produces a WR1 season.
- Key upside condition: team pass attempts rising under Fleury while his share holds,
  or a touchdown rate catching up to his yardage (12-plus scores).
- Key downside condition: Shaheed's full offseason and Kupp's presence spreading
  targets enough to pull his share below roughly 28 percent, combined with a
  low-attempt Seattle offense; or ball-security issues costing him red-zone trust.
- Confidence: high on role and share; medium on repeating 15-plus yards per
  reception; medium on touchdowns.

## Team history

## What would change this assessment

- Target share over the first four games: above roughly 30 percent confirms the 2025
  concentration; below 25 percent means the Fleury offense distributes differently.
- Shaheed or Kupp posting a route share and target rate that clearly cuts into his
  downfield targets rather than adding to the whole.
- Any Darnold absence; 2025 established his connection with Darnold, while the prior Seattle season had a different starting quarterback.
- A repeat of the 2025 fumble total early in the season.
- Fleury demonstrably shifting the pass game toward two-tight-end and play-action
  structures that reduce three-receiver snaps.
