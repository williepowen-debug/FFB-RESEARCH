# AJ Barner

## Durable player profile

### Traits and role fit

Barner is a complete, in-line tight end — 6-foot-6, 251 pounds — whose real-football
value is split between blocking and receiving and whose fantasy value comes from
red-zone and short-yardage trust rather than route volume. In 2025 he was Seattle's
starting tight end in all 17 games, caught 52 passes for 519 yards and 6 touchdowns,
and was also used as a short-yardage ball carrier (10 carries for 14 yards and 1
touchdown, per the official career table). He caught Seattle's only offensive touchdown in the Super
Bowl LX win. Seattle drafted him in the fourth round in 2024 out of Michigan after
three seasons at Indiana.

Two patterns matter.

**Year-two growth was real but moderate.** Receptions rose from 30 to 52, yards from
245 to 519, touchdowns from 4 to 6, and yards per catch from 8.2 to 10.0. On 68 targets
that is a roughly 15 percent target share — a starting tight end's share in a
run-first offense, not an elite one.

**Touchdowns outrun volume.** Ten touchdowns on 82 career catches, plus the
short-yardage carries, show a player the staff trusts at the goal line. Touchdown
equity is his fantasy case; yardage is not.

### Strengths, limitations, and relevant context

Strengths: 34 of 34 games in two seasons; every-down starter status; goal-line and
short-yardage trust; a coordinator with a tight-end coaching background; the only
established tight end in the room after Nick Vannett's release at cutdown.

Limitations: 10.0 yards per reception and a 15 percent target share cap his ceiling;
Smith-Njigba's target dominance leaves limited volume; Elijah Arroyo (2025 second-round
pick, 15 catches as a rookie) is the receiving alternative if Seattle wants more
two-tight-end sets to split routes rather than concentrate them.

Context: Barner is on his rookie contract (fourth round, 2024). He appeared in
FOX 13's 2026-09-06 notebook as a full participant with an oblique note but was not
listed on the official Week 1 participation rows; the repository treats him as
available and undesignated.

### Coaching/scheme fit

Brian Fleury coached the 49ers' tight ends from 2022 before adding run-game
coordinator duties, and Seahawks.com's camp storyline (John Boyle, 2026-07-14) framed
him as likely to create expanded opportunities for the position. The same article
notes that Arroyo's receiving upside could split those opportunities. Fantasy coverage
has described a shift toward more two-tight-end personnel under Fleury; that is
unverified until Week 1 personnel charting. If Horton is inactive in Week 1 and with

targets in the short term. See

## Fantasy assessment

- Applicable format: tight-end-premium and best ball first; a low-end TE1 or
  high-end TE2 in standard-size redraft leagues.
- Range of outcomes: central expectation of 50-65 receptions, 500-650 yards, 5-8
  touchdowns. A ceiling season requires a route rate near 80 percent and 80-plus
  targets under Fleury; the floor is a 2024-style 30-catch blocking role if Arroyo
  takes the receiving snaps.
- Key upside condition: first-team route participation above roughly 75 percent in
  Weeks 1-4 while keeping the goal-line and short-yardage work.
- Key downside condition: Fleury using two-tight-end sets to give Arroyo the routes and
  Barner the blocks; or the pass game funneling even harder to Smith-Njigba.
- Confidence: high on TE1 status and availability; medium on touchdown equity; low on
  route-rate growth.

## Team history

- 2024-present: Seattle Seahawks. Selected in the fourth round (121st overall) of the
  2024 NFL Draft out of Michigan (transfer from Indiana, 2020-2022).

## What would change this assessment

- Route participation rate in Weeks 1-4: above 75 percent confirms a receiving role;
  below 60 percent confirms the blocker-plus-touchdowns reading.
- Arroyo's route share in two-tight-end sets, which determines whether more tight-end
  snaps mean more Barner routes or more split routes.
- Targets per game at or above 5 sustained for a month, which would be a new level.
- Any loss of the short-yardage and goal-line role, which is the basis of his
  touchdown equity.
- An oblique or other injury designation, since one independent report noted a soft
  flag in early September.
